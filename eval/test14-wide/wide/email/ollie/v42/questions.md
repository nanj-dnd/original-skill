**What everyone gets**

The main claim a pasted prompt produces: "Our software cuts your fuel and mileage costs by 15–20%; can we have 15 minutes?" The choices it makes:

- **Angle**: cost savings on fuel and miles.
- **Examples**: an unnamed "logistics company like yours" that saved a round percentage.
- **Structure**: hope-this-finds-you-well opener → their pain → our fix → social proof → "15-minute call?"
- **Reader**: an ops head assumed to be chasing efficiency, nothing specific about their fleet or network.
- **Tone**: polite-salesy, a little breathless.

Four questions, answer like `1b 2a 3c 4a`:

**1. Lead with what?** The default leads with fuel/mileage savings.
a) That: fuel and miles
b) Driver hours and overtime (the planning fits more stops into legal hours)
c) Missed delivery windows and customer complaints
d) Planner time: the hours someone spends building routes by hand each morning

**2. What real material can I use?** The default cites a vague "a company like yours saved 18%."
a) A named customer with a number (give me the name, what changed, and the figure)
b) A number from our own product or trials, no customer name (give me the figure)
c) Nothing citable yet; write it without numbers and let the ask carry it
d) Something I know about *their* company (a route, a region, a hiring post, a complaint I saw); tell me what

**3. The ask.** The default asks for a 15-minute call.
a) 15-minute call
b) We run one week of their routes through the software and send back the comparison, no call needed
c) A one-line reply to a single question (e.g. "how are routes built today?")

**4. How it sounds, and whose name.** Default is polite-salesy under a sales rep's signature.
a) Plain and short, from you as founder/builder
b) Plain and short, from a salesperson
c) More formal, as you'd write to someone senior you don't know
