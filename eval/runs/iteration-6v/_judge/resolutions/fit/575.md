# Nothing Grows in January

Every year around this time, the same thing happens. The calendar turns over, and we're all encouraged to become new people overnight. Exercise more, eat better, finally sort out the shed. By the middle of February most of those promises have quietly slipped away, and we tell ourselves we just didn't try hard enough.

I'm not sure that's true. I think a lot of resolutions fail not because of the people making them, but because of when they're made.

Think about what the garden is doing in January. Very little, and on purpose. The ground is cold and often too wet to work. The days are short. Perennials have drawn back into their roots, bulbs are waiting under the soil, and the trees are bare. None of that is failure. It's rest, and it's what makes the growth in spring possible.

No gardener would sow tender seeds outdoors in January and then blame themselves when nothing came up. We'd know straight away it was the wrong time. Yet that's more or less what we ask of ourselves with a New Year's resolution: start something new, all at once, at the darkest and coldest point of the year, when our energy is low and the evenings are long. It's hardly surprising that so many of those efforts don't take.

What gardeners do in winter instead is plan. We look through the seeds we've saved and the ones we'd like to buy. We think about what did well last year and what struggled. We sketch out beds, clean and sharpen tools, and decide what we'd like to try. It's quiet work, but it matters. When spring arrives we're ready, and starting feels natural rather than forced.

Maybe our resolutions deserve the same care. Rather than expecting a change to take root on the first of January, we could use the winter months to think it through. What do we actually want? What got in the way last time? What small first step would be realistic? Then, as the days lengthen and the soil warms, we can begin, with the season on our side instead of against us.

So if you've made a resolution and it's already wobbling, please don't be hard on yourself. You may simply have planted it too early. Let it rest for now, give it some thought, and try again in spring.

Things tend to grow better when the time is right.
