# Why Most Resolutions Fail (and Why My Welsh Didn't)

Every January a lot of us make promises to ourselves, and by spring most of them have quietly disappeared. There are plenty of theories about why: the goals were too vague, too ambitious, there was no plan. I think the honest answer is simpler. People lose motivation.

Not because they're lazy. Motivation is a mood, and moods don't last. The fizz you feel on the first of January is real, but it was never going to carry you to March. So the useful question isn't "how do I stay motivated?" It's "what keeps me going once the motivation has gone?"

I've been thinking about this because I'm on a 700-day streak learning Welsh on an app. Seven hundred days. I'd love to tell you that's down to my iron discipline, but most of you know me, so I won't bother. [One line on why you picked Welsh, or what's been hardest about it.]

What actually keeps it going is my daughter. She's 12, she's learning too, and at dinner we check each other's streaks. That's it. That's the whole system. No speech, no chart on the fridge. Just "did you do yours?" across the table, from someone who will absolutely notice if the answer is no.

That small question does the job motivation can't. Over 700 days there have been nights I didn't feel like it. On those nights I'm not doing it for the version of me who was excited about Welsh at the start. I'm doing it because I'd rather not be the one at dinner with the broken streak, explaining myself to a 12-year-old.

That, I think, is why most resolutions fail. They live entirely inside one person's head. When the motivation runs out, nobody else is there to notice. The goal goes quiet, and nothing happens, because there's no one to say anything.

So if you're making a resolution this year, here's my one piece of advice: don't count on still wanting it in February. Find someone to check in with, whether that's a partner, a friend or a kid, and make your progress something they can actually see: a streak, a tick on a calendar, a number. Keep it simple enough to ask about over dinner.

Then it matters a lot less whether you feel like it on a given night. Someone's going to ask.
