# January Is a Terrible Month to Start Anything

Every year I make a resolution or two, and every year I forget them so completely that by March I couldn't tell you what they were. I used to blame myself. Now I blame January.

Think about what January actually is. It's cold. It's dark by five. You've just spent December eating cheese for breakfast and spending money you didn't have, and now, in the bleakest, brokest weeks of the year, you've decided to become a whole new person. That's not a fresh start. That's a dare.

The other problem is that we didn't pick the date. The calendar did. Most resolutions don't start because something in our lives changed. They start because a number changed. And a goal that begins for no particular reason tends to end for no particular reason, usually right around the time the novelty wears off.

That happens fast. Strava, the fitness app, once went through its users' data and found that motivation reliably fell off a cliff around the second Friday of January, which it cheerfully named "Quitter's Day." And the stat that gets passed around most often says only about 8% of people actually keep their resolutions. Even if that number is a little shaky, nobody's arguing it should be higher.

To be fair to January, fresh starts really do help. Researchers at Wharton found that people are more likely to go after goals right after "temporal landmarks" like the start of a week, a month, or a birthday. The catch is that January 1st is the one landmark everybody shares. It comes with a crowd, a lot of pressure, and the faint embarrassment of joining a fad.

So here's what I'd suggest instead:

**Pick your own start date.** A birthday, a move, the first of some unremarkable month like April. A landmark that means something to you beats one that means something to everyone.

**Start when you have a reason, not a deadline.** If you can't say why you want something beyond "well, it's January," that's worth noticing.

**Make it small enough to be boring.** Not "get fit," but "walk after dinner on Tuesdays." Boring survives February. Ambitious usually doesn't.

**Put it somewhere you'll trip over it.** Half my resolutions didn't fail. They just got lost.

And if you're reading this in early January and already dreading the year ahead, relax. The most sensible thing you can do this month is nothing much. Wait for spring. It'll still be a new year.
