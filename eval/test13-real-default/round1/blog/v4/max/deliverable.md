# Why resolutions fail: the date was always the problem

I've made New Year's resolutions. I couldn't tell you what any of them were. None of them failed dramatically. I didn't cave in week three and eat a cake with my hands. I just forgot I'd made them, which is somehow worse. You can't even learn from a goal you can't remember.

I think that's how most resolutions actually go, and I think the date is to blame. Not willpower. Not vagueness. January 1st.

**1. Nothing in your life changed on January 1st**

A real reason to change usually comes from something: a comment from a doctor, a bill, a photo, a week that went badly. Resolutions come from a calendar. The year rolled over, so a goal was supposed to follow. But a goal that arrives on schedule has no event holding it in place. It's a promise made because it was the time for promises, which is exactly why it slides off.

**2. You picked it from the menu**

When the date is the prompt, you reach for the stock answers: eat better, move more, read more. Not because those are your problems, but because they're the kind of thing people resolve. A goal chosen from a list was never personal, and nothing impersonal survives a dull Tuesday.

**3. It's a terrible week to start anything**

You're tired, broke, back at work, and every routine you had got scrambled over the holidays. The one day everyone agrees to begin new habits is also one of the worst days of the year to begin anything.

**4. It's annual, so it just expires**

If you set goals once a year, the one that slips has to wait twelve months. Nobody re-sets a resolution in March. It doesn't fail. It quietly reaches the end of its shelf life.

**What to do instead**

- Start when something happens. Tie the goal to an event in your life, not a number on a calendar.
- Pick the thing that's actually bugging you, even if it's boring and nobody else would resolve it.
- Choose an unremarkable start date: a normal Tuesday in a normal week.
- Write it down somewhere you'll see it. Worst case, next January you'll at least know what you forgot.

The calendar isn't going to change you. It can't even get you to remember what you asked it for.
