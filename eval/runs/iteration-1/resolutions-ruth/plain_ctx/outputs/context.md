MY ANSWERS:
1d 2d 3b 4d

1: I don't think it's the method at all, really. Most resolutions are about improving yourself on your own, and nobody is waiting for you to show up. You can make "walk 20 minutes a day" as specific as you like, but if you skip it on a wet Tuesday nobody notices except you, and frankly we're all very good at forgiving ourselves. In my experience the ones that stuck were the ones where somebody else was counting on me.

2: I'd rather it didn't end with a list of tips. Those are everywhere already. What I'd like it to say is that if you want something to stick, tie it to another person instead of to a better system. So it's a bit of advice, I suppose, but it shouldn't read like a technique.

3: Yes. For years I resolved to "walk more," every January, and I never did it. Then my neighbour Joan had her hip done and needed someone to walk with while she got her strength back. We've walked every weekday at 9 o'clock for two years now. Rain, cold, it doesn't matter. The only thing that changed was that Joan was waiting on her front step. I'd never let Joan down. I let myself down all the time. (She's quicker than me now, which I find a bit cheeky, but there we are.)

4: My readers are mostly other retirees here in town. Polite and a bit chatty is how I sound, like we're talking over the fence. I'd also say that most of this resolution business is written for young people trying to be more productive. At our age most of us aren't trying to be productive. We're trying to stay connected. I'd like the post to talk to that, and the gym in January and Atomic Habits won't mean much to my lot.
