A year ago I stepped into my first management role, leading a three-person marketing team. A few lessons stuck with me:

**Communication is most of the job.** It's easy to assume that saying something once means it landed. Repeating priorities, checking for understanding, and being clear up front saves a lot of rework later.

**Delegation means letting go of the how, not just the what.** Handing off a task and then hovering over it isn't really delegating. Sharing the goal and context and then trusting people with the approach usually leads to better results and more ownership.

**Feedback works best small and often.** Saving it for formal reviews makes every conversation feel heavy. Regular, specific comments, both positive and constructive, keep feedback routine and useful.

None of this is new advice, but practicing it is different from reading about it. I'm grateful to my team for a good first year, and I'm looking forward to the next one.
