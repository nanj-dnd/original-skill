# I Tracked 11 New Year's Resolutions in a Spreadsheet. Two Survived.

For three years I've logged every New Year's resolution I made in a spreadsheet. The total so far: 11 resolutions, 2 that stuck.

That's about 18%, which is roughly normal. When psychologists John Norcross and Dominic Vangarelli followed 200 people through their resolutions in the late 1980s, 19% were still keeping them two years later. So I'm not unusually weak-willed. I'm average. The useful part is that my spreadsheet shows *which* 18% made it.

The two survivors:

1. **An automatic transfer to savings on payday.** I set it up once. It has run on its own ever since.
2. **Cancelling my gym membership and buying a doorway pull-up bar.** One cancellation, one purchase.

Every resolution that needed a daily decision failed.

The survivors weren't small goals. Saving money every payday for years is a big one. What they had in common is that each needed one decision, made once. The failures needed a decision every day, which is 365 a year. So the number that predicts whether a resolution survives isn't how ambitious it is. It's how many times you have to decide to do it.

Willpower doesn't scale. Automation does.

If you're into personal finance, you already know this. "Pay yourself first" works because the money moves before you get a chance to decide anything, not because you're disciplined. Most resolutions ask you to be disciplined 365 times in a row, and almost nobody manages it.

The fair objection is the pull-up bar. It doesn't do the pull-ups for me. But compare the decisions. The gym meant a separate decision to go, every single time. The bar hangs in a doorway I walk through all day, so going there takes no decision at all. Cancelling the membership also stopped a monthly charge without any further effort from me. When something can't be fully automated, putting it where you already are is the next best thing.

So before you write this year's list, give each resolution one test: **how many decisions per year does it need?**

- **One:** do it on January 1 and you're done. Set up the automatic transfer, raise your retirement contribution, cancel the subscription, buy the thing that puts the habit in your path.
- **365:** redesign it until it needs one, or cross it off now. My spreadsheet says it wasn't going to make it anyway.

Make your resolutions with your bank and your floor plan, not your willpower. Then don't think about them again.
