## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Social media has been a double-edged sword for democracy: it has given ordinary people a voice and helped them organise, but it has also spread misinformation and deepened polarisation. Its overall effect depends on regulation and digital literacy."

- **Angle:** "Both sides," ending in a call for balance, regulation and media literacy.
- **Examples:** The Arab Spring for the good side; Cambridge Analytica, the 2016 US election and January 6 for the bad side.
- **Structure:** Intro, then benefits, harms, solutions, and a conclusion.
- **Reader:** A general teacher or examiner, with US-centric framing.
- **Tone:** Neutral and academic, with some hedging.

## A few questions before I write

Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`.

**1. What's your actual view?** The default says it's a mixed bag, with the outcome depending on regulation.
- a) Mostly bad: it has done real structural damage, and "balance" undersells it.
- b) Mostly good: the panic is overstated, and the evidence on polarisation and misinformation is weaker than people assume.
- c) The question is framed wrong: platforms amplify whatever a country's institutions already are, so the effect is "neither, it depends on the country."
- d) Mixed, but on one specific mechanism (for example, it helps protest movements but hurts parties and governing) rather than the vague "double-edged sword."

**2. Which case should carry the essay?** The default uses the Arab Spring, Cambridge Analytica and 2016 USA.
- a) A country or event you know well, such as India, your own country or a local election. Name it and give any detail or number you know.
- b) One deep case instead of a tour of five.
- c) Use the research itself as the material, such as studies on echo chambers and deactivation experiments, and argue from evidence rather than anecdote.
- d) Keep the familiar cases but read them against the grain. For example, did the Arab Spring actually succeed?

**3. Who is this for, and how should it sound?** The default is a neutral, hedged, academic tone for a general marker.
- a) A school or university assignment with a formal academic register. Tell me the course level and any rules on AI use, and I'll keep to thesis and outline only.

- c) A competition or application essay, written as you, in a plain and personal voice.

**4. What does "democracy" mean here?** The default leaves it vague, meaning roughly "elections plus public debate."
- a) Elections and voting behaviour.
- b) Public debate and shared facts.
- c) Participation and protest, meaning who gets a voice.
- d) Leave it broad, but define it explicitly in the intro.
