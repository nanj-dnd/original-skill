# "It's fine": a five-minute talk on AI coding assistants

**Length:** about 700 spoken words, 5 minutes.
**Who it's for:** the engineers in the room.
**What should happen afterwards:** each engineer runs one small swap experiment next week.

## Outline

**1. Opening: why the person up here is an ordinary user (0:00–0:45)**
- Be honest about it: you got volunteered, you're a backend engineer, you've been here two years, and you use Copilot sometimes. Your verdict is "it's fine."
- Your guess is that most engineers here would say the same. Not hype, not alarm. Just "fine."
- The turn: "fine" is what you say about something you've never checked. That's the real problem with the question "should we use it for everything?"

**2. Answer the question straight (0:45–1:30)**
- No, not for everything. But "use it a lot, review everything, use it wisely" doesn't help either, because it never says *where* the line is.
- The line isn't one company-wide setting. It sits in a different place for each person and each kind of task, and most of us put ours there by habit, not by evidence.
- Say this once and move on. The talk is not a list of pros and cons.

**3. Where "fine" comes from for you (1:30–2:30)**
- One task where Copilot really saves you time: [your example: a kind of task from your backend work where you accept its suggestions].
- One task where you ignore it or turn it off: [your example: where its suggestions cost you time or you don't trust them].
- The point: you didn't decide either of those. They just settled. You've never tried it on the second one on purpose, or done the first one without it to see what it's really saving.
- Keep it to your own experience. Don't claim anything for the team that you haven't seen.

**4. The ask: one swap, next week (2:30–4:00)**
- Pick **one task you'd normally do by hand** and give it to the assistant properly. Don't take one suggestion and give up.
- Pick **one task you'd normally hand to it** and do it yourself.
- For each, write down two things: roughly how long it took, and what you had to fix or throw away.
- Post a few lines in [channel name] by [day]. No write-up and no template.
- Why a swap and not a policy: the swap moves your line based on what actually happened on your own work. A policy would just pick someone else's line.

**5. Say what this doesn't do (4:00–4:30)**
- One week of notes from each person isn't a study, and nobody should treat it as one.
- What it does is turn "fine" into something specific you can argue about, which is more than this question usually gets.
- Optional, only if you'll follow through: [offer to collect the posts and share what came up, at a time you choose].

**6. Close (4:30–5:00)**
- Go back to the opening: "I came up here with 'it's fine.' Next Friday I'd like to have a better answer than that, and I'd like you to have one too."
- End on the ask, not on "use it wisely": one swap, two notes, one post.

## Opening paragraph

I'll be honest about how I ended up up here: I got volunteered. I'm a backend engineer, I've been here two years, and I use Copilot sometimes. If you asked for my review, I'd say it's fine. My guess is that a lot of the engineers in this room would say the same. Not "it changed how I work," not "it's going to ship a disaster," just "fine." The trouble is that "fine" is what you say about something you've never actually tested. So I'm not going to stand here and tell you whether to use AI for everything, because I don't know where that line is for you, and I don't really know where it is for me either. What I can give you in the next five minutes is a way for each of us to find out, using one week of our own work.
