# Nobody Was Waiting for Me

Every January, for more years than I'd like to count, I resolved to walk more. And every January it came to nothing. Nobody minded. Nobody noticed. I forgave myself, because I'm very good at that, and I suspect you are too.

Most of what gets written about resolutions says we fail because we go about them the wrong way. Be more specific, they say. Start smaller. Write it down. Nearly all of it is written for young people trying to squeeze more out of their working day. Not many of us round here are trying to be more productive. We're trying to stay in touch with each other.

I don't think method was ever my trouble. You can make "walk more" as specific as you like: twenty minutes, every morning, straight after breakfast. Lovely. Then it's a grey Tuesday, the kettle's on, and the only person you'd be letting down is you. That's an easy person to let down. I do it all the time.

Here's what changed. Two years ago my neighbour Joan had her hip done, and she needed someone to walk with while she got her strength back. We've walked every weekday at nine o'clock ever since. Rain, cold, it doesn't matter. I didn't set a better goal or buy a gadget. The only thing that changed was that Joan was waiting on her front step.

I'd never let Joan down. That's the whole secret, if there is one.

There is a catch, and I'd be fibbing if I left it out: you can't just invent a Joan. A friend who agrees to "keep you on track" isn't quite the same thing, because the two of you will let each other off by February. What works is somebody who actually needs you there, so that not turning up would cost them something. That's harder to arrange than a note on the fridge. But at our age it's often closer to hand than we think. Someone down the road is getting over an operation. Someone a few doors along has given up driving. Someone has just lost a husband or wife and doesn't much fancy the evenings.

So if you're thinking about a resolution this year, I'd skip the question of what you'd like to improve about yourself. Ask instead who might be glad to see you at nine o'clock. Start there, and the rest tends to follow.

As for Joan, she's quicker than me now, which I think is a bit cheeky. But there we are. She's on her step every weekday, and so am I.
