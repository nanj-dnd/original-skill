One year ago, I became a manager for the first time.

I thought the hard part would be the decisions. It turned out the hard part was everything around them.

A few things I learned:

**1. My calendar stopped being mine.**
I used to measure a good day by what I shipped. Now a good day is one where my team could ship because I cleared something out of their way. That reframe took me months, and I still catch myself missing the old scoreboard.

**2. "I'll just do it myself" is a trap.**
It's faster today and slower forever. Every time I grabbed a task because I could do it in 20 minutes, I took away a chance for someone to do it in 40 and own it next time.

**3. Silence is not agreement.**
Early on, I'd end a meeting thinking everyone was aligned. They weren't. They were being polite. I learned to ask "What would make this plan fail?" instead of "Any concerns?" The first question gets real answers.

**4. 1:1s are not status updates.**
If I spend them asking about projects, I've wasted them. The best 1:1s I had this year were about [something specific: a career worry, a frustration someone finally voiced, a piece of feedback I didn't want to hear].

**5. Feedback is a gift, but only if you unwrap it right away.**
I sat on hard conversations because I didn't want to be the bad guy. Every time, waiting made it worse for both of us. Clear is kind.

**6. I got it wrong a lot.**
[Optional: one honest, specific mistake. These are the lines people actually remember.]

I'm not sure I'm a good manager yet. I'm sure I'm a better one than I was in month one, mostly because of a team that was patient while I figured it out.

To anyone stepping into their first management role: you will feel like you're doing it badly. That feeling is mostly just the gap between how much you care and how much you know so far. The gap closes.

Thank you to [team / your own manager / a mentor] for the year.
