**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because they're vague, too ambitious, and driven by willpower instead of systems.

The default choices a pasted prompt makes:
- **Angle**: it's a design problem (bad goals), fixed with SMART goals and habit-stacking.
- **Examples**: gym memberships in January, "eat healthier," the oft-cited "most quit by February" line.
- **Structure**: hook → three reasons → three fixes → upbeat close.
- **Reader**: a generic self-improver who needs tips.
- **Tone**: warm, listicle-adjacent, mildly motivational.

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4d`:

**1. Angle.** The default says resolutions fail because the goals are badly designed. What's your actual take?
   a) Agree — it's mostly bad goal design
   b) The timing is arbitrary; January 1st is a terrible moment to change anything
   c) They fail because people resolve to fix the symptom, not the thing underneath
   d) Honestly, failing is fine — the point is the attempt, and "fail" is the wrong frame

**2. Your material.** The default uses stock examples (gym, diet). Do you have a resolution of your own that stuck or didn't, and roughly how long it lasted or what it was?
   a) Yes, one that failed — I'll describe it in a line
   b) Yes, one that stuck — I'll describe it in a line
   c) One of each, briefly
   d) No personal case; keep it general

**3. Structure.** The default is three reasons → three fixes → pep-talk ending. For a personal blog, do you want:
   a) Reasons plus a practical fix at the end
   b) Argument only, no tips — make the case and stop
   c) A single thread from your example to the general point
   d) Short, punchy, more essay than guide

**4. Tone.** It's your personal blog, under your name. Should it read as:
   a) Plain and conversational, first person
   b) Wry / a bit sceptical
   c) Reflective, slower
   d) Written in my own voice (I'll point you to a voice skill or samples)
