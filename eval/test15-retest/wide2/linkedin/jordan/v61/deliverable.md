A year ago I took on a marketing team of three. The most useful thing I've learned since fits in one sentence: my job is no longer the work. It's the people doing it.

That sounds obvious. In marketing, it is surprisingly easy to forget.

Marketers tend to be promoted for their craft: the sharp headline, the campaign that landed, the brief that rescued a launch. Those instincts don't switch off with a new title. When a draft arrives that isn't quite there, the quickest fix is to rewrite it yourself. It ships faster. It may even read better. And it teaches the person who wrote it only one thing: their version wasn't needed.

With a team of three, the arithmetic is unforgiving. Every hour spent redoing someone's work is an hour not spent making three people better at theirs. The leverage is no longer in the copy. It's in the feedback, the context and the trust that let someone own a piece from brief to launch.

That changes how a manager's week should be measured. "What did I produce?" is the wrong question. The right one is "What can my team do now that they couldn't before?"

That is harder to put on a dashboard. It is also the only measure that compounds.

One year in, here is the lesson I'd pass to anyone moving from doing the work to leading it: your craft earned you the role. Your team is the role.
