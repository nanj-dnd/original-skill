---

Twelve months leading a marketing team of three, and the most useful thing I can report is what I haven't mastered yet.

The core lesson is the one every new manager hears: your job is no longer the work. It's making the work possible for others. I agree with it completely. I just haven't finished learning it.

In marketing, that is harder than it sounds. The work is visible. A headline, a campaign brief, a launch email: every draft that crosses a manager's desk invites an edit, and every edit feels like help. On a team of three, the line between guiding and taking over is a single comment in the margin.

What the role asks for is mostly restraint. Asking what someone intended before suggesting what to change. Letting a strong version ship because it's theirs, even when it isn't the one you would have written. Judging a week by what the team moved forward, not by what you personally touched.

None of this is complicated. All of it runs against instinct, which is why one year isn't enough to get it right.

I'd rather say that plainly than wrap year one in a neat list of lessons. Management is a different job from the one most people are promoted for, and the first year is mostly learning how different it is.

Year two starts now. The principle stays the same. The practice is what I'm working on.
