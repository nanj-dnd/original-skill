**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt gets: "Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] runs a large fleet. Our software cuts fuel costs and delivery times by X% using AI route optimisation. Companies like [Logo] saved Y%. Worth a 15-minute call next week?" The choices behind it:

- **Angle**: cost and fuel savings, framed as a percentage.
- **Opener**: flattery or "I noticed your company…" research line.
- **Proof**: a named customer logo and a round savings figure.
- **Structure**: compliment → pain → product → stat → calendar ask, four short paragraphs.
- **Tone**: polished SaaS-sales voice, signed by a salesperson, 15-minute-call close.

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`):

1. Default angle is "save X% on fuel and miles". What does your software actually fix best for a head of ops?
   a) Fuel and mileage cost
   b) Drivers finishing late / overtime and missed delivery windows
   c) Dispatchers spending hours planning routes by hand each morning
   d) Something else (say what in a few words)

2. Default proof is a named customer and a round percentage. What real material can I use?
   a) One real customer result: name (or "a 60-truck regional carrier" style description) plus the number
   b) A real number from your own testing or a pilot, no customer name
   c) A specific pain you've heard from an ops head in a call (give the line or the gist)
   d) Nothing yet; write without a figure

3. Default opener is "I noticed your company…". How should it start?
   a) With the ops head's own problem, stated plainly, no flattery
   b) With a one-line question about how they plan routes today
   c) With the result from Q2, first sentence

4. Default is salesperson-polished, 15-minute-call ask. Whose name goes on it and what's the ask?
   a) Founder/CEO, plain and direct, asking for a 10-minute call
   b) Sales rep, warm but brief, asking for a call
   c) Founder, asking only for a reply or a quick "worth a look?" rather than a meeting
   d) Something else
