## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Management isn't about doing the work; it's about helping others do their best work."
- **Angle:** the shift from individual contributor to multiplier ("my success is now my team's success").
- **Examples:** generic lessons with no real moments: "listen more than you talk," "1:1s matter," "it's okay to not have all the answers," "delegate."
- **Structure:** a hook line ("A year ago I became a manager. Here's what I learned 👇"), then a numbered list of 5 to 7 lessons, then a thank-you to the team and a "What would you add?" question.
- **Reader:** other new or aspiring managers, broadly.
- **Tone:** humble, upbeat, a bit inspirational, with emoji and one-line paragraphs.

## A few questions first

Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`, and add any free text where you want.

**1. The default makes it a list of 5 to 7 lessons. What should the shape be?**
- a) One lesson, told through one real moment, in depth
- b) A before/after: what I believed going in vs. what I believe now
- c) The thing I got wrong, and what I'd tell myself on day one
- d) Keep the list, but make each item concrete

**2. Your real material. The default has no actual moments. Give me one or more, in your own words:**
- a) Something that happened (a hard conversation, a mistake, a win, a day it clicked)
- b) A number (team size, how many 1:1s, people hired or promoted, something you measured)
- c) A line someone said to you that stuck
- d) Nothing specific. Keep it general (it'll stay closer to the default)

**3. The default's lesson is "it's about the team, not me." What did you actually take away?**
- a) That one, and I want it said well
- b) Something less comfortable (e.g. I avoided feedback, I held on to the work, I was too nice or too controlling)
- c) Something about myself (what I'm good or bad at, what I now enjoy)
- d) Something else, in a few words

**4. How should it sound, and what are the facts? The default is upbeat and emoji-heavy.**
- a) Plain and direct, no emoji, a few short paragraphs
- b) Warm and reflective, still no hashtag stuffing
- c) Upbeat LinkedIn style, a couple of emoji is fine
- d) Something else

Also tell me, if any apply: your role or company (or whether to leave it out), anyone to thank or tag, a length limit, and anything to avoid (e.g. naming your team, mentioning your employer).
