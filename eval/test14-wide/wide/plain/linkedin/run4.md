**One year ago, I became a manager. Here's what I got wrong, and what I'd tell myself on day one.**

I thought managing was mostly about making good decisions. It's not. It's about making it possible for other people to make good decisions, and then getting out of the way.

Some things I learned the hard way:

**1. Your calendar is the job now.**
I spent the first three months trying to stay "hands-on" so I wouldn't lose my edge. What I actually did was become a bottleneck. The moment I stopped treating 1:1s and planning as interruptions to my "real work," the team started moving faster than I ever could have alone.

**2. Silence is not agreement.**
Early on, I'd share a plan, hear no objections, and assume buy-in. Then things would quietly stall. Turns out people were holding back because I was the new manager and nobody wanted to be the first to push back. Now I ask directly: "What's the strongest argument against this?" and wait through the awkward pause.

**3. Feedback delayed is feedback denied.**
I sat on a hard conversation for six weeks because I was waiting for the "right moment." There wasn't one. The person told me afterward they wished I'd said something sooner — they'd sensed something was off and it was worse not knowing.

**4. You will not be liked all the time, and that's fine.**
I wanted to be the manager everyone enjoyed working with. But some of my best calls this year — [reprioritizing a project / saying no to a request / changing a process] — frustrated people in the moment. Trust comes from being fair and consistent, not from being easy.

**5. Your old strengths can become new weaknesses.**
I was promoted because I was good at [your IC skill — e.g. shipping fast, deep technical work]. As a manager, that same instinct made me jump in to "fix" things instead of coaching people through them. Letting someone struggle a bit is often the most useful thing I can do.

**What I'd tell myself on day one:** You're not the smartest person in the room anymore, and you shouldn't try to be. Your job is to make the room smarter.

To my team — thanks for your patience while I figured this out. Still figuring it out.
