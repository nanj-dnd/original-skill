## Shortlist

**A. The forgiving judge (2)**
We don't drop resolutions because we're weak or our plans are vague. We drop them because we're kind to ourselves. On a wet Tuesday you'll always let yourself off, but you can't let yourself off on Joan's behalf. So the advice isn't "be stricter with yourself," it's "put someone else in the judge's chair."
- **Only yours because:** answer 1 ("very good at forgiving ourselves") and your line in answer 3, "I'd never let Joan down. I let myself down all the time."
- **Strongest objection:** it can sound like a nicer name for an accountability partner, which is the self-help technique you didn't want.
- **Test:** it has to survive "so just get a buddy?" The post needs to show that Joan wasn't checking up on you; she needed you to turn up, and that's different.

**B. Wrong age, wrong goal (4)**
Resolution advice assumes you want to be more productive. Most of us here gave that up gladly some time ago. What we're after is staying connected, so it's no wonder a solitary "walk more" never took: it was solving a problem we don't have. A resolution that brings in another person is aimed at the thing we actually want.
- **Only yours because:** answer 4 (retirees in town, connection rather than productivity), with Joan as the proof.
- **Strongest objection:** some of your readers do have real health goals, and "it's about connection" could brush those aside.
- **Test:** your readers will recognise it straight away or they won't. If the post gets "that's exactly it" over the fence, it worked.

**C. Borrowed specificity (6)**
The honest wrinkle: you say method isn't the issue, yet "9 o'clock every weekday, rain or cold" is exactly what the method people would tell you to write down. The difference is that you never chose it. Joan's hip and her front step chose it. A routine you set for yourself is easy to renegotiate, and one somebody else's need sets for you isn't.
- **Only yours because:** it comes from the tension between your answer 1 and the details of your own case.
- **Strongest objection:** a sceptic would say "so method *does* matter, you just got it for free." This is the angle that comes closest to the default.
- **Test:** it has to survive that sceptic. It holds only if the post shows that the time on its own wasn't what kept you going, since you could have picked 9 o'clock any January.

**D. She's quicker than me now (10, an unlikely one)**
You took it on to help Joan get her strength back, and two years later she's the one setting the pace. What holds a resolution isn't being needed, it's needing each other: she waits for you and you wait for her. Resolutions you make alone fail because there's no one to wait for.
- **Only yours because:** a single detail in answer 3 that no pasted prompt could have produced.
- **Strongest objection:** it may be too tender for a post meant to explain why resolutions fail. The argument risks turning into a story about a friendship.
- **Test:** whether it still makes the point to a reader who has never met Joan. If they finish thinking "nice story" rather than "who could I walk with?", it hasn't worked.

**My recommendation: A.** It's built on your own sharpest line ("I'd never let Joan down. I let myself down all the time."). It gives you advice that doesn't read as a technique, and it has room for B's point about connection and for the "I don't have a Joan" reader in a paragraph each.
