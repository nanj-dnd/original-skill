## What everyone gets

**Claim:** "Managing is about people, not tasks. Your job is no longer doing the work, it's helping others do theirs."
- **Structure:** a hook line ("A year ago I became a manager. Here's what nobody told me 👇"), then 5 to 7 numbered lessons.
- **Examples:** stock lessons: stop doing it yourself, delegate, listen more than you talk, give feedback early, impostor syndrome is normal.
- **Reader:** a general LinkedIn audience, especially other new managers.
- **Tone:** humble and inspirational, with short one-line paragraphs.
- **Ending:** "What did your first year teach you? 👇"

## A few questions first

Reply like `1b 2d 3a 4c`. Add free text anywhere.

**1. The main lesson.** The default says "it's about people, not the work." Which should yours be?
- a) Keep that, but make it specific to me
- b) Something I got wrong at first and then fixed
- c) Something nobody warned me about (for example: managing up, how lonely it gets, losing a good person, or no longer being "one of the team")
- d) My own: ___

**2. Your real material.** The default uses generic lessons. Give me one real moment: something that happened, a number, or a line someone said to you. Also tell me your role and field, and anything I should not name (the company, team members).
- a) A 1:1 or conversation that changed how I manage: ___
- b) A mistake I made and what it cost: ___
- c) A number (team size, hires, someone promoted, someone who left): ___
- d) Something else: ___

**3. Shape.** The default is a numbered list of 5 or more lessons. Which shape do you want?
- a) One story, one lesson, told properly
- b) A short list of 3, where each lesson comes from a real moment
- c) Before and after: what I believed on day one versus what I believe now
- d) Keep the list format, just done well

**4. How it should sound with your name on it.** The default is humble-inspirational, with emoji and a question at the end.
- a) Plain and conversational, with no emoji or bait question
- b) Polished and professional, since recruiters and leadership read it
- c) Honest and a little wry, willing to say "I was bad at this"
- d) Other, or a length limit: ___
