# Why Most New Year's Resolutions Fail (and the One I Kept)

Every January I make resolutions, and by February most of them have quietly disappeared. I don't think I'm unusual. Ask anyone how last year's resolutions went and you'll usually get a laugh before you get an answer.

As far as I can tell, the reason is simple: people lose motivation. On January 1st you're full of good intentions. The year is fresh and anything seems possible. Then it's a wet Tuesday in the third week, you're tired, and the thing you promised yourself feels like a chore. Motivation is a feeling, and feelings don't last. They were never going to.

So the real problem isn't that motivation fades, because that's guaranteed. The problem is that most resolutions have nothing else to run on. They need you to want it as badly on day forty as you did on day one, and nobody does.

I know this because I've broken plenty of resolutions. Nothing dramatic, just the usual ones that fizzle out without anyone noticing, including me. But I've also kept one.

I'm learning Welsh on an app, and I'm on a 700-day streak. I'd love to say that's down to my iron discipline. It isn't. It's down to my 12-year-old daughter.

She's learning too, and at some point we started checking each other's streaks at dinner. It's a small thing and takes about a minute. But it means that every evening, someone at the table is going to ask. On the nights I'd rather not bother, I do my lesson anyway, because I'm not going to be the one who breaks the streak in front of my daughter.

There have been plenty of days when I didn't feel like it at all. My motivation came and went just like everyone else's. The streak survived because it never depended on motivation. It depended on dinner, which happens every day whether I'm in the mood or not, and on someone across the table who would notice.

That's the most useful thing I can tell you about resolutions. Expect your motivation to run out, because it will. Before it does, tie your resolution to something that keeps going without it: a person, a routine, a time of day when you already show up. Then let that carry you through the days you don't care.

Blwyddyn Newydd Dda. Happy New Year.
