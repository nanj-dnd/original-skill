A year into managing, here's what I'd tell anyone about to start: the hard part isn't the decisions or the workload. It's the conversations you'd rather not have.

Three things I'd pass on.

1. Waiting doesn't make it smaller.
The thing you don't say this week, you say next month, and by then it has company. A small note early is a favour. A long one late is a surprise.

2. Soft isn't the same as kind.
Cushion a hard point enough and the person leaves unsure what you meant. That doesn't protect them. It just hands them the problem without the information.

3. Most people already suspect it.
A hard conversation is rarely news to the person on the other side. More often than you'd expect, the reaction is some version of relief that someone finally said it out loud.

None of this makes the conversations pleasant. It just makes the cost of skipping them obvious.

If you're about to become a manager: put the conversation you're dreading in the calendar. That's the job.
