**What everyone gets**

Main claim: "Managing is nothing like being a strong individual contributor; the job is to make others succeed, not to do the work yourself."

Choices a pasted prompt makes:
- **Angle**: humble confession → list of lessons (delegation is hard, 1:1s matter, feedback early, you can't fix everything).
- **Examples**: generic ("I used to jump in and solve it myself"), no specific person or number.
- **Structure**: one-line hook, 5–7 bullet lessons each with an emoji, closing question ("What did you learn in your first year?").
- **Reader**: everyone on LinkedIn, aimed at other new managers.
- **Tone**: earnest, polished, a little inspirational.

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`:

**1. Angle.** The default claims "the job is to make others succeed, not do the work." Which is closest to your real takeaway?
- a) That one: letting go of doing the work myself was the hard part
- b) The hardest part was the people stuff I had no training for (performance conversations, someone struggling, conflict)
- c) I was wrong about what managing was *for*: it's less about the team and more about what's between the team and the rest of the company (priorities, shielding, context)
- d) Something else: tell me in a sentence

**2. Your real material.** One true moment from the year. The post will be built on it, so the more specific the better:
- a) A conversation with one person on my team (what happened, roughly what was said)
- b) A mistake I made and what it cost (a missed deadline, a person who left, a project that slipped)
- c) A number: team size, how many 1:1s, hours in meetings, something I tracked
- d) I'd rather not share specifics: write it general

**3. Shape.** The default is a hook plus a bulleted list of lessons and a closing question.
- a) One lesson, told as a short story, no list
- b) Three lessons max, each with the thing that taught me it
- c) The bulleted list is fine, just make it mine

**4. Sound.** Whose name is on it and how should it read?
- a) Plain and direct, like I'd say it to a colleague
- b) Reflective, a bit more polished
- c) Dry, with some self-deprecation
