1. It's not an official policy announcement, and nobody's buying licenses on my say-so. It's closer to a philosophy piece, but with a specific proposal attached. I'm not worried about code quality. The assistants write decent code and I'm happy for people to keep using them for new work. My worry is skill atrophy. I want to make the case that juniors should do their first six months of debugging without an assistant. Pilots still train on manual flying even though the autopilot can fly the whole approach, because when it disconnects you have to be able to hand-fly the airplane. So the bottom line is: use the tools to build, but build the debugging muscle yourself first.

2. Technically the whole company is in the room, so sales, HR and the rest will hear it. But I really care about the engineers and their managers. Managers are the ones who'd have to back the six-month thing. I don't need it dumbed down for everyone else. I just don't want it so deep in the weeds that the managers tune out.

3. They're well past "new territory." Usage is heavy and has been for a while. I run our incident reviews and do on-call, and I can see the effect. Over the last six months our median time-to-resolve on P2s went from 38 minutes to 61. In reviews I keep seeing engineers who can't follow a stack trace through code they supposedly wrote.

4. Data-driven and serious, but not doom-and-gloom. I really don't want this to come across as the old guy yelling at the autopilot. I'm not anti-AI. A bit of dry humor is fine if it lands naturally, but the numbers and the real incidents should carry it, not jokes.
