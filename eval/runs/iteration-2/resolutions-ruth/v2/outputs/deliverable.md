# Nobody Was Waiting for Me

Every January for more years than I'd care to count, I made the same resolution: walk more. And every January, give or take a week, I stopped. Nothing dramatic. I'd look out at a wet Tuesday, decide tomorrow would do, and forgive myself by teatime. I'm very good at forgiving myself. I suspect most of us are.

You'll have seen the usual explanations for why resolutions fail. The goal was too vague, apparently, or too big, or we hadn't got the right system. Most of that advice is written for younger people trying to squeeze more into their day, and I don't think it explains much. You can make "walk more" as specific as you like: twenty minutes, every morning, marked on the calendar. If you skip it, who notices? Only you, and you've already let yourself off.

Here's what actually changed things for me. Two years ago my neighbour Joan had her hip done and needed someone to walk with while she got her strength back. So I said I would. We've walked every weekday at nine o'clock ever since. Rain or cold, it makes no difference. I didn't find a better method or buy a step counter. The only new thing was Joan, waiting on her front step.

I'll be honest about the difference, because it doesn't flatter me. I let myself down all the time. I would never let Joan down. And I didn't set this up to keep myself on track. Joan needed a hand, and the walking came along with it. That, I think, is why it stuck. It was never really my resolution. It was a promise to her.

Which makes me wonder whether we've got the whole business the wrong way round. Most resolutions are about improving ourselves on our own, in private, where nobody is expecting us. But at our age most of us aren't trying to be more productive. We're trying to stay connected: to keep seeing people, to be some use to someone. Those are the things that get us out of the door on a cold morning.

So if there's something you'd like to stick this year, my only suggestion is this. Don't tie it to a better plan. Tie it to a person. Think of someone who'd be glad of the company, and go and knock.

As for Joan, she's quicker than me now. I find that a bit cheeky, but there we are.
