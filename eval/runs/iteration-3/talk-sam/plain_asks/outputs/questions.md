Happy to write this. A few quick questions first so the talk sounds like you and fits the room:

1. **What's your position?** Do you want to argue for "yes, use it for everything," against it, or something in between (for example "yes for X, never for Y")? Or should the talk lay out both sides and not pick one?

2. **Who's in the room, and what's your role?** Is this the whole company, including non-engineers, or mostly engineering? And are you speaking as an engineer, a manager, or a leader? That changes how much jargon I can use and how much weight a recommendation from you carries.

3. **What should people do or feel afterward?** For example: start using a tool, be more careful with it, support a new policy, or just have a shared way to talk about it. Is this tied to an actual decision or policy change?

4. **What's actually happening at your company?** Do you have tools rolled out, an existing policy, or real stories (a big productivity win, a bug or security scare, an adoption number)? One concrete local example usually works better than general industry claims.

5. **Tone and format?** Casual and a bit funny, or straightforward and serious? Will you have slides, or is it just you talking? Are there sensitive topics to handle carefully or avoid, like worries about jobs, a recent incident, or legal and IP concerns?

If you'd rather not answer all of these, the first and third matter most.
