**Fixed vs open.** Fixed: about 400 words, personal blog, and any statistic used. One flag up front: the popular "80% of resolutions fail by February" figure has no solid source I can point to. The best-known study (Norcross et al., 2002) found about 46% of people who made resolutions were still on track at six months, compared with 4% of people who wanted to change but made no resolution. Everything else is open: the claim, the angle, what goes in, the structure and the reader.

## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** Most resolutions fail because they're too big, too vague and powered by willpower that runs out by February. The fix is to make them smaller, specific and habit-based.

- **Thesis:** failure is a design flaw in the goal (not SMART enough), and better goal-setting fixes it.
- **Hook:** the "80% fail by February" or "Quitter's Day" statistic, plus the crowded January gym.
- **Examples:** generic ones (lose weight, go to the gym, read more), no real person.
- **Structure:** stat, then 3 or 4 reasons, then a bulleted tips list, then an upbeat "you've got this" close.
- **Reader:** any self-improver who wants to do better this year.
- **Leaves out:** the writer's own experience, the shakiness of the statistic, and whether failing is actually a bad thing.
