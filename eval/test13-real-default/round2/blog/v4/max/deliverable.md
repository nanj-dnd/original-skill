**Resolutions don't fail. They get forgotten.**

I've made New Year's resolutions. I couldn't tell you what most of them were. That's not quite the same as failing at them. Failing would mean I tried, hit something hard, and stopped. What actually happened is that they drifted off sometime in January and I didn't notice they were gone.

I think that's the usual story, and I think it says something about where resolutions come from.

Most of them come from the date. The year is ending, there's a general feeling that something should be reviewed and improved, and so you produce a goal to fill the slot. The goal might be a perfectly good one. But the thing that made you say it out loud was the calendar, not anything in your life. Nothing changed on December 31st except the number.

That matters, because the date is the one part of a resolution that doesn't last. By the second week of January it's just a Tuesday. Whatever energy came from the sense of a fresh start is gone, because the start isn't fresh anymore. If the resolution was borrowed from that feeling, there's nothing left holding it up. It doesn't break. It just has no reason to still be there.

Compare that with the changes that actually stick. People tend to make them at odd times: a doctor says something, a job ends, someone moves, a bad night turns into a decision. The timing is ugly and the change is specific, because it's an answer to something real. Nobody forgets a decision they made because they had to. You forget the ones you made because it was the right week to make one.

This is why the usual advice doesn't quite reach the problem. Make it smaller, make it specific, attach it to a habit. All sensible. But it treats the problem as one of design, and I think it's mostly one of origin. A well-designed resolution with no reason behind it is still a resolution with no reason behind it. You've built a better container for something that was never going to be there in February.

None of this means January is a bad time to change something. It means the date shouldn't be the reason. If there's something you'd want to do anyway, and it happens to be December, fine. If you're reaching for a goal because the year is ending and it feels like you should have one, that's the kind I've made before, and I couldn't tell you how any of them ended, because they didn't end. They were just gone.
