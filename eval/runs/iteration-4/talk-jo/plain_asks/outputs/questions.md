Happy to help with this. A few questions first so the talk sounds like you and fits the room:

1. **What's your position?** Are you arguing yes, no, or "it depends," or do you want to lay out both sides and let the room decide? If you lean one way, what's the main reason?

2. **Who are you, and who's listening?** What's your role (engineer, eng manager, exec, someone outside engineering)? Is the all-hands mostly engineers, or the whole company, including sales, ops and leadership, who may not know how these tools work day to day?

3. **Why now?** Is there a policy decision coming, a recent incident (a bug, a security or licensing scare, a big productivity win), pressure from leadership, or a disagreement among the teams? And how much do your engineers use these tools today?

4. **What do you have that's your own?** Things like a story from your team, an internal number (PR throughput, review time, defect rates), or a moment where AI clearly helped or clearly hurt. One real example from your company will land better than any general argument I could write.

5. **What should people do or feel afterward?** For example: accept a new guideline, try the tools, be more careful, or just understand the trade-offs.

6. **Tone and format:** Serious, light, or a bit provocative? Will you use slides, or just talk? And do you want the opening paragraph written to be read word for word, or as talking points?

If you only have time for a couple, 1, 3 and 4 matter most.
