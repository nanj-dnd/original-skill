Here are the questions that will help most:

**The substance**
1. What are 2–4 lessons that actually stuck with you? Rough notes are fine. Even a phrase like "I stopped trying to be the best engineer on the team" gives me plenty to work with.
2. Is there a specific moment, mistake, or story that captures one of those lessons? A concrete anecdote, like a tough 1:1, a call you got wrong, or a moment something clicked, is usually what makes these posts land instead of reading like generic advice.
3. What surprised you most, or what did you get wrong going in?

**Context**
4. What field or role are you in, and what's your background? For example, an engineer promoted to lead your former peers, a new hire into a manager role, or something else.
5. Roughly how big is your team, and is there anything you'd rather *not* mention, like your company name or specifics about reports?

**Tone & format**
6. What vibe do you want? Reflective and honest, light and self-deprecating, punchy and practical, or something else?
7. What length are you thinking? A short post of about 100–150 words, or a longer one of about 250–400 words with a hook and a list?
8. How do you feel about the typical LinkedIn style, with one-line paragraphs, emojis, and a closing question like "What did you learn your first year?" Lean into it, or keep it more understated?
9. Who are you hoping reads it? Other new managers, your own team, people in your industry, or potential employers?

Answer whichever ones you have thoughts on. Skip the rest and I'll make sensible choices.
