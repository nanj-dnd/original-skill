## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "AI coding assistants are powerful, but not for everything. Use them for the boring parts and keep humans in charge of the parts that matter. It's augmentation, not replacement."

Choices it makes:
1. **Thesis:** a safe middle path ("it depends"), with the line drawn by task type: yes for boilerplate, tests and docs; no for architecture and security.
2. **Reader:** assumes the room is engineers, or leaders deciding policy for engineers.
3. **Stakes:** code quality and risk (hallucinated APIs, security holes), plus a nod to juniors' skills wasting away.
4. **Speaker:** a neutral explainer with no personal stake or story.
5. **Structure:** an opening statistic, then pros, cons and guidelines, ending with "use it wisely."
6. **Leaves out:** anything specific to your company, what it means for the non-engineers in the room, and a concrete ask.
