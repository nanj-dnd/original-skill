MY ANSWERS:

1: Wrong question. "Resolutions fail" mixes up attempts and outcomes. Most attempts fail, sure. That's normal, and it isn't what decides whether you get there. Closest to c, but the point isn't partial credit. Count attempts, not outcomes.

2: A failed attempt is data. My first three quits weren't failures. They're how I found my triggers: the first coffee, driving, my brother. You don't fix failing with tips. You learn from it.

3: Smoking. 15 years a smoker, quit for good on the 4th serious try, 6 years clean now. Attempt 2 lasted 5 weeks and broke at a wedding. That's how I learned weddings plus drinking were the trigger, and attempt 4 was planned around exactly that. What changed wasn't willpower. I knew what would get me.

4: An argument, but with my story as the evidence. No numbered tips. Readers are friends, a few former students, people from a quit-smoking forum. The forum people have all relapsed, so don't talk down to them.

5: Yes, go after how "failure" gets measured. If you want a number that holds up, use the one about most successful quitters needing several attempts. Check it first. And no "be kind to yourself" closing. If it reads like a self-help book I'm not posting it.
