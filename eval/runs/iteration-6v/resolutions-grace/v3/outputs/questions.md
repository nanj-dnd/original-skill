**Fixed parts:** any statistic about resolution failure rates. The usual one ("80% of resolutions fail by February") has a weak source, and I can't check it here. If the post uses a figure, I'll mark it unchecked or leave it out.
**Open parts:** the claim, the angle, what to include, the structure, the reader, and the ending.

## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** Most resolutions fail because they're vague, too ambitious and run on willpower. Make them specific, small and habit-based (systems, not goals) and they'll stick.

Choices it made:
1. **Thesis:** the person's method is the problem (vague goals, willpower), not the resolution or the ritual.
2. **Hook:** opens with a failure statistic ("80% fail by February", "Quitter's Day").
3. **Examples:** generic ones, like the January gym crowd and the dry-January slip.
4. **Structure:** a stat hook, then 3 or 4 numbered reasons, then fixes, then a "you've got this" ending.
5. **Reader:** a generic self-improver who arrives from search.
6. **Leaves out:** the writer's own experience, and whether "failing" a resolution is actually bad.
