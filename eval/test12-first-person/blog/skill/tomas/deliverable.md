**Count the attempts**

Every January someone writes that most New Year's resolutions fail. The numbers vary, the conclusion doesn't: people set goals, people quit, the gym empties out by February.

The frame is wrong. Most attempts fail. That's not the same thing, and the difference matters.

I smoked for fifteen years. I quit on my fourth serious attempt, and I've been clean for six. By the usual way of counting, the first three attempts were failures. Three resolutions, three collapses. That's the story the "most resolutions fail" articles would tell about me, right up until the fourth one happened to stick.

But that isn't what happened. Attempt two lasted five weeks and broke at a wedding. Counted as an outcome, that's five weeks of work gone over one evening. Counted as anything else, it was information. Weddings and drinking were my trigger. I didn't know that going in, and nothing short of standing at a wedding with a drink in my hand was going to tell me. By attempt four I knew, and attempt four was planned around exactly that.

The same goes for the rest. The first three attempts, between them, taught me where the cigarette actually lived: the first coffee, driving, and my brother. Not "stress", not "habit". Those three specific things. I didn't know those going in either. The attempts found them. Attempt four wasn't a better resolution. It was the same resolution with three attempts' worth of data behind it.

So here's the counting mistake. If you count outcomes, I failed three times and succeeded once. If you count attempts, I made four, each better informed than the last, and the fourth closed it out. Most people who quit smoking for good needed several tries. That isn't a sign their resolution was weak. It's how it normally goes.

This changes what a broken attempt means. It doesn't mean you lacked willpower, and it doesn't mean the resolution was wrong. It means you now know something you couldn't have learned any other way: the exact situation that gets you. Write it down. Next attempt, that situation is the thing you plan around.

Most resolutions don't fail. Most attempts do, and the resolution is what's left standing across them. If your January attempt breaks, look at where it broke. That's the whole point of it.
