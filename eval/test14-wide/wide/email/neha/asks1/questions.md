Happy to. A few questions so the email lands rather than reads like a template:

1. **What's the product, concretely?** Name, and the one thing it does better than the obvious alternatives (Descartes, Onfleet, Google's OR tools, a spreadsheet). "Route optimisation" on its own won't get past a head of ops who sees this pitch weekly.

2. **Do you have a proof point?** A number from an existing customer (e.g. "cut fuel spend 11% at a 90-truck fleet") is the single most useful thing I can put in the email. If you have none yet, say so and I'll write around it honestly.

3. **Who are you to them?** Your name, role, company, and whether there's any warm angle: a mutual contact, you saw them hiring dispatchers, they posted about delivery delays, you met at a trade show. Cold with a hook beats cold without one.

4. **Who's the recipient, and what do you know about them?** Name if you have it, company type (last-mile parcel? LTL freight? food distribution?), rough fleet size, region. The pain of a 40-van grocery delivery fleet is different from a 300-trailer linehaul operation.

5. **What's the ask?** 15-minute call, a free route audit on their actual data, a demo, a reply? One ask only, so tell me which.

6. **Tone and constraints.** UK or US spelling (you wrote "optimisation", so UK by default)? Formal or plain-spoken? Hard word limit? Any claims or words you're not allowed to use (compliance, "guaranteed", etc.)?

7. **Pricing or trial**: do you want to mention a pilot, a price point, or keep that off the first email entirely?

Answer what you can; anything you skip I'll make a sensible default and flag it.
