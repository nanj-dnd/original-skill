**One year ago I became a manager. Here's what nobody told me.**

I thought the hard part would be the decisions. It wasn't. It was the silence.

A few things I learned the hard way:

**1. Your output is no longer your output.**
For years I measured myself by what I shipped. Suddenly the scoreboard was other people's work. It took me months to stop jumping in to "just fix it" — every time I did, I robbed someone of the chance to grow.

**2. One-on-ones are not status updates.**
My first few months of 1:1s were glorified standups. Once I started asking "What's getting in your way?" and "What do you want to be doing in two years?", everything changed. The real information lives in those answers.

**3. Clarity is kindness.**
I avoided hard feedback because I wanted to be liked. What I was actually doing was letting people stay stuck. Vague praise and vague concern help no one.

**4. You will be lonely, and that's normal.**
You're no longer one of the team, and you're not quite leadership either. Find other first-time managers. Talk to them. Often.

**5. Protect your people's time like it's your own.**
Half the job is saying no to things so your team can say yes to the right things.

**6. Your mood is contagious.**
A stressed manager makes a stressed team. I learned to process my own anxiety somewhere other than the team channel.

I made plenty of mistakes this year. I'll make more. But I'm starting to understand that management isn't a promotion — it's a career change.

To everyone who's been patient with me while I figured this out: thank you.

What did *you* learn in your first year managing? I'd genuinely love to hear it.
