> I was asked to talk about whether engineers should be using AI coding assistants for everything. Good news: this is the shortest debate of the day, because we already do. Pretty much everything. It's just how we work now. So I want to talk about something more useful, which is what that changed. What he thinks: the real risk is skill atrophy, not code quality. Over the last 6 months their median time-to-resolve for P2 incidents went from 38 to 61 minutes, and in incident reviews he keeps seeing engineers who can't read a stack trace through code they "wrote". Real case: a 2am outage where the on-call engineer pasted the error into the assistant four times, got four different confident fixes, and it took Marcus 10 minutes to find it by reading the log.

Audience: whole company, but he mostly cares about engineers and their managers hearing it.

How he answers: measured, slightly long, likes analogies from aviation (he's a private pilot).
