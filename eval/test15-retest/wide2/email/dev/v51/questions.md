## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Our software cuts your fuel and delivery costs, so let's talk."
- **Opening:** "I noticed [Company] has been growing…", a generic personalisation line.
- **Pain points:** fuel costs, late deliveries and driver overtime, listed together.
- **Proof:** "Clients see up to 20% savings," with no name or context.
- **Structure:** three short paragraphs (hook, benefit list, call to action).
- **Ask:** "Do you have 15 minutes for a call next week?"
- **Tone:** friendly-corporate, signed by a sales rep.

## A few questions first

Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`.

**1. What real result can the email stand on?** The default uses a vague "up to 20%".
- a) A named or describable customer with a number (for example, "a 60-van parcel firm cut planned miles by X% in Y weeks"). Tell me the case and the figure.
- b) A measure from a pilot or trial, even if it was small. Give me the number and what was measured.
- c) No customer results yet, so lean on what the product specifically does.
- d) Something else you have, such as a demo stat or a time-to-plan figure. Paste it.

**2. What should the hook be?** The default leads with fuel cost savings, which every vendor claims.
- a) A specific daily headache, such as re-planning routes when orders or drivers change at 6am.
- b) Low switching pain: it works with their current TMS or telematics, so nothing gets ripped out.
- c) A trigger about them, such as a new depot, a hiring push or a contract win. Tell me what you know about the target.
- d) Keep the cost and fuel angle, but make it concrete with your number from Q1.

**3. Whose name is on it, and how should it sound?** The default is a polished sales rep voice.
- a) Founder or CEO, plain and direct, peer to peer.
- b) Sales or BD rep, professional but not stiff.
- c) Someone with ops or logistics background, talking shop.

**4. What's the ask?** The default asks for a 15-minute call.
- a) Offer to run one week of their real routes through the software and send back the comparison.
- b) A low-commitment reply: "Worth a look? Just reply yes and I'll send a 2-minute video."
- c) Keep the short call, but tie it to a specific outcome.
- d) A pilot offer, for example one depot for 30 days. Tell me the terms.

If you'd rather skip these, say "just write it." I'll pick one non-default angle the task supports and keep it free of invented specifics.
