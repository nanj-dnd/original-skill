Not a one-answer task, so here's the default first.

**What everyone gets**
Main claim: build a consumer app that nudges individuals toward lower-carbon choices — most often a carbon-footprint tracker, usually with an AI layer (snap a receipt or meal photo, get a CO₂e estimate and a greener swap).
Choices it makes:
- Angle: individual behaviour change, not infrastructure, policy, or adaptation.
- Examples: receipt/meal scanner, "carbon score" dashboard, gamified streaks.
- Structure: problem → solution → tech stack → demo script with a scripted "happy path" on stage.
- Reader: hackathon judges who reward polish and a visible AI feature.
- Tone: pitch-deck enthusiastic, with a big-number stat about emissions up front.

Four questions so the idea fits your team rather than the generic one. Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`.

**1. Who is it for?** (Default: individual consumers tracking their own footprint.)
a) Consumers — but something people would actually open twice
b) A specific operator — a building manager, farmer, fleet owner, school, café
c) A city, utility, or government with public data to act on
d) People *adapting* to climate effects (heat, floods, air quality), not reducing emissions

**2. What can your team actually build in a weekend?** (Default assumes a web app + an LLM API call.)
a) Web/mobile front end plus API calls — no hardware, no heavy data work
b) Comfortable with real datasets, maps, or ML — we'd rather do something with data
c) We have hardware/sensors (Raspberry Pi, Arduino, phone sensors) and want to use them
d) Mixed team; tell me what you'd suggest and I'll pick

**3. Is there a real dataset, place, or person you have access to?** (Default uses made-up sample data.)
a) A public dataset we like (e.g. a city's open energy/air/transport data) — name it
b) A real place or organisation we can get numbers from (our campus, a workplace, a relative's farm or shop)
c) Nothing specific yet — open to whatever data is easiest to get
d) We have something unusual (an API key, a sensor feed, a contact) — tell me what

**4. How is the demo judged and what wins there?** (Default: a 3-minute stage walkthrough on a scripted happy path.)
a) Live demo, short pitch — judges reward polish and a wow moment
b) Judges are technical or domain experts — they reward a working core and honest limits
c) Video or booth format — we need something people can poke at themselves
d) Not sure / mostly for fun — we care more about learning than winning

Also useful, if you have it: team size, and how many hours of build time you really get.
