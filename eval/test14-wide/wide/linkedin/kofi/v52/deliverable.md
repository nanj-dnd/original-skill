Four months into my first year as a manager, someone on my team resigned.

In her exit interview she said she'd never received clear feedback from me.

I thought everything was fine.

If I had to pick one thing from the year, it's that. Not because it's dramatic. Because of how wide the gap was. She spent four months not knowing where she stood with me. I spent the same four months with no idea that was the case. Two people in the same meetings, with completely different pictures of what was going on.

What I had to stop doing was treating "nobody's raised a problem" as "everyone knows where they stand." Those feel like the same thing from the manager's chair. They aren't. Someone who isn't complaining might be fine. They might also be sitting in a fog, having decided it isn't worth asking you to clear it.

The uncomfortable part isn't that I was wrong. It's that I had no idea, right up until she told me on the way out.

So if you're in your first year and your read on the team is "everything's fine," ask where that read came from. Then ask the people on your team, one at a time, whether they know what you think of their work. If the question feels unnecessary, that's roughly how I felt too.
