# I Tracked My New Year's Resolutions for Three Years. Here's Why Most of Them Failed.

For the last three years I've logged every New Year's resolution I've made in a spreadsheet. The count so far: 11 resolutions, 2 that stuck.

That's a bad hit rate. But the spreadsheet showed me a clear pattern. The two that worked had one thing in common, and none of the nine that failed had it.

## The nine that failed

Every resolution that failed needed me to make a decision every day. Choose to work out. Choose not to spend. Make the better choice again tomorrow.

That's a plan built on willpower, and willpower is a weak plan. It holds up in the first week of January, while you're motivated. But a resolution that runs on daily decisions has to win hundreds of times in a row, and it only has to lose a few times before you give up. Every tired day and busy week is another chance for it to break.

## The two that stuck

The two that worked each took one setup action. After that, they ran on their own.

**An automatic transfer to savings on payday.** I set it up once. The money moves before I can spend it, so I never have to decide each month whether to save. Saving happens unless I go out of my way to stop it.

**Cancelling the gym membership and buying a doorway pull-up bar.** The gym meant deciding every day to go there. The bar is right at home, with no trip involved. Cancelling the membership also ended a recurring bill, so it was a money win too.

Neither one asks anything of me day to day. That's why they lasted.

## How to pick a resolution that lasts

Before you commit to anything this year, run it through these checks:

1. **Count the decisions.** If you'd have to choose it every day, expect it to fail. Look for a version that needs just one choice.
2. **Find the setup action.** What can you do once, this week, so the default does the work? Automate a transfer, cancel a subscription, move the equipment where you'll see it.
3. **Make the good option the easy one.** Put it where you already are. Add friction to the thing you want less of.
4. **Automate your money goals.** Savings, debt payments, retirement contributions: if your bank or employer can schedule it, let them.
5. **Track it.** A simple spreadsheet is enough. Three years of mine taught me more than any pep talk about motivation.

You don't need more willpower this January. You need fewer decisions.
