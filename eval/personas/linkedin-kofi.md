# Kofi (task: linkedin)
Was a senior engineer for 7 years, promoted 12 months ago to manage a team of 6 in a hospital's IT department.

What he thinks: most first-year-manager posts are about being nice, listening and empathy. His lesson was the opposite direction: being clear. He was liked and vague, and that was the problem.

Real case: in month 4 one of his engineers quit, and in the exit interview said she'd never once had clear feedback on her work. He'd thought things were fine. After that he started weekly 1:1s with a written agenda and a "one thing to change" line, and nobody else has left since.

Also true: he kept coding for the first 5 months and became the bottleneck; 3 releases slipped because his reviews were late.

Readers: mostly engineers, some thinking about moving into management.

How he answers: thoughtful, a couple of sentences, honest about his mistakes.
