Happy to help with this. A few questions first so the outline sounds like you and fits the room:

1. **What's your position?** Do you think engineers *should* use AI assistants for everything, shouldn't, or is your answer "it depends" with a line you want to draw? Or are you presenting both sides and leaving the decision open?

2. **What do you want to happen after the talk?** For example: announce or preview a policy, change how people are working now, get leadership to fund or approve tools, or just start a conversation.

3. **Who are you, and who's listening?** What's your role (IC engineer, eng manager, CTO, someone outside engineering)? Is the audience mostly engineers, or the whole company, including sales, ops and execs who don't write code?

4. **Where does your company stand today?** Are engineers already using these tools, and which ones? Is there an official policy, or a recent incident, win or debate that's prompting this talk?

5. **Do you have material of your own to use?** Things like a story from your team, a metric (speed, bugs, review time), a near-miss, or a quote. One real example from your company will land better than anything general I could write.

6. **Format and tone:** Are you using slides or just talking? Should it be light and funny, or serious and direct? And is it really 5 minutes, or does that include Q&A?
