Four months into managing, one of my engineers left.

In her exit interview she said she had never once received clear feedback on her work.

I had genuinely thought things were fine.

That was the problem. I'd read "fine" from the absence of complaints. She'd read the absence of feedback as not knowing where she stood. We'd been looking at the same silence and drawing opposite conclusions.

Nobody trained me for this. Most of what prepares you for the job is about the work: planning, reviews, delivery. None of it covers how someone can be quietly unsure of herself for four months while you tell yourself the team is in good shape.

What I'd tell a new manager now:

Silence is not a signal. If you can't remember the last time you told someone, specifically, what they're doing well and what they should change, they can't remember it either.

"Things seem fine" is a sentence about you, not about them. It means nobody has brought you a problem. It doesn't mean nobody has one.

Feedback you haven't given doesn't feel like a decision. It feels like nothing happened. It still counts.

I didn't lose her because of one hard conversation I handled badly. I lost her because of the ones I never noticed I weren't having.
