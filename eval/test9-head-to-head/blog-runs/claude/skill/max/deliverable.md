## The problem with January 1

Every New Year's resolution starts on a date nobody chose.

That sounds like a small thing. It isn't. Think about how real changes begin. Someone quits smoking the week their kid is born. Someone starts running because a doctor said a sentence they couldn't unhear. Someone finally learns to cook because the new flat is too far from any takeaway. In each case the change has a reason, and the reason has a date, and the date is whenever the reason showed up. The change is a response to something.

A resolution reverses this. The date arrives first, on its own, and then you go looking for a change to attach to it. January 1 doesn't know anything about you. It's the same day for everyone, which is exactly why it's useless as a trigger: it can't be about your life in particular, because it's about the Earth completing a lap.

So you pick something. Usually something you've vaguely wanted for a while, which is a polite way of saying something you've had plenty of chances to start and haven't. Nothing about your situation has changed since December 29. The only new thing is the number on the calendar, and the number can't carry any weight once the first week is over. It's not that you lack willpower. It's that you've asked a date to do the job a reason should be doing, and the date clocked off at midnight.

There's a real effect here, to be fair. Psychologists have found that people do feel more motivated right after a landmark like a new year or a birthday. The slate feels clean. But a feeling of freshness is not a reason, and it fades at about the speed you'd expect. The gyms know this. They sell January memberships on the assumption that you'll stop coming, and they're right often enough to build a business on it.

Meanwhile the changes that stick tend to start on a random Tuesday in March, because that's when the thing happened. Nobody writes those down. Nobody announces them. They just start, because something made them necessary, and necessity doesn't check the calendar.

So the question worth asking about a resolution isn't whether it's specific enough or small enough. It's simpler than that: if January 1 didn't exist, would you be doing this anyway? If yes, you don't need the date. If no, the date won't help.
