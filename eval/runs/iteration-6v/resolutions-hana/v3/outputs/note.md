**Angle**: Resolutions fail because we start them in January, and no gardener would plant anything then. Use winter to plan and start in spring.

**Built from**: 4 (a gardening blog for other gardeners, and your view that January is the worst month to start anything because nothing grows), with 1 ("people give up") kept and moved: yes, people give up, but the timing makes them. 2 gave no case and 3 left the ending to me, so I chose a short practical ending (plan in January, start with the first sowing) over a tips list.

**Strongest objection**: Plenty of resolutions have nothing to do with the seasons. Saving money, cutting back on drinking or calling your mother more don't need warm soil, and "wait until spring" can be just a nicer way of putting things off. The post works best if readers take the timing as one real cause, not the only one. **Test**: think back over your own resolutions, or ask a few gardening friends. Did the things you started in spring, alongside the garden, last longer than the ones you started on 1 January? If they didn't, the post is a nice metaphor more than an explanation, and it's worth softening the "the fault isn't in you" line.

**Other directions**:
- A "rotation" post: treat a failed resolution the way you'd treat a failed crop. Move it, change the soil, try again next season, without calling it a failure.
- A straight argument for scrapping New Year's resolutions and having "first sowing day" resolutions instead.
