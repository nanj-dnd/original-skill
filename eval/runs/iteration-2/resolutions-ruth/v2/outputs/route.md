## Before the post

**Angle:** Resolutions fail because they're private promises nobody else is waiting on, and we forgive ourselves too easily. What makes something stick isn't a better system but someone who needs you to turn up. That matters most at our age, when the point is staying connected, not being productive.

**Built from:** 1 (nobody's waiting, we forgive ourselves), 3 (years of failed "walk more", then Joan's hip and two years of weekday walks), 4 (retiree readers, connection over productivity, no gym or *Atomic Habits*), and 2 (one piece of advice at the end, not a list of tips).

**Strongest objection:** "Find an accountability buddy" is already a stock tip in the very listicles you want to avoid, so the post could read as tip number four in a cardigan. **Test:** does the piece survive that rebuttal? It has to keep the difference clear: you didn't recruit Joan to keep *you* on track. She needed help, and the walking came with it. If a reader could finish it thinking "so, get a walking buddy", it's slipped back into technique. A second check for you: does it hold for people who have no Joan nearby? The close points them at finding someone who'd be glad of the company, not at forming a pact.

**Where two answers pull against each other:** you want advice that doesn't read like a technique (2), and the advice really is just "tie it to a person". I've handled that by keeping it to a single line at the end and making the story carry the argument, not the instruction.
