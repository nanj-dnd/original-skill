# Your Resolution Didn't Fail. Your Scorekeeping Did.

Every January the same headline comes around: most New Year's resolutions fail. It gets repeated so often that nobody checks the framing. I teach chemistry, so checking framing is most of my job, and this one is wrong.

Most resolutions don't fail. Most *attempts* fail. Those are different things, and mixing them up is a big reason people quit trying.

I smoked for fifteen years. I quit on my fourth serious attempt, six years ago. By the usual scoring, I failed three times. That's not what happened. Each attempt told me something the one before it hadn't.

The first one showed me my triggers: the first coffee of the morning, driving, and my brother. The second lasted five weeks and fell apart at a wedding. One evening, a few drinks, and five weeks were gone.

That wasn't a character flaw. It was a result. Weddings plus drinking were my strongest trigger, and I had no way of knowing that until I ran into it. So attempt four was planned around exactly that situation. I didn't hope I'd be stronger next time. I knew what was coming and I had a plan for it.

None of this will surprise anyone who has quit smoking. Estimates of how many tries it takes vary a lot, but none of them say one. A 2016 Canadian study put the average for successful quitters at around thirty attempts. If each of those counts as a failure, then nearly everyone who ever quit is a failure. That tells you the scoring is broken, not the people.

In a lab, an experiment that doesn't give you the product you wanted isn't wasted. You find out what went wrong, change the variable, and run it again. Nobody writes "failed" in the notebook and gives up on chemistry.

So here's the method. It's the whole thing.

When an attempt breaks, write down what broke it. Be specific. "I was stressed" is useless. Where were you, what time was it, who were you with, had you been drinking? Then plan the next attempt around that exact situation. Not around willpower, and not around a fresh start date. Around the thing that actually broke the last one.

And count attempts, not failures. Mine took four. Yours might take fourteen. Either way, only the last one has to work.
