## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "AI coding assistants are powerful tools but not replacements. Use them for boilerplate, tests and docs, keep a human in the loop, and review everything they write."

Choices it made:
1. **Thesis:** the safe middle. "Not for everything, but for a lot."
2. **Angle:** a productivity-versus-risk trade-off (faster output vs. security holes, made-up APIs, skills getting rusty).
3. **Examples:** generic ones. A vendor "X% faster" stat, a hallucinated-library anecdote, nothing from this company.
4. **Structure:** stat hook → benefits → risks → guidelines → "use it wisely."
5. **Assumed audience:** a room of engineers, even though an all-hands is the whole company.
6. **Leaves out:** the speaker's own stake, any decision actually on the table, and anything that happened here.
