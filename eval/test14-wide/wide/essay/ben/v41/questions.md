**What everyone gets**

The pasted-prompt thesis: "Social media has been both good and bad for democracy, but on balance its harms (polarization, misinformation, echo chambers) now outweigh its benefits (participation, giving voice to the marginalized), and the answer is better regulation and digital literacy." The choices baked into that:

- **Angle**: a balanced "both, but net negative" verdict that lands on reform.
- **Examples**: Arab Spring as the good; 2016 US election, Cambridge Analytica, Brexit, January 6 as the bad.
- **Structure**: intro → benefits → harms → "it's complicated / what to do" → conclusion.
- **Reader**: a generic grader who wants both sides acknowledged.
- **Tone**: careful, hedged, third-person, no stated position until the last paragraph.

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

**1. Verdict.** The default says "mixed, but net bad, fix it with regulation." Where do you actually land?
a) Net bad, and I'll argue it straight, not hedge
b) Net good, the panic is overstated
c) The question is wrong: it depends on the platform or the country, and the essay should say which
d) Genuinely undecided, so the essay should test the question rather than answer it

**2. Scope.** The default stays at the level of "social media" and "democracy" in general, mostly US examples. What's the frame?
a) One country or election you know well (name it in your reply)
b) One mechanism: e.g. algorithmic ranking, anonymity, or ad-funded attention, and follow that
c) A comparison: two countries or two platforms
d) Keep it general, global

**3. Your material.** The default uses Arab Spring, 2016, and Cambridge Analytica. What do you have that a pasted prompt wouldn't?
a) A specific case you've followed (a movement, a ban, a platform policy, an election): tell me which
b) A study or figure you've read and want to build on
c) Something from your own feed or use: what you've watched happen to a group, a community, or a conversation
d) Nothing specific, pick the least-used strong examples

**4. Reader and tone.** Who is it for?
a) Coursework with a grader: measured, third-person, cites sources
b) Coursework, but the assignment rewards taking a position
c) An opinion piece or blog: first-person allowed, direct
d) Something else (say what)
