**One year as a manager. Here's what actually surprised me.**

I thought the hard part would be the decisions. It wasn't. The hard part was everything around them.

A few things I learned the slow way:

**1. Your output is no longer your output.**
I spent the first few months still trying to be the best individual contributor on the team. That's a trap. My job became creating the conditions for other people to do great work, and that's a completely different skill.

**2. Clarity is kindness.**
Vague feedback feels gentle. It isn't. The most respectful thing I can do for someone is tell them clearly where they stand and what "good" looks like.

**3. Silence in 1:1s is information.**
If someone has nothing to bring up week after week, it usually means I haven't earned the trust for the real conversation yet. Not that everything is fine.

**4. You can't delegate accountability, but you can hoard it by accident.**
Every time I stepped in to "just fix it," I quietly told someone I didn't trust them with it. Letting people own outcomes, including the messy ones, was harder than it sounds.

**5. The calendar tells the truth.**
Whatever I said my priorities were, my calendar revealed what they actually were. Auditing it quarterly was humbling.

**6. Managing up is part of the job, not a distraction from it.**
Protecting the team's focus meant translating context in both directions, and I underestimated how much of my week that would take.

The biggest shift: I stopped measuring my days by what I shipped and started measuring them by what the team could now do that it couldn't before.

Year two goal: do all of this with less white-knuckling.

To the people I managed this year: thank you for your patience while I figured it out in real time.

What did your first year as a manager teach you?
