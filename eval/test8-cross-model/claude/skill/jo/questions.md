**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt yields a "balanced" talk: *AI assistants are a powerful tool, but not for everything. Use them for boilerplate, tests and exploration; stay hands-on for architecture, security and anything you must deeply understand.* It makes these choices:

- **Angle**: "it depends" — a middle path that nobody disagrees with.
- **Examples**: generic ones (autocomplete, test generation, a hallucinated API).
- **Structure**: hook → benefits → risks → guidelines → "use judgment."
- **Reader**: engineers, with a nod to leadership about productivity.
- **Tone**: measured, TED-lite, ends on "the tool doesn't replace the engineer."

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

**1. Position.** The default lands on "it depends." Where do you actually stand?
- a) Yes, use them for nearly everything; the skill now is reviewing and directing
- b) No, "everything" is the mistake; there's a category of work that must stay manual
- c) The question is wrong; it's about *how*, not *whether* (review process, ownership, tests)
- d) Genuinely undecided, and I want to say so and frame the decision for the room

**2. Your real material.** The default uses invented examples. What's one thing that actually happened on your team that makes this question live? (Reply with a sentence or two.) If you have a number — share of PRs with AI-generated code, a bug count, time saved on something, a cost — add it.

**3. Reader and what you want from them.** The default addresses engineers vaguely. Who's really in the room and what should change on Monday?
- a) Mostly engineers; I want a behaviour change (e.g. a review norm, a disclosure habit)
- b) Mixed company; I want non-engineers to understand the tradeoff, not just hear "AI = faster"
- c) Leadership sets expectations; I want to influence a policy or a metric they're watching
- d) Mostly engineers; I want to provoke a debate, not close one

**4. Tone.** It's your name on it, 5 minutes, all-hands.
- a) Plain and direct, a bit blunt; sounds like me talking, not presenting
- b) Measured and professional; I'm speaking partly on behalf of the eng org
- c) Light and self-deprecating; I want them relaxed before the point lands
