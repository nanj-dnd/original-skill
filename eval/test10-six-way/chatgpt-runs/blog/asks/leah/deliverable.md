# Why Most New Year's Resolutions Fail (And Maybe It's Not Your Fault)

Every January, we do the same thing. We convince ourselves that this is the year we'll finally get our lives together. We set goals, buy gym memberships, download habit trackers, and promise ourselves that we'll wake up at 5 or 6 AM every morning.

There's just one problem with that advice: it assumes we all live the same life.

Three years in a row, I made the same New Year's resolution. Get to the gym at 6 AM. Three years in a row, I had given up by February.

For a while, I blamed myself. Maybe I wasn't disciplined enough. Maybe I lacked consistency. Maybe I just didn't want it badly enough.

Then I looked at my schedule.

I'm an ER nurse. I rotate between 12-hour day and night shifts every few weeks. A 6 AM gym session sounds great until you've just finished a night shift, haven't slept properly, and your body has absolutely no interest in cooperating.

And that's when it hit me: maybe the problem wasn't my willpower. Maybe the advice was never designed for someone like me.

Most habit-building advice seems to come from the perspective of someone with a predictable 9-to-5 job. Wake up at the same time. Exercise every morning. Follow a routine. Build consistency.

Great advice, if your schedule allows it.

But what about nurses? Warehouse workers? Parents with newborns? Anyone whose working hours, responsibilities, or sleep patterns change constantly?

We keep treating the inability to follow a fixed routine as a personal failure, when the routine itself might be completely unrealistic.

What finally worked for me was changing one thing: I stopped attaching my gym habit to a time and started attaching it to an event.

The gym became the first thing I do after every shift, whether that's a day shift or a night shift. No 6 AM target. No rigid timetable. Just one consistent trigger.

I've kept that going for 14 months now. Something I couldn't sustain for three consecutive Februaries has become part of my life.

So, if your New Year's resolutions keep falling apart, maybe stop asking yourself why you lack discipline. Start asking whether the system you're trying to follow actually fits your life.

Find an anchor that works with your schedule, not against it. It could be after a shift, after dropping the kids off, or before heading home.

Your routine doesn't have to look like everyone else's to work.

And honestly, that's probably the point.
