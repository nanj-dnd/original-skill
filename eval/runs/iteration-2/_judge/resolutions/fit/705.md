# Output 705

# I'd Never Let Joan Down

For more Januarys than I care to count, I resolved to walk more. And every year, somewhere around the first wet Tuesday, I didn't.

I used to think that was a want of willpower. I don't any more. I think it was kindness. When the only person you've made a promise to is yourself, you're also the judge who hears the case, and most of us are very lenient judges. *It's raining. My knee's a bit off. I'll go tomorrow.* Nobody notices, nobody minds, and we forgive ourselves before the kettle's boiled.

Then my neighbour Joan had her hip done and needed someone to walk with while she got her strength back. That was two years ago. We've walked every weekday at nine o'clock since, rain or cold.

I want to be clear about one thing, because people assume otherwise: Joan has never once checked up on me. There's no chart and no telling-off. She simply stands on her front step at nine, waiting. That turned out to be enough. I let myself down all the time, but I'd never let Joan down.

I've come to think most resolution advice is written for someone else entirely. It's for young people trying to squeeze more out of the day: up earlier, get more done, track it all on the phone. Good luck to them. But most of us here stopped trying to be productive some time ago, and gladly. What we're after now is quieter and matters more. We want to stay connected, to have a reason to put our coat on and somebody who'd notice if we didn't.

So no wonder "walk more" never took. It was a fine goal aimed at the wrong thing. On my own, walking was a chore I was meant to do. With Joan, it's the best part of the morning: the weather, the neighbours, whatever's in the paper.

If I've any advice, it isn't to try harder. Tie the thing you want to a person. And if there's no Joan next door, I'd wager there's someone on your street who'd be glad of the company and is too polite to ask.

I'll own up to one last thing. I only started going for Joan's sake. These days she's quicker than me, which I find a bit cheeky, and I'm fairly sure she gets me out of the house as much as I get her. Neither of us is keeping a resolution any more. We're just waiting for each other at nine.

# Ruth (task: resolutions)
67, retired accountant, widowed, writes a blog mostly read by other retirees in her town.

What she thinks: resolutions fail because they're about improving yourself on your own, and nobody is waiting for you to show up. The ones that worked in her life were the ones where someone else was counting on her.

Real case: she resolved to walk more for years and never did. Then her neighbour Joan, recently out of hip surgery, needed someone to walk with, and they've walked every weekday at 9 for two years. "I'd never let Joan down. I let myself down all the time."

Also: she thinks a lot of resolution writing is aimed at young people trying to be productive, and retirees are trying to stay connected, not productive.

How she answers: longer, with small anecdotes; polite; sometimes goes off on a tangent.
