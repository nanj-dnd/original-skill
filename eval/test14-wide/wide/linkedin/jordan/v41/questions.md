**What everyone gets**

Main claim: "The biggest thing I learned is that management isn't about having the answers — it's about enabling your team."

- Angle: humble reflection, "I was wrong about what the job was"
- Examples: letting go of doing the work yourself, 1:1s, learning to delegate, giving feedback earlier
- Structure: one-line hook, 5 numbered lessons, closing question ("What did your first year teach you?")
- Reader: other new or aspiring managers, generic LinkedIn audience
- Tone: reflective, polished-vulnerable, short lines with white space

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`:

**1. The default's claim is "the job shifted from doing the work to enabling others." Is that your year?**
a) Yes, that's the main thing
b) No — the hard part was feedback and difficult conversations
c) No — the hard part was managing upward (my own boss, priorities, saying no)
d) Something else (one line)

**2. One real moment to build it on — pick a type and add a line about it:**
a) A specific conversation with a report (what was said, roughly)
b) A number: team size, something that changed, how long something took
c) A mistake you made and what it cost
d) None — write it without personal details

**3. The default is 5 numbered lessons. Shape?**
a) Keep the list, but 3 lessons not 5
b) One lesson, told through the moment from Q2
c) Short — under 120 words, one point
