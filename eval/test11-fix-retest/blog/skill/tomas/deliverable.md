**Attempt four**

I smoked for fifteen years. I've been clean for six. In between there were four serious attempts to quit, and if you'd asked me after the third one, I'd have told you I failed three times.

I don't say that anymore. Here's why.

The first attempt showed me the first coffee of the morning. I hadn't noticed it was a cigarette with a coffee attached rather than the other way round. The second attempt lasted five weeks and died at a wedding. Drinks, a crowd, somebody holding a pack out — five weeks gone in about a minute. The third showed me driving, and my brother. Different trigger each time, and each one I only found by walking into it.

By the fourth attempt I had a list. The big one was weddings plus drinking, so the fourth attempt was planned around exactly that. It's the one that held.

Now, every January, people make a resolution, and by spring most of them are back where they started. The usual explanation is that they lacked discipline or picked the wrong goal or should have started smaller. There are entire books about it. I don't think any of that is the problem.

The problem is the scoring. We count outcomes. One resolution, one year, pass or fail. By that count, I failed to quit smoking in three separate years and succeeded in one, which is a 25 percent hit rate and sounds like bad luck.

Count attempts instead and it's a different story. Four attempts. Three of them produced information I couldn't have got any other way. I didn't know weddings were the problem until a wedding ended attempt two. Nobody could have told me that in advance, and if they had I wouldn't have believed it. The only way to find out what trips you up is to try, trip, and look at what your foot caught on.

Most attempts fail. That's not the sad part; that's the mechanism. The sad part is treating each one as a verdict and starting the next one from scratch, with no notes, as if January wiped the board.

So if you're making a resolution: fine. Just don't ask whether it worked. Ask what it showed you, write that down, and bring it to the next attempt. The fourth one is where you'll have enough to go on.
