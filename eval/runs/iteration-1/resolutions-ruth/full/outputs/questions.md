**Fixed parts:** about 400 words, a personal-blog register, the topic (why resolutions fail). Any statistic I use gets checked. For example, the "80% fail by February" line goes round everywhere but has no solid source. Everything else is open.

## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** Resolutions fail because they're vague, too big and run on willpower. Set small, specific, trackable goals and build systems and habits instead.

- **Thesis:** the fault is in the *method* (bad goal-setting), so better technique fixes it.
- **Angle:** a friendly self-help explainer that ends upbeat with "here's how to make this year different."
- **Examples:** the gym that's packed in January and empty by February, "lose weight" vs. "walk 20 minutes a day," a nod to *Atomic Habits* or SMART goals, and the "80% fail by February" stat.
- **Structure:** a hook stat, then 3 to 4 reasons, then 3 to 4 tips, then an encouraging close.
- **Assumed reader:** someone who wants to keep their resolution and needs a better method.
- **Leaves out:** the writer's own experience, whether failing is actually bad, and whether January 1 is the problem at all.

## A few questions

Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4: <own words>`. Every option can be swapped for your own words.

**1. The default says resolutions fail because of bad *method*: too vague, too big, too reliant on willpower. Where do you put the fault?**
- a) Method. That's basically right.
- b) It isn't a failure of method at all. A lot of resolutions are things people don't actually want; they're what they think they *should* want.
- c) The ritual itself: picking an arbitrary date and making a public vow sets people up to quit at the first slip.
- d) Your own words

**2. The default treats failing as the problem to fix and ends with tips. What should the post do with failure?**
- a) Fix it: practical advice for making resolutions stick.
- b) Defend it: dropping a resolution is often the right call, or tells you something useful.
- c) Neither: just explain why it happens, with no advice at the end.
- d) Your own words

**3. The default writes from nowhere in particular, with a general "you" and a gym example. Is there a real case of your own behind this?**
- a) Yes, a resolution I failed at (tell me which one and roughly what happened)
- b) Yes, one I actually kept, and it was different from the ones I dropped (what made it different?)
- c) Someone else's: a friend, partner or family member (what happened?)
- d) No personal story; keep it general

**4. The default assumes the reader is a fellow resolution-maker who wants to succeed. Who reads your blog, and how do you sound to them?**
- a) Friends and family. Casual, a bit confessional.
- b) People who come for a particular subject (which one?), so I'd like it tied back to that.

- d) Your own words
