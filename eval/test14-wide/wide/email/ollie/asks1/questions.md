Happy to — a few questions so the email actually lands:

1. **Your company and product** — What's the product called, and what's the one thing it does better than alternatives (e.g. faster routing, handles time windows, multi-depot, real-time re-routing, easy integration with existing TMS)?

2. **Proof** — Do you have any concrete results to cite? A customer name, a % reduction in miles/fuel/driver hours, or a pilot timeline? Even one number makes a big difference.

3. **The recipient** — Do you have a specific name and company, or should I write it with placeholders? Any trigger for reaching out now (recent expansion, hiring drivers, fuel-cost news, mutual connection)?

4. **Their operation** — What kind of logistics: last-mile delivery, LTL/FTL trucking, courier, field service? Roughly how many vehicles/routes a day? This shapes which pain point I lead with.

5. **The ask** — What do you want them to do: book a 15-minute call, reply to a question, agree to a free route audit on a sample of their data?

6. **Tone and sender** — Who's it from (founder, sales rep, title)? Casual and punchy, or more formal? Any length limit (e.g. under 100 words)?

7. **Anything to avoid** — Phrases or claims you don't want in there, or competitors not to mention?
