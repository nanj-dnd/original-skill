**What everyone gets**

The main claim a pasted prompt produces: "Your trucks are driving more miles than they need to; our software cuts mileage and fuel by 10–20%, and most customers see ROI in months." It makes these choices:

- **Angle**: fuel and mileage savings, the pain every logistics prospect is assumed to share.
- **Evidence**: a round, unattributed percentage ("typically 15% fewer miles") and a vague "companies like X".
- **Structure**: noticed-you hook → their pain → our product → social proof → "do you have 15 minutes next week?"
- **Reader**: a stranger who hasn't heard the pitch, so it explains what route optimisation is.
- **Tone**: breezy-professional, first name, one emoji-free exclamation of enthusiasm.

Four questions so I can write something that isn't that. Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`, adding any specifics in a line after.

**1. Lead problem.** The default leads with fuel/mileage savings. Which is the real pain for this person?
a) Fuel and total miles
b) Missed delivery windows / customer complaints about timing
c) Dispatchers spending hours a day building routes by hand
d) Driver overtime and churn from long, uneven days

**2. Your real material.** The default uses a made-up "15% fewer miles". What can I actually put in?
a) A named customer and a number (fleet size, % or hours saved, timeframe) — give it to me in a line
b) A pilot or internal figure, even rough ("one 40-truck fleet, 11% fewer miles in 6 weeks")
c) Something specific you know about *this* prospect (fleet size, a recent expansion, a new depot, a bad review about late deliveries)
d) Nothing yet — write around it, no invented stats

**3. The ask.** The default ends with "15 minutes next week?" What do you actually want from the reply?
a) A short call
b) Permission to run their routes through the tool on one week of their data and send back the result
c) Just a one-line reply to a specific question
d) An intro to whoever owns dispatch/planning

**4. How it sounds and who signs.** 
a) Plain and short, from you by name, no company boilerplate
b) Slightly more formal, from you with title and company
c) Founder-casual, like a peer who's run a fleet or dispatch desk (only if that's true — tell me)

Also tell me the product name and your company, and the prospect's company if you want it named.
