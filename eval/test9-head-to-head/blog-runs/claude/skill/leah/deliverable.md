**Why your resolution dies in February (it's not you, it's the template)**

Every piece of advice about keeping a resolution says the same thing. Pick a time. Same time every day. Build a morning routine. Protect it.

I believed that for three years. Three Januaries in a row I set a 6am gym habit, and three Februaries in a row it died, because that's when my rotation flipped to nights. "Same time every day" is a lovely idea if your days are the same. Mine aren't. Neither are yours if you're a nurse, a tech, a porter, anyone whose week is written by a rostering system rather than a Google Calendar.

Here's what I eventually worked out. My goal wasn't badly designed. I wasn't lazy or unmotivated or any of the other things the February articles suggest. The advice was written by and for people who get up, go to work, come home, and sleep, in that order, at roughly the same hours, forever. Once you see that, every "morning routine" post reads like instructions for a kitchen you don't own. When does a morning routine happen if your morning is 7pm?

So I stopped tying the habit to a clock and tied it to an event instead. The new rule: gym is the first thing after any shift ends. Day shift, night shift, long day, doesn't matter. Badge out, go. I've kept that one for fourteen months, which is roughly thirteen months longer than the previous three attempts combined.

Why does it work? Because the shift ending is the one fixed point in a week that has none. The clock moves. Handover doesn't. You already have a thing you do without thinking the moment you finish, whether that's coffee or sitting in the car staring at nothing for ten minutes. You're not building a habit from scratch; you're bolting it onto something that already fires.

It doesn't have to be the gym. Pick whatever you wanted to do this year and ask: what's the event, not the hour? After handover. Before I eat on break. The moment I take my shoes off at home. If the answer is still "at 6am," you'll be back here next February, and I'll be glad of the company, but I'd rather you weren't.

One suggestion, then. Anchor to an event, not a clock. The clock was never yours anyway.
