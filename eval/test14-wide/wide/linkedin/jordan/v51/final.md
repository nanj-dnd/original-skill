One year as a manager. The hardest part was the people side, and nothing had trained me for it.

Three situations stood out.

Performance conversations. When someone's work isn't where it needs to be, it's easy to soften the message until it stops being feedback. Vague can feel kind in the room and still leave the other person guessing for weeks. Plain words, said early, are usually the kinder option.

Someone on the team struggling. It rarely gets announced. It can look like slower replies or a task that quietly stalls. It helps to ask sooner than feels polite, and not to try to fix everything in the same conversation. Often people mainly need to know someone noticed.

Conflict. Staying out of it can feel neutral. Usually it just delays things, and the people around it pay for the delay. A small disagreement is far easier to talk through than a big one.

If you're about to manage people for the first time, think early about how you'll handle the conversation you're dreading.
