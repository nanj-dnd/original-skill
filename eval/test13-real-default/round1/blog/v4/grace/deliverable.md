**Why most New Year's resolutions fail**

I've got a 700-day streak on a Welsh app. My daughter, who is 12, is learning it too, and at dinner we check each other's streaks. It's become one of those small rituals a family picks up without deciding to. Somebody asks "did you do your Welsh?" and if the answer is no, you get up from the table and do it.

I've been thinking about why that one has held, because most things like it don't. The usual explanation for failed resolutions is that people aren't disciplined enough, or they pick the wrong goal, or they aim too high. I don't think it's any of those. I think it's simpler. People lose motivation. Everyone does. The feeling you had on January 1st isn't a character trait, it's a mood, and moods go. The question is what's left when it does.

For most resolutions, the honest answer is nothing. The goal was carried entirely by wanting it, and once the wanting fades, there's no reason to keep going that isn't "I said I would." That's a thin reason on a Tuesday night when you're tired. Nobody is watching, nobody will notice, and skipping costs you nothing you can feel.

What's different about the Welsh isn't that I want it more than other things I've wanted. It's that the habit stopped being mine alone. There's a person across the table who will ask. There's a number that goes back to zero if I don't. And there's a fixed point in the day, dinner, where it comes up whether I'm in the mood or not. None of that requires motivation. On the days I have some, fine. On the days I don't, the structure does the job instead.

I'd put it this way: a resolution is a promise to yourself, and promises to yourself are the easiest kind to break, because you're the only one who has to agree to let it go. The habits that stick are the ones you've handed partly to someone else, or to a routine that runs without you having to feel anything about it.

So if you're making one this year, I wouldn't spend much time on the goal. Spend it on what will keep the goal alive in March, when you've gone off it. Who's going to ask you about it? When, in the day, does it come up on its own? If you can't answer those, you're relying on a feeling to last, and feelings don't.
