**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because people set vague, oversized goals without a system, so motivation fades by February.

Choices a pasted prompt makes:
- Angle: it's a goal-setting problem (specificity, habits, "systems over goals")
- Examples: the gym in January, "read more", "lose weight"
- Structure: hook about January crowds → 3 reasons → tidy advice to pick one small habit
- Reader: a generic self-improvement audience
- Tone: upbeat, mildly confessional, ends on a reassuring note

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`)

1. Why do you actually think they fail? The default says "bad goal-setting."
   a. Agree, it's mostly about how the goal is set
   b. The date is arbitrary; January is the wrong time to change anything
   c. People resolve to fix things they don't really want to change (social pressure, not desire)
   d. They don't fail — "failing" is normal practice and the framing is wrong

2. What's your real material? The default uses a made-up gym example.
   a. A resolution of yours that failed (tell me what it was and roughly how long it lasted)
   b. One that worked, and what was different
   c. Something you've watched a friend/family member do
   d. No personal example; keep it general

3. How should it end? The default ends on tidy advice.
   a. Practical: one thing to do instead
   b. A shrug: observation, no advice
   c. A question back to the reader
   d. Something provocative: don't make resolutions at all

4. How should it sound on your blog?
   a. Plain and conversational, first person, like talking to a friend
   b. Dry and a bit sardonic
   c. Reflective, slower, more essay-like
   d. In my own voice (I'll use your voice skill)

If you have a number worth using (how many years you've tried, how long your last one lasted, a stat you trust), drop it in with your answers.
