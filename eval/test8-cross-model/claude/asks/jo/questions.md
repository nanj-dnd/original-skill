Happy to help. A few questions so this lands well in 5 minutes:

1. **What's your actual position?** "For everything" is a strong framing — are you arguing yes, no, "it depends," or deliberately presenting both sides and leaving it open? And is this your personal view, a team/leadership stance, or a proposal for a policy?

2. **Who's in the room and what's their current mood?** Is this engineering-only or the whole company (sales, support, exec)? Are people already heavy users, skeptical, divided, or burned by a specific incident? Any existing company policy or debate I should be aware of?

3. **What do you want to happen after you sit down?** A decision, a pilot, a norm (e.g., "always review AI-generated code"), a discussion, or just awareness?

4. **Any concrete stories or data you can use?** A win (shipped faster, caught a bug), a failure (bad PR merged, security issue, licensing concern), or metrics like adoption %, PR volume, incident counts. Specifics beat generalities in a short talk.

5. **Which tools and context?** Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, something internal? And what kind of codebase — regulated/security-sensitive, greenfield startup, legacy monolith? That changes which risks and benefits matter.

6. **Tone and format:** Slides or just talking? Conversational and a bit provocative, or measured and diplomatic? Any constraints — e.g., don't name specific vendors, avoid sounding like you're mandating anything?

7. **Where do you personally stand on the nuances** — e.g., "use AI for boilerplate/tests but not architecture," "fine as long as you can explain every line," "junior engineers should limit use to build skills"? Any of those resonate, or any you specifically disagree with?

Answer whatever's relevant (even briefly) and I'll draft the outline and opening paragraph.
