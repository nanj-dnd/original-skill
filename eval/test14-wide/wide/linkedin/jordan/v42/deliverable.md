Every piece of management advice says the job is enabling other people to do the work.

Correct. What it leaves out is that enabling, up close, looks a lot like standing around not doing things, which is a hard skill to be proud of.

A year in, three findings:

The fastest way to get something done is still to do it yourself. That is exactly why you shouldn't. Each time a manager quietly fixes the thing, the team learns that the thing gets fixed. Nothing else. It's speed this quarter, billed next quarter, with interest.

Having the answer is a trap. Say it and the room writes it down. Ask "what do you think" and wait, and the room eventually produces a better one, slowly, while you resist the urge to help. The waiting is the job. Nobody puts "waited" on a slide.

The output doesn't fit on a CV. The team shipped it. You arranged the furniture. Making peace with that is, reportedly, most of the first year.

So: management is enabling others. Everyone says it. Everyone is right. It turns out saying it and doing it are two different jobs, and only one of them comes with a title.
