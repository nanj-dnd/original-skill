A year ago I went from doing marketing to managing three people who do marketing. I assumed the hard part would be the work itself: the campaigns, the deadlines, the numbers. It wasn't.

The hard part was the people side, and I underestimated it almost completely.

Three things I had to unlearn:

**Doing it myself.** As an IC, speed meant picking up the task. As a manager, that same instinct quietly tells your team you don't trust them. Handing something off and letting it be done differently than I would have done it was harder than any brief I've written.

**Assuming people could read my mind.** Things that felt obvious to me (priorities, what "good" looked like, why a decision was made) often weren't. Most of the friction I ran into traced back to something I hadn't actually said out loud.

**Waiting to give feedback.** I used to hold onto it until the "right moment." There is no right moment. Small, early, and honest beats polished and late every time.

I'm still figuring most of this out. But if you're about to step into your first management role: the work will take care of itself. Spend your energy on the people.
