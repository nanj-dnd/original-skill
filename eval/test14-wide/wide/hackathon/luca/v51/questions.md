**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt returns something like: *a personal carbon-footprint tracker / "climate copilot" — an app that reads receipts, bank transactions or travel and shows your emissions, suggests greener swaps, maybe gamifies it.* The choices it quietly made:

- **Angle**: individual behaviour change (you, the consumer) rather than a business, grid, or local-institution problem.
- **Examples**: food, flights, commuting; a dashboard with a CO₂ number.
- **Structure**: idea → mobile app + LLM → "demo: scan a receipt, see your score".
- **Reader**: generic judges; nobody's actual data.
- **Tone**: polished pitch-deck language ("empower users", "actionable insights").

Four questions so the idea is yours, not the model's. Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`.

**1. Who has the problem?** (default: an individual consumer)
- a) A consumer / household
- b) A small business or building operator (restaurant, school, office, landlord)
- c) A city, utility, or grid — public data at scale
- d) A specific community you know (farmers, cyclists, your campus, renters…)

**2. What should the demo prove?** (default: a nice dashboard with a CO₂ number)
- a) Insight: show a judge something true they didn't know
- b) Action: something happens — a device, a booking, a notification, a purchase changes
- c) Reach: a crowd/map/network effect that's convincing even with few users
- d) Honesty: the tool exposes greenwashing or bad data rather than producing a score

**3. Your real material — pick what you actually have, the demo will be built on it.**
- a) A dataset or API you already know is usable (name it, even roughly)
- b) A place or thing you can measure live this weekend (a building, a fridge, a bike route, a bill)
- c) A frustration one of you personally has with climate stuff (say what it is in a few words)
- d) Nothing specific — we'll have to find something

**4. Team shape and judging constraints**
- a) 2–3 people, mostly software; judges care about demo polish
- b) Includes hardware/sensor people; judges like physical demos
- c) Mixed with design/non-technical people; judges reward storytelling and clarity
- d) Other — say what, plus any rules (sponsor API required, must deploy, etc.)
