**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because they're vague, too ambitious, and driven by a calendar date rather than real motivation, so the fix is small specific goals, systems over outcomes, and habits that survive a bad week.

Choices a pasted prompt makes:
- Angle: the resolver is at fault (bad goal-setting), not the ritual itself
- Examples: gym memberships in January, "lose weight" vs "walk 20 minutes"
- Structure: hook with the "80% fail by February" stat → 3-4 reasons as subheads → tidy "what to do instead" close
- Reader: a general self-improvement audience
- Tone: friendly coach, slightly motivational

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`)

1. Who's to blame? The default blames the person's goal-setting.
   a. The person (vague goals, too many at once)
   b. The ritual itself (January 1st is an arbitrary date, resolutions are a performance)
   c. Neither, it's the environment (your life in February is the same life that produced the habit)
   d. Resolutions don't really "fail", they just work differently than people expect

2. Your real material. Since this is your own blog, one real moment: a resolution you made and what happened to it, or something someone said to you about theirs.
   a. I'll give one (type it)
   b. Use something general, no personal story
   c. Build it around a specific resolution I'll name but keep the details light

3. Ending. The default closes with a "do this instead" fix.
   a. Keep a practical fix at the end
   b. No fix, just the diagnosis, let the reader sit with it
   c. End on a question or a turn, not advice

4. Tone for your readers.
   a. Plain and conversational, like talking to a friend
   b. Dry and a bit wry
   c. Reflective, more essay than post
