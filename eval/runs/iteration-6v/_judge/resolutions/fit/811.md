# Most Resolutions Fail Because We're Lazy. Plan for It.

Every January, people make the same mistake. They write a resolution for the person they'd like to be, then hand it to the person they actually are.

The person they'd like to be gets up at six, cooks on Sundays and reads before bed. The person they actually are hits snooze, orders in on Wednesday and scrolls until midnight. By February the resolution has quietly gone, and the usual explanation is that it was too vague or too ambitious.

Here's a blunter one: most of us are lazy. Not shamefully so. Laziness is just our normal setting: doing whatever takes the least effort right now. Eating what's within reach. Watching the next episode because it starts on its own. That setting doesn't change on January 1.

Most resolutions are plans to stop being lazy, and that's why they fail. They ask you to beat your own default every single day, with nothing on your side but resolve, and resolve is the first thing to go when you're tired.

So stop fighting the laziness and put it to work. Make the thing you want to do the easiest thing to do, and the thing you want to stop the hardest.

**Do the hard part once.** Spend the effort at the start, while you're keen, so that later you only have to follow a path you've already laid. Put the running shoes by the door. Buy the vegetables already chopped. Set the savings transfer to leave your account automatically on payday, so saving needs no decision at all.

**Add friction to what you want less of.** Log out of the app so getting back in takes three steps. Charge your phone in another room. Don't keep the snacks in the house. A lazy person won't go to the shop at ten at night for them, and that's the point.

**Shrink the daily ask until it's hard to refuse.** Not "write every day" but "open the document." Not "an hour at the gym" but "put on the kit." Once you've started, stopping often feels like more effort than carrying on.

**Pick one.** Five resolutions means five daily fights. One means one, and you might win it.

None of this takes more willpower. It takes about an hour in early January, rearranging your surroundings so your laziness pushes you the right way instead of the wrong one.

You won't become a different person this year, and you don't need to. Set things up so the person you already are, tired and distracted and looking for the easy option, ends up doing the right thing anyway.
