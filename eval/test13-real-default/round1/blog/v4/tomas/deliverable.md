# Count the attempts

I quit smoking on my fourth serious attempt. I'd smoked for fifteen years. I'm six years clean now.

If you only look at the last attempt, that's a success story. If you look at the first three, it's three failures. Both readings are wrong, and the second one is the one that does the damage every January.

"Most New Year's resolutions fail" is true, but it's measuring the wrong unit. It treats one attempt as the whole thing. Most attempts at anything hard fail. Ask anyone who has quit smoking for good how many times they quit before the one that stuck. The answer is almost never once.

So the question isn't why your resolution failed. It's what the attempt told you.

Attempt two lasted five weeks and died at a wedding. I could have filed that as "five weeks, failed," and for a while I did. What it actually said was: drinking at a wedding will take you out. Not drinking in general. Not weddings in general. The two together. Specific, repeatable, worth knowing.

Attempt four was planned around exactly that. Not around willpower, not around a date on a calendar. Around the thing I'd already watched break.

The first three attempts, between them, gave me the full list: first coffee of the morning, driving, and my brother. You don't find those by sitting and thinking about it. You find them by failing in front of them. Each attempt broke in a particular place, and the place was the information.

That's the reframe. Count attempts, not outcomes. A failed attempt isn't a mark against you, and it isn't a "lesson" in the vague sense either. It's a location. Where exactly did it go? What time of day, who was there, what was in your hand? Write that down and you have the plan for the next one.

This is not an argument that failure is secretly fine. It isn't. Three failed quits is three times I went back to something I'd said I was done with. But "failed" is only the verdict if you stop counting. If you're on attempt three, you're not someone who failed twice. You're someone two attempts in, holding two pieces of data most people never bother to collect.

The resolution frame wants you to grade the year. Don't. Note where it broke, and go again.
