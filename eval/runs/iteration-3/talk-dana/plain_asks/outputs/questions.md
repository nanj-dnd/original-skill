Happy to write this. A few questions first so the outline argues your point and fits the room, rather than being a generic pros-and-cons list:

1. **What's your answer?** Do you think engineers should use AI assistants for everything, for most things but with limits, or less than they do now? Or do you want to lay out the tradeoffs and leave the room to decide?

2. **What should happen after the talk?** For example: a policy change, a pilot, leadership backing for something, engineers changing habits, or just a conversation getting started.

3. **Who's in the room, and what's your role?** Is it mostly engineers, or a mix with sales, ops, leadership and others who don't code? Are you speaking as an engineer, an eng manager, an exec, or someone outside engineering?

4. **Where does the company stand today?** Are people already using these tools, and is that official or informal? Is there a policy? Has anything happened, good or bad, that people will be thinking about, like a bug or security scare traced to AI-written code, a big productivity win, or arguments about code review?

5. **Do you have stories or numbers I should use?** Your own experience, a team's results, or internal data make a much stronger talk than general claims.

6. **What tone, and what format?** Light and conversational, or serious and persuasive? Will you have slides, and is there Q&A inside the 5 minutes or after?

If you only have time for a couple of these, answer 1 and 3. They change the most.
