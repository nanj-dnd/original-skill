Here are the questions that would most change what I suggest:

**Team & skills**
1. How many people are on the team, and what are their strengths (frontend, backend, ML/data, hardware, design, pitching)?
2. Any languages, frameworks, or tools you're fastest in or want to use?

**Constraints & format**
3. How long is the hackathon, really? (e.g., 24h, 36h, 48h) And how much of that will you actually spend building?
4. Is hardware allowed or available (Raspberry Pi, sensors, Arduino), or is this software-only?
5. How long is the demo slot, and is it live, recorded, or slides plus a video?

**Judging & goals**
6. What are the judging criteria (technical depth, impact, business viability, design, use of sponsor tech)?
7. Are there sponsor prizes or required APIs/platforms (e.g., a specific cloud, LLM provider, or climate dataset)?
8. What's your goal: win the main prize, target a specific track, build something you'd keep working on, or learn a technology?

**Direction**
9. Any climate sub-areas you're drawn to or want to avoid? (e.g., personal carbon footprint, energy/grid, food waste, transport, climate adaptation/disasters, agriculture, supply chain emissions, policy/ESG)
10. Who do you picture as the user: individuals, small businesses, cities/governments, farmers, or enterprises?
11. Do you have access to any interesting data, domain expertise, or real users/partners (e.g., someone who works in energy, a campus sustainability office)?

**Taste**
12. Do you want something safe and polished, or a riskier, more ambitious idea that could really stand out?
13. Any ideas you've already considered or rejected, so I don't repeat them?
