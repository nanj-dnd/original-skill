1: None of those quite fit, and honestly the framing is the problem. I'm not worried about code quality. The code it writes is mostly fine, and I'm happy for people to use it for new code, including non-boilerplate. What worries me is skill atrophy, specifically in debugging. People are losing the ability to reason through a failure in code they nominally wrote. My position is that juniors should spend their first six months debugging without an assistant. Think of how pilots still train on stick-and-rudder flying even though autopilot does most of the work. You learn the manual skill first so you can take over when the automation hands you a mess at 2am. Please don't frame this as "pull back on AI." I'm not anti-AI, and if it comes across as a luddite rant, I lose the room.

2a: Over the last six months, our median time-to-resolve for P2 incidents went from 38 minutes to 61.

2b: We had an outage at 2am recently. The on-call engineer pasted the error into the assistant four times and got four different confident fixes. None of them were right. When I got on, it took me about ten minutes to find it just by reading the log. I'm not telling that to dunk on the engineer. It's the pattern I keep seeing in incident reviews: people who can't follow a stack trace through their own code.
