One year ago I went from senior engineer to managing a team of six in our hospital's IT department. Two things I wish someone had told me:

**Being liked is not the same as being clear.** I avoided hard conversations because I didn't want to be "that" manager. Four months in, someone on my team moved on, and in her exit conversation she said she'd never gotten clear feedback from me. She was right. I thought I was being kind. I was being vague, and vague isn't kind.

**Stop coding everything yourself.** For months I kept picking up the hardest tickets because I was fastest. That wasn't helping the team. It was robbing them of the work they needed to grow, and me of the time to actually manage.

If you're an engineer thinking about management: the job isn't being the best engineer in the room anymore. It's making sure everyone else can do their best work. I'm still learning that.
