**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because people set vague, oversized goals and rely on willpower instead of systems.

- Angle: the "set smaller goals, build habits" self-help line.
- Examples: gym memberships in January, "read more," "lose weight."
- Structure: hook → 3–4 reasons (vague, too big, willpower, no plan) → tidy fix at the end.
- Reader: a general audience that already half-knows this.
- Tone: upbeat, mildly instructional, second person.

Four questions, answer like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

**1. The claim.** Default says resolutions fail from vague goals and weak systems. Which is closer to what you actually think?
- a) That's right — it's a planning problem
- b) They fail because January is an arbitrary date; the decision was never real, just calendar pressure
- c) They mostly fail because the goal is someone else's idea of improvement, not yours
- d) They don't really "fail" — the framing is wrong, and trying and lapsing is fine

**2. Your material.** Default uses generic gym/reading examples. What's one resolution you made, and roughly how long it lasted (or what happened)? Options give the shape; put your actual case in the reply:

- b) One I dropped — I'll say which and when it died
- c) I don't make them, and that's part of my view
- d) No personal case; keep it general

**3. Structure.** Default is hook → list of reasons → fix.
- a) Fine, list of reasons
- b) One argument built out, no list
- c) Start from the personal case and widen from there
- d) Short, punchy, mostly observations, no fix offered

**4. Tone.** Your blog, your name on it.
