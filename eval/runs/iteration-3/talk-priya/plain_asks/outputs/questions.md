Happy to help with this. A few questions first so the outline fits your talk and doesn't read like a generic pros-and-cons list:

1. **What's your position?** Are you arguing that engineers *should* use AI assistants for everything, that they *shouldn't*, or for something in between (for example "yes for X, never for Y")? Or do you want to lay out both sides and let the room decide?

2. **Who's talking, and who's listening?** What's your role (IC engineer, eng manager, CTO, someone outside engineering)? Is the all-hands mostly engineers, or the whole company, including people like sales, ops and leadership who don't write code?

3. **Why is this coming up now?** Is there a decision coming (a tool rollout, a usage policy, budget or licences), something that recently went well or badly, or a debate already going on internally? And what should people think or do once your 5 minutes are up?

4. **What's actually going on at your company?** Are engineers already using these tools, and which ones? Do you have stories, numbers or concerns I can use, such as speed gains, bugs or incidents, security or IP worries, code review load, or how junior engineers are learning? Real specifics will make the talk land far better than general claims.

5. **Format and tone:** Will you use slides, or just speak? Should it be light and a bit funny, or more serious and persuasive? Is there anything you want to stay away from (naming vendors, sounding like you're criticizing a team, and so on)?
