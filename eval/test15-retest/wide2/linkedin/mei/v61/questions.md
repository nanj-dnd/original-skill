**What everyone gets**
Claim: "Managing isn't about being the best individual contributor. It's about making your team successful."
- Angle: the IC-to-manager identity shift ("my job is no longer to do the work")
- Examples: a numbered list of 5 to 7 lessons (listen more, delegate, 1:1s matter, feedback is a gift, it's okay not to have all the answers)
- Structure: hook line, short one-sentence paragraphs, list, a "What did you learn?" question at the end
- Reader: other new or aspiring managers
- Tone: humble, grateful, a thank-you to the team, a few hashtags

**A few questions first** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4`):

**1. Which lesson should carry the post?** The default spreads across 5 to 7 tidy lessons.
a) One lesson, told in depth through one story
b) Something I got wrong and had to undo
c) A lesson that contradicts common management advice (tell me which)
d) Keep the list of lessons, but make each one specific to me

**2. Give me one real moment from this year.** The default uses none, only general truths. Any of:
a) Something that happened: a conversation, a mistake, a decision you'd make differently
b) A number: team size, people hired or lost, how your calendar changed, a result
c) A line someone said to you (a report, your boss, a peer)
d) Write it in your own words: one or two sentences is enough

**3. How should it sound?** The default is humble and grateful with an end question and hashtags.
a) Plain and direct, like you'd say it to a friend, no hashtags
b) Warm and reflective, with a thank-you to the team
c) Crisp and professional, written for leaders and recruiters reading your profile
d) Light and self-deprecating

**4. Anything fixed?** For example: length (short, about 150 words, or long, about 300), whether to name your company or team, people to tag or thank, things to avoid saying (confidential details, criticism of anyone), and your role or field if it should appear.
