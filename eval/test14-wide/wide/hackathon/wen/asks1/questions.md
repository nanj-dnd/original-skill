Happy to — a few quick questions so the idea actually fits your team and the event:

1. **Team & skills** — How many people, and what's the skill mix (frontend, backend, ML/data, hardware, design)? Any tech stack you'd prefer or want to avoid?

2. **Time & format** — Is it a full 48 hours or something shorter? In-person or remote? Will you have hardware access (sensors, Raspberry Pi, etc.), or is this software-only?

3. **Judging criteria** — Do you know what the judges weight most (technical depth, impact, polish, business viability, use of a sponsor's API)? Are there sponsor tools or datasets you'd get bonus points for using?

4. **Climate angle** — Any sub-area you're drawn to (consumer behavior, energy, agriculture, carbon markets, disaster resilience, supply chain, policy)? Or anything you want to steer *away* from because it's overdone (e.g., another carbon footprint calculator)?

5. **Audience for the demo** — Who are the judges — engineers, VCs, climate domain experts, general public? That shapes whether the demo should be live-and-interactive vs. a tight scripted walkthrough.

6. **Ambition level** — Are you optimizing to win, to learn something new, or to have a fun shippable thing by Sunday? And is there any appetite for a hardware/physical demo, or should it be purely on-screen?

7. **Data access** — Are you comfortable relying on public APIs/datasets (e.g., grid carbon intensity, satellite imagery, weather, EPA data), or do you want something that works fully offline in case the venue wifi is bad?

Answer whatever you can — even partial answers are fine, and I'll pick the idea accordingly.
