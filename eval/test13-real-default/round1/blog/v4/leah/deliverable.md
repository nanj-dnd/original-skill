**The resolution advice was never written for you**

Every piece of advice about keeping a resolution assumes you have a morning. Not a morning as in the thing that happens after you sleep. A morning as in 6am, same wall clock, Monday to Friday, followed by a commute and a desk. "Same time every day." "Build a morning routine." "Do it before work so nothing gets in the way."

If you work rotating 12s, or a warehouse floor, or you're up with a newborn, none of that is a plan. It's a description of someone else's week.

Here's mine. Three years in a row I resolved to be a 6am gym person. Three years in a row it was dead by February, and not for any of the reasons the articles list. My rotation would flip to nights, and 6am stopped being a time I could do anything with. On nights, 6am is either the middle of your sleep or the tail end of a shift. The habit wasn't failing. The clock it was tied to had stopped meaning anything.

That's the part the resolution articles get wrong. They look at the February drop-off and decide the person was vague, or overambitious, or didn't want it badly enough. Nobody checks whether the advice fit the life it was handed to. If your schedule doesn't repeat, "same time every day" isn't a rule you failed to keep. It's a rule with nowhere to attach.

What finally worked for me wasn't a better alarm. It was swapping the clock for an event. The gym is the first thing I do after any shift ends. Day shift, night shift, doesn't matter. Shift ends, I go. That's held for 14 months now, across every rotation flip, which is roughly a year longer than any of the 6am versions managed.

The difference is that a shift ending happens in every version of my week. 6am doesn't. The anchor has to be something your life reliably produces, not something a calendar produces.

So if you're planning something for January: before you write down a time, ask what in your week actually repeats. Maybe it's clocking out. Maybe it's handover. Maybe it's the baby going down, whenever that turns out to be. Hang the habit on that. If the thing you pick disappears when your rotation changes, it was never going to hold, and that's not a verdict on you.

The advice isn't bad. It's just addressed to someone with a 9-to-5. Feel free to forward it along.
