Before I draft this, a few questions so the talk sounds like you and fits the room:

1. **What's your answer?** Do you think engineers *should* use AI assistants for everything, shouldn't, or is your point that "everything" is the wrong question? If you haven't settled on it yet, which way are you leaning?

2. **What's your role, and why are you the one giving this talk?** For example, an engineering leader setting direction, an individual engineer with strong opinions, or someone outside engineering asking the question. That changes how much authority the talk can claim and how hard it can push.

3. **Who's in the room?** Roughly how big is the company, and what share of the audience is engineers versus sales, ops, leadership and so on? Should non-engineers leave with something too?

4. **Where does your company stand today?** Is there an official policy, a tool everyone already uses, a rollout underway, or a recent debate or incident (a bug, a security worry, a big productivity win) that made this a topic?

5. **What should people do or think afterward?** Change a habit, back a policy, start a discussion, or just get a clear framing of the tradeoffs?

6. **Do you have your own material?** A personal story, a team example, or numbers from your own work would make this far stronger than general claims about AI tools.

7. **Tone and format:** Is it light and conversational, or more serious? Will you have slides, and do you want the opening written word for word or as talking points?
