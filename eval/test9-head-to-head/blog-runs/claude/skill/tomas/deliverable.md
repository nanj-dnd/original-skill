**Your resolution didn't fail. You stopped counting.**

Every January the same post goes around: most resolutions are dead by February. Everyone nods. Nobody asks why that's supposed to be bad news.

It isn't. Most attempts at anything hard fail. That's what "hard" means. The problem isn't the failure rate. It's that people score one attempt, call the result final, and wait eleven months to try again with the same plan.

I quit smoking on the fourth serious attempt. I've been smoke-free six years. By the usual scoring, I failed three times. By any useful scoring, I ran three experiments.

Attempt two lasted five weeks and broke at a wedding. At the time that felt like a verdict. It was actually the most useful thing that happened all year, because it told me exactly where my plan had a hole: drinking, in a crowd, with people I don't see often. I'd never have found that by thinking harder. I found it by breaking.

The first three attempts, between them, gave me my trigger list. First coffee of the day. Driving. My brother. Attempt four wasn't more willpower. It was the same person with a map of where the previous three went off the road, and a plan for each spot. It held.

So here's the only resolution advice I'll offer.

Stop measuring whether the resolution survived. Measure how many attempts you made and what each one told you. An attempt that breaks in week five and leaves you with a written note saying *where* and *why* is worth more than one that limps along to March on vague good intentions and fades without a lesson.

Concretely: when it breaks, and it will, write down the date, the place, who you were with, what you'd been doing in the hour before. Don't editorialise. Don't write "I was weak." Write "Saturday, wedding, third drink, outside with the smokers." That's a finding. Then plan the next attempt around that finding specifically, and start it as soon as you've written the note. Not next January. Now.

One attempt per year is a terrible experimental design. Four attempts in eighteen months, each one patching the hole the last one found, is how things actually get quit.

You're not failing. You're running trials. Just make sure you keep the results.
