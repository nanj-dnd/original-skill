**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt yields: "Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] runs a sizeable fleet. Our software cuts fuel costs and delivery times by X% through AI route optimisation. Companies like yours have saved Y. Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week?" Its choices:

- **Angle**: generic savings pitch, leading with the product and a round-number percentage.
- **Proof**: an unnamed "companies like yours" case or an invented stat.
- **Structure**: compliment → product → stat → ask for a call.
- **Reader**: treated as a budget-holder who cares about cost, not as someone running a day-to-day operation.
- **Tone**: polished, salesy, slightly breathless; "I hope this finds you well."

A few questions so this one isn't that. Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`.

**1. What's the hook?** The default leads with your product and a savings percentage.
- a) A specific operational pain you've seen at mid-size carriers (e.g. dispatchers re-planning by hand when a driver calls in sick, or empty return legs)
- b) Something specific about *their* company (a route, a region, a recent change like a new depot or contract)
- c) A result from an existing customer, stated concretely
- d) Keep it product-first; just make it tighter

**2. Real material.** What do you actually have that I can put in it? Pick what applies and tell me the detail.
- a) A named or describable customer and one number (e.g. "a 40-truck regional carrier cut planning time from 3 hours to 20 minutes")
- b) A measurable claim about the product itself (what it does that others don't, with a figure)
- c) A real observation about the target company
- d) Nothing concrete yet; write around it

**3. The ask.** The default asks for a 15-minute call.
- a) A short call
- b) Something lower-effort: send them a one-page comparison, or offer to run one of their routes through it
- c) Just a reply: "worth a look, or wrong person?"

**4. Voice.** Whose name is on it, and how should it sound to a head of ops?
- a) Plain and direct, from you (founder/sales lead), no polish
- b) Plain but slightly more formal
- c) Peer-to-peer, as someone who's worked in logistics ops (only if true; tell me the background)
