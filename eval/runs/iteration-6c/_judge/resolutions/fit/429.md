# My Resolution Wasn't the Problem. The Clock Was.

Three Januaries in a row, I resolved to go to the gym at 6am. Three Februaries in a row, the habit was dead.

I used to call that a willpower problem. It wasn't. Every year, the rotation flipped me onto nights, and 6am stopped being the start of my day. On nights, 6am is either the tail end of your shift or the hour you're trying to get to sleep. The resolution didn't die because I gave up on it. It died because the time it was built on stopped existing.

Most resolution advice quietly assumes you work 9 to 5. "Same time every day." "Build a morning routine." "Block it out in your calendar." That works if this week looks like last week. If you're on rotating 12s, doing nights in a warehouse, or up every few hours with a newborn, there is no "same time." There's whatever this week hands you. And when the habit collapses, you blame yourself instead of the plan, which was never built for your life in the first place.

Here's what finally worked for me: the gym is the first thing I do after any shift ends. Day or night. No clock involved. I've kept that up for 14 months now, longer than all three 6am attempts put together.

If your schedule won't hold still either, here's what I'd pass on:

**Anchor the habit to an event, not a time.** "After my shift" happens every working day, whether that shift ends at 7pm or 7am. "At 6am" only happens when 6am is convenient. Pick something that's always going to happen: clocking out, the first coffee after you wake up, the baby's first nap.

**Check the anchor against your real rota.** Before you commit, walk it through your actual week. If that's three nights and then two days, does your anchor show up in both? If it only works on day shifts, it's going to have the same February I had, three times over.

**Let the size flex and keep the place.** The gym after a night shift won't look like the gym after a day shift, and it doesn't have to. What keeps the habit alive is that it always comes at the same point in the day, whatever time that turns out to be. A short session in the right spot beats a perfect one that keeps getting skipped.

So if you've heard all the usual advice and it never fit, that's not a character flaw. Nobody wrote that advice for your week. Build the habit around something that happens every shift, at whatever hour it happens.
