**Fixed parts:** about 400 words, written for your personal blog, on why most New Year's resolutions fail. Any statistic has to be real. The common "88% fail" and "80% quit by February" figures are shaky (one comes from an unpublished 2007 online survey, the other has no clear source), so I'll only use a number if I can stand behind it.
**Open parts:** the claim, the angle, the examples, the structure, who it's for and the tone.

## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** Resolutions fail because they're vague, too big and run on willpower. Make them specific, start small, build habits and get someone to hold you accountable.

- **Thesis:** it's a method problem (bad goal-setting plus willpower running out), not a problem with the goal itself.
- **Examples:** the packed gym in the first week of January, the "most people quit by February" statistic, "Quitter's Day."
- **Structure:** a hook stat, then three to five reasons, then a list of tips (SMART goals, tiny habits, accountability partner).
- **Reader:** a generic self-improver who wants a fix before January 1.
- **Tone:** upbeat and encouraging, a mild "you've got this."
- **Leaves out:** the writer's own experience, whether the goal was worth wanting, and whether failing a resolution is even a problem.

## A few questions

Reply like `1b 2a 3: <own words>`. Any answer can be your own words instead of a letter.

**1. The thesis.** The default says resolutions fail because of bad method (vague goals, too much reliance on willpower). Where do you stand?
- a) Agreed, it's mostly a method problem
- b) No, they fail because people pick goals they think they *should* want, not ones they actually want
- c) The premise is off: "most fail" is overstated, or failing and restarting is just how change works
- d) Your own words

**2. The ending.** The default ends with a tips list (SMART goals, small habits, accountability). How should yours end?
- a) Keep practical tips
- b) No tips, just the diagnosis, and let it land
- c) Argue against making resolutions at all, or offer something to do instead

**3. Is there a real resolution behind this?** Whichever way you answered above, a specific case usually decides what the post can say.
- a) One I've made and broken more than once (which one, and roughly how long did it last?)
- b) One that actually stuck (which one, and what was different about it?)
- c) Something I watched happen to someone else
- d) Nothing specific, keep it impersonal

**4. The reader.** The default writes for a generic self-improver looking for a fix before January 1. Who is this for?
- a) People about to set resolutions this year
- b) People who have already given up on theirs and feel bad about it
- c) Regular readers who know me, so it can be personal
- d) Your own words

**5. Tone.** The default is upbeat and encouraging. What do you want?
- a) Upbeat and encouraging
- b) Wry and self-deprecating
- c) Blunt and contrarian
