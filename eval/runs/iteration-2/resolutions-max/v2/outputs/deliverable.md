# My Resolutions Didn't Fail. I Just Forgot Them.

Every January someone explains why New Year's resolutions fail, and the explanation is always the same: the goals are too big, too vague, and powered by willpower that runs dry by February. I don't think that's wrong. But when I look at my own track record, it doesn't quite describe what happened.

I've made resolutions. I couldn't tell you what most of them were. They didn't collapse in some dramatic moment of weakness. They just stopped being there. At some point early in the year they slid out of my head, and I didn't notice they were gone until it was time to make new ones.

You can't run out of willpower on something you've forgotten about. So I've started to think the real problem is the date.

To be fair to January 1, it's a great day for *starting* things. Psychologists even have a name for this, the "fresh start effect": people are more likely to begin a new goal right after a landmark like New Year, a birthday, or even a Monday. The clean page feels good. It gets you moving.

But a date is a terrible thing to hang a goal on, because a date only happens once. On January 1 the resolution has every reason to exist. Everyone's making them, the year is new, you're in the mood. By the second week of January none of that is true anymore. Nothing in an ordinary Tuesday points back to a promise you made on New Year's Eve. The calendar gave you a push and then walked off.

I think that's also why vague goals fail. "Get healthier" and "read more" aren't just hard to measure. They aren't attached to anything. No moment in your week reminds you of them. A vague goal and an arbitrary date fail in the same way: nothing in your actual life sets them off.

The changes that do stick tend to start somewhere else, when something in your life actually shifts. A new job, a worrying check-up, a move, a friend who asks you to come running with them. The reason is built into your days, so the goal keeps turning up whether you remember it or not.

So here's my one suggestion, and then I'll leave you alone. When you notice you want to change something, start that day, not next January 1. If the reason is real enough to make you want it, it's real enough not to wait for the calendar.
