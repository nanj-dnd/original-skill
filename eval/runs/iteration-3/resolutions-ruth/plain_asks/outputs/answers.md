Thank you for asking first, that's thoughtful of you. I'll take your questions in order.

1. Yes, I have one of each, and they belong together. For years, and I mean years, every January I resolved to walk more. I'd buy new shoes, go out twice, and by the middle of February I'd be back to watching the weather from the kitchen window. Then two years ago my neighbour Joan had her hip done and her physio told her she needed to walk every day, but she wasn't steady enough to go alone. So I said I'd go with her. We've walked every weekday at 9 o'clock for two years now. Rain, cold, the lot. (Last winter we did laps of the garden centre when the paths were icy, which the staff found very amusing.) The thing I keep telling people is this: I'd never let Joan down. I let myself down all the time. Please use that line, it's the heart of it.

2. "Something else." I don't think it's really about vague goals or the ritual. I think resolutions fail because they're about improving yourself on your own, and nobody is waiting for you to turn up. When I look back, the ones that worked in my life were the ones where somebody else was counting on me. Same reason I never missed a filing deadline in forty years of accounting but couldn't keep a diary going past the third of January. So please don't go with your default about willpower. That's the opposite of what I'm saying.

3. Warm and reflective, I'd say, with a little dry humour. I tend to tell a small story and then say what I make of it. I don't have a post handy to paste, I'm afraid, but if you write it the way I'm writing to you now you won't go far wrong.

4. Mostly other retirees here in town. Some people I know from church and the library, some I don't. That matters, actually. So much of what's written about resolutions is aimed at young people trying to be more productive, getting up at 5 and all that. My readers aren't trying to be productive. Most of us are trying to stay connected. A resolution that gets you out the door to meet someone does far more good at our age than one about efficiency.

5. Keep it personal, please. Maybe one figure if it's well known, but my readers don't come to me for statistics. They get enough of those from their doctors.

6. Reflective, but with a gentle nudge at the end. Something along the lines of: instead of a resolution about yourself, find someone who could use you, a neighbour, a friend who's on her own, and make the promise to them. I wouldn't want a bulleted list of tips. It isn't that kind of blog.
