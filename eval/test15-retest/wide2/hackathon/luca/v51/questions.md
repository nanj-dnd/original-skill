## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Build an AI-powered personal carbon footprint tracker that nudges people toward greener habits."
- **Angle:** mitigation through individual behaviour change (the most common climate-hackathon pitch).
- **User:** a generic eco-conscious consumer.
- **Build:** a mobile/web app that scans receipts or links bank data, estimates CO₂ with an LLM or emissions API, and shows a dashboard.
- **Hook:** gamification with streaks, badges and a friends leaderboard.
- **Demo:** live walkthrough where someone scans a receipt, the number appears, and a slide shows "if 1M people used this…" impact maths.

## A few questions before I write

Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`, and add a sentence wherever an option asks for detail.

**1. Who it's for.** The default serves individual consumers. Who should yours serve?
- a) Individuals, but a specific group (e.g. renters, students, commuters in your city)
- b) An organisation you can reach: your campus, office or a local business. Name it.
- c) People facing climate *impacts*, such as heat, flooding or smoke, rather than people cutting emissions
- d) Keep it consumer-wide like the default

**2. Your real material.** The default runs on generic emissions estimates and has no real user. What do you have to build from?
- a) A real problem someone you know has. Describe it in a line.
- b) A dataset, API, sensor or hardware you can actually get this weekend. Name it.
- c) Just the team. Give the size, skills and stack, e.g. "3 people: React, Python, one designer."
- d) Nothing yet, so pick something buildable from public data

**3. What the judges reward.** The default demo optimises for a polished app walkthrough plus big impact maths. What will win in your room?
- a) Technical depth: a working, non-trivial build
- b) Real-world impact and a credible path past the weekend
- c) Storytelling and polish: a 3-minute pitch with a strong moment
- d) Don't know or unstated

**4. Which climate problem.** The default picks mitigation via personal habits. Where should yours sit?
- a) Mitigation, but at the system level (energy, buildings, food waste, logistics)
- b) Adaptation: helping people cope with heat, floods or air quality
- c) Measurement or verification: making climate claims checkable
- d) Open, so choose whatever best fits my answers above
