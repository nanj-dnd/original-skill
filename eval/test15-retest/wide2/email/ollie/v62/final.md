## Angle

- **Angle:** Open on what a missed delivery window really costs an ops team, then ask one honest question instead of booking a meeting. Without proof to hand, a question is easier to answer than a pitch.
- **Built from:** 1b (missed windows as the hook), 2d (lead with the problem, not proof) and your note that you don't know any real numbers yet, 3b (warm, professional, from an SDR), 4 (nothing invented, no specific recipient, no product details).
- **Strongest objection:** Ops heads reply to results, and a pain-only email with no proof can read as fishing. **Test:** Send this to 20 to 30 prospects and a standard "20 minutes next week?" version to a matched group, then compare reply rates after two weeks.
- **Other direction:** Lead with the dispatcher's day, the morning re-plan when a driver calls in sick, rather than the customer-facing miss.

---

**Subject:** When a delivery misses its window

Hi,

When a delivery misses its time window, the cost rarely stops at one late drop. There's the second attempt, the call from an unhappy customer, and a dispatcher reshuffling the rest of the day around it.

I'm on the sales team at a company that builds route-optimisation software, and missed windows are usually where planning problems show up first.

I won't pretend to know how your routes run, so here's one question instead: when a window gets missed on your network, what's usually behind it? Is it the plan itself, or what happens after drivers head out?

If it's the plan, I'd be glad to show you how we approach it. If it's the second, thanks for reading, and I won't take up more of your time.

Best,

**Before you use it**
- Add your name, company, product name and signature under "Best,". Put the recipient's first name in the greeting once you have a list.
- If you can confirm with your team which parts of the problem the product handles (time windows, live re-planning, driver hours), one plain sentence about that would strengthen the third paragraph.
- If your team has a real customer result you can share, add it as a single line after the second paragraph.
