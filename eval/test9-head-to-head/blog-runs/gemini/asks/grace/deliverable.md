Every January, millions of us participate in a beautiful, collective delusion: the New Year’s resolution. We are suddenly convinced that as the clock strikes midnight, some magical atmospheric shift will instantly transform us from perfectly adequate humans into highly disciplined, green-juice-drinking, marathon-running machines.

I am, of course, absolutely guilty of this. If you look closely at my bank statements from a few years ago, you will find a painfully optimistic charge on January 2nd for a premium gym membership. I went all in. I bought the expensive moisture-wicking shirts. I bought one of those giant water bottles that aggressively yells at you to hydrate. For three glorious weeks, I was unstoppable. But by the second week of February, my pristine gym shoes had retired to the darkest corner of my closet, and the only heavy lifting I was doing was carrying Thai takeout from the front porch.

Why do we do this to ourselves? Why do most resolutions crash and burn before Valentine’s Day?

The main culprit is that we try to change our entire personality overnight. We don’t just want to read more; we want to read a book a week while simultaneously learning conversational French and giving up sugar cold turkey. We treat our lives like an extreme home makeover show, relying entirely on a sudden burst of willpower. But motivation is a notoriously fickle friend. It is loud and exciting on New Year's Eve, but it completely ghosts you on a freezing Tuesday morning when your alarm goes off at 5:30 AM.

This year, instead of setting ourselves up for a spectacular February burnout, I am trying something different: micro-habits.

The idea behind micro-habits is to make your goal so ridiculously small that you cannot possibly fail. Instead of resolving to work out for an hour every day, commit to doing exactly two push-ups before your morning shower. Instead of vowing to read fifty books this year, aim to read one single page before you go to sleep.

It sounds almost suspiciously easy, but that is the entire point. By setting the bar on the floor, you bypass your brain’s natural resistance to massive change. Two push-ups often turn into ten, and one page naturally turns into a chapter. And even if they don't, you still kept your promise to yourself.

So, as we stare down another January, let’s give ourselves a break. Let's aim a little lower, celebrate the tiny victories, and save our money on the moisture-wicking shirts.
