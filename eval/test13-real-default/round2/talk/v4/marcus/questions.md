**What everyone gets**

Main claim: "AI coding assistants are a powerful tool, not a replacement — use them for the boring parts, keep humans in charge of the hard parts."

- Angle: balanced middle ("yes, but with judgment"), so nobody in the room disagrees and nobody remembers it.
- Examples: boilerplate, tests and docs on the "yes" side; architecture, security and "code you don't understand" on the "no" side.
- Structure: hook question → the upside → the risks → three guidelines → "let's be thoughtful" close.
- Reader: addressed to a generic audience, no sense of who's actually in the room or who you are to them.
- Tone: upbeat, a bit TED, opens with a rhetorical question ("How many of you shipped AI-written code this week?").

A few questions before I write — reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`:

**1. Where do you actually land?** (Default: "use it for some things, not everything.")
a) Roughly that — it's a tool, use judgment.
b) Further: use it for almost everything; the real bottleneck is review and taste, not whether to use it.
c) More sceptical: we're already over-using it and it's showing in the codebase or the team.
d) Genuinely unsure — you want to frame it as an open question for the company, not give an answer.

**2. One real thing from your team to build on** — this is what will make it yours:
a) A specific moment: an incident, bug or save that an assistant caused or caught (tell me what happened).
b) A number: share of PRs or lines that are AI-written, review time, something you've measured or seen in a dashboard.
c) A line someone said — an engineer, a reviewer, a PM — that stuck with you.
d) Nothing specific; keep it general.

**3. Who are you to this room?**
a) An engineer talking to fellow engineers, with the rest of the company listening in.
b) An eng lead or manager setting (or proposing) direction.
c) Talking mainly to non-engineers who want to know what this means for the product and the business.

**4. How should it sound?**
a) Plain and conversational, the way you'd actually say it standing up.
b) More polished — all-hands formal.
c) A bit provocative, deliberately picking a fight with the comfortable answer.
