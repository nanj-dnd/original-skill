**One year ago I became a manager. Here's what I got wrong, and what I'd tell myself on day one.**

I thought the hard part would be the work. It wasn't. The hard part was learning that my job was no longer the work.

A few things I learned the slow way:

**1. Doing it yourself is the easy way out.**
When someone on my team was stuck, my instinct was to jump in and fix it. It felt helpful. It was actually me avoiding the harder job of coaching them through it. Every time I "saved" a deliverable, I robbed someone of a chance to get better.

**2. Silence is not consensus.**
I used to end meetings thinking everyone agreed because nobody objected. Turns out people were agreeing to *me*, not the idea. Now I ask "what would make this fail?" before I ask "does this make sense?"

**3. Feedback delayed is feedback denied.**
I sat on a hard conversation for three weeks because I wanted to get the wording perfect. By the time I had it, the moment had passed and the person felt blindsided. Imperfect and timely beats polished and late.

**4. Your calendar is your real priorities.**
I said my team came first. Then I looked at my calendar and saw 1:1s were the first thing I'd move when something "urgent" came up. People notice that. I stopped moving them.

**5. You will feel useless, and that's often a good sign.**
Some weeks I shipped nothing I could point to. But the team shipped more than ever. It took me months to accept that my output now shows up in other people's work.
