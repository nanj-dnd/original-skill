# You're Counting the Wrong Thing

Every January someone passes around a number: most New Year's resolutions are dead by February. I don't know where it comes from, and I'm not sure anyone does. But suppose it's right. What exactly is it counting?

It's counting attempts. One try, one start date, one verdict by Valentine's Day. Then it calls that the outcome and announces that resolutions fail. That's timing one lap and calling it the race.

I smoked for fifteen years. I've been clean for six. In between, I quit seriously four times. By the February test, I failed three times out of four. By the only test that matters, I quit smoking.

The three that didn't hold weren't wasted. Each one showed me something I couldn't have worked out by thinking about it: the first coffee of the day, driving, my brother. Attempt two lasted five weeks and ended at a wedding. That's how I learned that weddings plus drinking were going to get me every time. Attempt four was planned around exactly that, and it's the one that stuck.

What changed between the first attempt and the fourth wasn't willpower. I didn't get tougher. I got better informed. I knew what would get me, because three attempts had already shown me.

That's why I don't buy most of the advice that shows up this time of year. Make it smaller, make it specific, build a system. Maybe. But all of it assumes the first attempt is supposed to work, and that if it doesn't, you set it up wrong. For anything hard, the first attempt mostly isn't going to work. Its job is to find out where you break.

Smoking is the clearest case I know. Most people who quit for good got there after several attempts, not one. If we measured quitting the way we measure resolutions, we'd conclude that quitting smoking doesn't work. It obviously does. It just doesn't usually work the first time.

Some of you reading this have slipped recently. I know, because I met some of you on a quit forum. I'm not going to tell you it's fine. I'm going to ask what it showed you. Where were you, who were you with, what time was it, what had you just done? That isn't a confession. It's the plan for the next attempt.

The resolution didn't fail in February. February was the first lap. Count the attempts, and keep the notes.
