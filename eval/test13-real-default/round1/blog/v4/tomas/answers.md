1: none of those. The frame's wrong. "Most resolutions fail" treats one attempt as the whole thing. Most *attempts* fail, and that's normal. You should be counting attempts, not grading outcomes. Most people who quit smoking for good needed several tries.

2a, sort of. I smoked for 15 years and quit on my 4th serious attempt; six years clean now. Attempt 2 lasted 5 weeks and died at a wedding. That's how I learned drinking at weddings was the trigger, so attempt 4 was planned around exactly that. The first three taught me my triggers: first coffee, driving, my brother. They weren't failures, they were data.

3c. The reframe: count attempts, not outcomes. Each failed one tells you something specific if you look at where it broke.
