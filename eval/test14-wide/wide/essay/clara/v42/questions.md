**What everyone gets**

Main claim: "Social media has had both positive and negative effects on democracy, but on balance it has done more harm than good." Choices a pasted prompt makes:

- **Angle**: hedged "both sides, net negative," ending with a call for regulation or media literacy.
- **Examples**: Arab Spring for the good; 2016 election / Cambridge Analytica, Brexit, January 6, Myanmar for the bad.
- **Structure**: intro → benefits paragraph → harms paragraphs → brief counterargument → conclusion.
- **Reader**: a generic instructor; nobody in particular.
- **Tone**: balanced, academic, no real stake.

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

**1. Stance.** The default says "mixed, but net harmful." Where are you?
a) Net harmful, and I want to argue it hard
b) Net good, the harms are overstated
c) "Good or bad" is the wrong question: the effect depends on something else (platform design, strength of existing institutions, who holds power) and I want to argue that
d) Genuinely undecided, let the evidence lead

**2. What "democracy" means here.** The default blurs elections, debate quality, and institutions together.
a) Elections and outcomes (who wins, turnout)
b) Quality of public debate and deliberation
c) Trust in institutions and willingness to accept losing
d) Participation: who gets a voice who didn't before

**3. Your material.** The default leans on Arab Spring and 2016. Do you have something closer?
a) A case I watched up close (an election, movement, local campaign, or online fight) — tell me what it was in a few words
b) A specific study, number, or reading from the course I'm meant to use
c) Use the well-known cases, but go deep on one instead of listing five
d) Nothing specific, pick for me

**4. Reader and rules.** The default assumes a balanced academic essay for an instructor.
a) Course assignment — tell me the course and any rules (sources required, first person allowed, etc.)
b) Argumentative/debate piece: pick a side and defend it
c) Op-ed style for a general reader
d) Something else
