**What everyone gets**

The main claim: "The biggest lesson of my first year managing was that my job is no longer to do the work, it's to make the team able to do it." Then the choices a pasted prompt makes:

- **Angle**: the IC-to-manager shift; letting go of control, learning to delegate.
- **Examples**: generic ones — 1:1s, giving hard feedback, "I thought I had to have all the answers", hiring.
- **Structure**: a one-line hook, a numbered list of 5 lessons, a closing question to the comments.
- **Reader**: other new or aspiring managers, written to feel relatable.
- **Tone**: vulnerable-but-confident, short lines, "here's what I'd tell myself a year ago".

Four questions, answer all at once (e.g. `1b 2c 3a 4a`, plus a sentence or two for Q2):

**1. The lesson.** The default's lesson is "stop doing the work, enable the team." What's yours, really?
a) That one, it's true for me too
b) Something I believed going in and had to reverse
c) A specific skill I underrated (feedback, 1:1s, saying no, managing up)
d) Something about myself, not about management technique

**2. One real moment** (the post is written as you, so I'll only use what you give me). Pick one and add a sentence:
a) A conversation: something a report, your own manager, or a peer said that stuck
b) A number: team size, people hired or lost, something shipped or missed, how long something took
c) A mistake: what you did, what it cost, what you changed
d) I'd rather not share specifics; write around it

**3. Shape.** The default is a hook plus a numbered list of five lessons.
a) Keep the list, but fewer and sharper (3)
b) One lesson, told as the story from Q2, no list
c) A short post, under 120 words, one idea
d) Longer and reflective, 250–300 words

**4. Reader and sound.** The default is relatable advice for other new managers.
a) Plain and direct, for peers and other new managers
b) More polished, aware that senior leaders and recruiters will read it
c) Casual and a bit funny, as if my own team will read it
