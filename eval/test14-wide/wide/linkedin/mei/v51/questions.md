**What everyone gets**

The main claim: "Managing isn't about being the best at the work anymore; it's about making other people effective." Choices a pasted prompt makes:

- Angle: humility arc — "I thought X, I learned Y" (usually: stop doing the work yourself, listen more, give feedback sooner).
- Structure: hook line, then a numbered list of 3–5 lessons, then a "still learning" sign-off.
- Examples: generic ("a 1:1 where I realised…", "a project that slipped") with no names, numbers or dates.
- Reader: other new managers, framed as advice.
- Tone: warm, polished, slightly inspirational; ends with a question to drive comments.

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`)

1. The default angle is "stop doing the work, start enabling people." Which is closest to what your year was actually about?
   a) That — letting go of doing it myself
   b) Hard conversations: feedback, performance, someone I had to let go or push
   c) Managing upward / being the buffer between my team and leadership
   d) Something else (tell me in a line)

2. One real moment the post can be built on — the default has none. Give me one of these, as specific as you can:
   a) Something that happened (a meeting, a mistake, a decision), with roughly when
   b) A number (team size, how many 1:1s, a result that moved, how long something took)
   c) A line someone actually said to you — a report, your boss, a peer
   d) I'd rather it stay general with no personal specifics

3. The default is a numbered list of lessons. Shape?
   a) One story told start to finish, with the lesson at the end
   b) One lesson, argued, no list
   c) A short list, but 3 max and each tied to a real thing
   d) Fine with the default list

4. How should it sound, under your name on LinkedIn?
   a) Plain and direct, short sentences, no inspirational close
   b) Reflective and warm, the usual LinkedIn register
   c) A bit dry or self-deprecating
   d) Hand it to my own voice skill
