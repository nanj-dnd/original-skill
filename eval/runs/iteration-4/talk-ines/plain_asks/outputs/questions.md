A few questions before I draft this, so the outline and opening fit your room instead of being a generic "AI: pros and cons" talk:

1. **What's your position?** Are you arguing for "yes, for everything," "no, not for everything," or a middle line (say, "yes, but here's where we draw it")? Or do you want to stay neutral and open the question up for the company?

2. **Who's in the audience?** At an all-hands it's probably mostly non-engineers (sales, ops, leadership). Should the talk work for them, or is it really aimed at the engineers in the room? And what's your role: engineer, eng manager, someone outside engineering?

3. **What's going on at your company that makes this worth raising now?** For example: a mandate or push from leadership, a tool rollout, an incident or bug traced to AI-written code, a debate inside the eng team, cost or security concerns. A concrete trigger is usually the best hook for the opening.

4. **Do you have your own material to draw on?** Stories, numbers, or examples from your team (a time an assistant saved a week, a time it caused a mess, adoption figures, review-load changes). Even one real anecdote will make this land far better than general claims.

5. **What should people do or think differently afterward?** For example: adopt a guideline, back a policy decision, stop treating it as all-or-nothing, or just understand the tradeoffs.

6. **Tone and format:** Serious and measured, or light with some humor? Will you have slides, or is it just you talking? And should I write the opening paragraph to be read word for word, or as talking points?
