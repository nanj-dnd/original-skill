**The resolution didn't fail. The clock did.**

Three Januaries in a row I resolved to be at the gym at 6am. Three Februaries in a row the rotation flipped and the habit died. Not because I stopped wanting it. Because 6am stopped being a time I could reliably be anywhere.

The usual reading of that is a willpower story. You wanted it in January; by February you didn't want it enough. Every article about why resolutions fail lands on some version of this, and then hands you the cure: same time every day, build a morning routine, stack it onto your coffee.

Read that advice again and notice who it's for. Same time every day. Morning routine. It assumes a life where Monday looks like Tuesday, where "morning" is a fixed thing that happens once per day at a predictable hour. That's a real life. A lot of people have it. Most of the people reading this don't.

Nurses, warehouse staff, anyone with a newborn: your week doesn't have a shape, it has a roster. The one ingredient the advice depends on, a stable clock, is the one ingredient you can't supply. So you build the plan on a time, the roster moves the time, the plan collapses, and then you get told it's a discipline problem. It isn't. It's a calendar problem wearing a discipline costume.

What finally stuck for me wasn't more resolve. It was changing what the habit was attached to. Gym is the first thing after any shift ends. Day shift, night shift, doesn't matter. Shift ends, gym. The rotation can flip as often as it likes; "after shift" still exists every single week, because a shift ending is something that happens to me regardless of what the clock says. Fourteen months now. 6am turned out to be a lovely idea for someone else.

The difference is small on paper and enormous in practice. A time belongs to your roster. An event belongs to you. Anchor a habit to a time and you've handed its survival to whoever writes the schedule. Anchor it to something that happens anyway, end of shift, the handover, the drive home, and it goes wherever your week goes.

So if you're about to write a resolution for January, don't pick a time. Pick a thing that already happens in your week no matter what the week looks like, and put the new thing right after it. The resolution was never the problem.
