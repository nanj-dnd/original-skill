## Opening Paragraph

"Good morning everyone. Let’s talk about the elephant in the IDE: AI coding assistants. If you look at the tech news right now, you’d think AI is going to write our entire codebase by next Tuesday. But as the engineers in the room know, the reality on the ground is a bit more complicated. Today, I want to take just five minutes to answer a question that’s been floating around: Should we be using AI for *everything*? The short answer is no. The slightly longer answer is that AI is an incredible exoskeleton, but it makes a terrible architect. Let's break down exactly where we should be leaning on these tools to move faster, and where we need to keep our own hands firmly on the keyboard."

---

## Presentation Outline (5 Minutes)

**1. The Hook & The Reality (1 minute)**

* **Deliver the opening paragraph** (above) to set the stage and grab attention from both technical and non-technical staff.
* **The Current State:** Briefly acknowledge which tools the team is already experimenting with (e.g., Copilot, Cursor, ChatGPT).
* **The Goal:** Frame the talk not as "pro-AI" or "anti-AI," but as "maximizing leverage without sacrificing quality."

**2. Where AI Shines: The "Exoskeleton" (1.5 minutes)**

* *Focus on velocity and reducing toil.*
* **Boilerplate & Scaffolding:** Generating the mundane setup code that nobody likes writing.
* **Test Generation:** Rapidly writing unit tests for edge cases (with human review).
* **Translating & Explaining:** Quickly deciphering legacy code, complex regex, or translating a script from Python to Go.
* **Takeaway:** Use AI for the highly repetitive, well-documented, lower-risk tasks.

**3. Where Humans Rule: The "Architect" (1.5 minutes)**

* *Focus on context, security, and business logic.*
* **System Architecture:** AI doesn't know our specific infrastructure, our technical debt, or our scaling bottlenecks.
* **The "Why" Behind the Code:** AI can write a function, but it doesn't understand our user's true intent or the nuanced product requirements.
* **Security & Edge Cases:** Hallucinations are real. Blindly trusting AI with critical security protocols or complex business logic introduces hidden vulnerabilities.
* **Takeaway:** We are paid for our judgment and domain expertise, not just our typing speed.

**4. The Path Forward: Our Philosophy (1 minute)**

* **AI as a Pair Programmer, Not an Autopilot:** The engineer is always the pilot in command. You are responsible for the code you commit, regardless of who (or what) drafted it.
* **The Golden Rule:** If you don't understand the code the AI generated, do not commit it.
* **Call to Action:** Encourage the team to share their biggest AI "wins" and "fails" in a designated Slack channel so the engineering org can learn together.
* **Wrap-up & Handoff.**
