**Angle**: Supporting people came easily; what cost me an engineer was never telling anyone plainly what to change.
**Built from**: 1b (feedback, not support, was the hard part), the exit-interview moment and the weekly-1:1 fix from 2c, structure 3a, tone 4a.
**Strongest objection**: One departure doesn't prove a pattern; she may have left for reasons the exit interview didn't surface. **Test**: Before posting, ask two current reports to tell you, unprompted, the last concrete piece of feedback they got from you. If they can't, the post is true.
**Other direction**: Frame the written agenda as the lesson (process beats intention) rather than the feedback itself.

---

In month four, an engineer on my team resigned. In her exit interview she said she had never once had clear feedback on her work.

I had thought things were fine. That was the problem. "Fine" was the only signal I'd been sending: no corrections, no specifics, nothing she could act on. From where I sat, that looked like support. From where she sat, there was nothing to work with.

Going in, I'd assumed the hard part of managing would be backing people: clearing blockers, making time, being on their side. That part comes fairly naturally to most of us. The hard part is telling someone, plainly, what isn't working yet. It feels unkind in the moment, so it's easy to put off, and the cost of putting it off is invisible until someone leaves.

After she left, I changed one thing. Every report now gets a weekly 1:1 with a written agenda, and each one includes one specific thing to change. Not a review, not a lecture. One thing. Written down, because anything said out loud is easy to soften into nothing.

It isn't a dramatic fix. It just means nobody on my team gets to month four without knowing where they stand.

---

**Before you use it**: Check whether she'd be comfortable with the exit-interview line being recognisable, even unnamed. If you have one example of a "one thing to change" that landed well, a single sentence of it would strengthen the last third.

**Distance from the default**: The claim moved from "enable others" to "honest feedback is the hard part" (1b); the listicle became one story (3a); hook line, hashtags, thank-you and closing question are gone (4a). It still shares with all three samples the "I avoided hard feedback" lesson, but that is your view, told through your engineer rather than a generic admission.
