## Outline

**1. Opening: take the question literally (0:00 to 0:40)**
- The question is "for everything?", so instead of answering in principle, show one real day.
- Name the day: [day/date]. Say it plainly for a room that mostly doesn't code: "here's what my job actually is, hour by hour."

**2. The day, stop by stop (0:40 to 3:30)**
About 30 seconds per stop, 4 to 5 stops. For each one: the task in plain words (no jargon; say what it does for a customer or colleague), what the AI did, and the verdict.
- **Stop 1: [task]. It did the work.** [What it produced; why you trusted it, e.g., easy to check.]
- **Stop 2: [task]. It helped.** [What it got you started on; what you still had to do yourself.]
- **Stop 3: [task]. I switched it off.** [Why: e.g., you needed to understand it yourself, or a mistake would be costly or hard to spot.]
- **Stop 4: [task]. It went sideways.** [What it got wrong, how you noticed, what it cost: minutes, hours, a review comment.]
- *(Optional) Stop 5: [task where AI wasn't relevant at all: a meeting, a decision, a conversation].* This shows non-engineers how much of the job isn't typing code.

Give each stop a quick visual tag (one slide, or just say it out loud): **did it / helped / off / sideways.**

**3. What the day shows (3:30 to 4:15)**
- Count the tags. "So, for everything? On [day], it was [X of Y] tasks."
- Name the line *your* day suggests, in one sentence: [where you drew it]. *(Fill in from your log. One line people often land on: it does the work where checking the result is quick, and stays off where being wrong is costly or hard to see. Use it only if your day actually shows it.)*
- One bridge to the room: the same sorting works for anyone's job. [Optional: one example from a non-engineering team you know.]

**4. Ending: the honest part (4:15 to 5:00)**
- "And here's what I can't tell you: how much faster all of that made me."
- The perception gap: in a 2025 study by METR, experienced developers using AI tools were slower on real tasks while believing they'd been faster. *(Quoted from memory, not checked: roughly 16 developers, about 19% slower, about 20% faster in their own estimate. Verify before you say any numbers; if you can't, say "one study found developers felt faster while actually being slower.")*
- Your own version: [whether you can tell for yourself; if your log gave you a hint either way, say it].
- Close with a question, not a policy: "So before we argue about *everything*, I think the question worth answering is: how would we know?"
