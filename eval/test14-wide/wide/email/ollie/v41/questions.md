**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt produces: "Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] runs a sizeable fleet across [region]. Our route-optimisation software has helped logistics firms cut fuel costs by 15–20% and reduce late deliveries. Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week?" Its choices:

- **Angle**: generic savings claim (fuel %, fewer late deliveries) with no evidence attached.
- **Opening**: flattery/observation about their fleet, then straight into the pitch.
- **Proof**: a round-number stat, no named customer.
- **Ask**: "15-minute call next week."
- **Tone**: polite-salesy, mid-formal, signed by a sales rep.

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`):

1. Proof. The default uses a vague "15–20% fuel savings." What real result can the email carry?
   a) A named (or describable) customer and one number: e.g. "a 40-truck regional carrier cut X" — tell me the figure and what it measured
   b) A number from your own pilots/benchmarks, no customer name — tell me the figure
   c) No hard number yet; lean on how it works instead

2. Angle. The default pitches cost savings. What does this buyer most likely care about?
   a) Fuel and mileage cost
   b) Driver hours / overtime / retention
   c) On-time delivery and customer complaints
   d) Dispatch time: planners spending hours building routes by hand

3. The ask. The default asks for a 15-minute call next week. Which fits you?
   a) Short call
   b) Offer to run their actual route data through it (free sample output)
   c) Just reply if relevant, no meeting requested

4. Voice. Whose name is on it, and how should it sound?
   a) Founder, plain and direct
   b) Sales/BD person, plain and direct
   c) More formal, company-letterhead tone

If you have a specific company or person in mind, or any detail about them (fleet size, region, what they haul), add it in the reply.
