One year as a manager. The part I wasn't ready for wasn't the work.

Planning, handing things off, sitting in more meetings: those are adjustments. You get better at them with repetition.

The part nobody trained me for is the people part. Three kinds of situation taught me more this year than anything else, and none of them come with a playbook.

Telling someone their work isn't where it needs to be. It's tempting to soften it until it stops being feedback. What it taught me: say the actual thing, early, in plain words. Vague feels kind in the room and leaves the person guessing for weeks.

Noticing someone is struggling. It rarely gets announced. It shows up as slower replies, a task that quietly stalls, a shorter version of someone who used to talk more. What it taught me: ask sooner than feels polite, and don't try to solve it in the same conversation. Most people mainly need to know you noticed.

Two people on the team not getting on. Staying out of it feels neutral. It isn't. It's a delay, and everyone around them pays for it. What it taught me: get it in the open while it's still small.

None of this was in the job description. It turned out to be most of the job.

If you're about to manage people for the first time, spend less time on your planning tools and more time thinking about how you'll handle the conversation you're dreading. That's the one that counts.
