# My 6am Gym Habit Died Every February. The Clock Killed It.

Three Januaries in a row I resolved to be at the gym at 6am. Three Februaries in a row it was dead. Not because I stopped wanting it. Because the rotation flipped me to nights, and 6am stopped meaning anything. It was the middle of a shift, or the hour I was finally getting to sleep, or some fog in between. The habit didn't quit. The clock it was tied to stopped existing.

If you work rotating 12s, you already know this feeling. You read the same resolution advice as everyone else: "same time every day," "build a morning routine," "lay your clothes out the night before." And you think, which night? Which morning? Your week has two of each, in the wrong order.

Here's what I've landed on: most New Year's resolutions don't fail on willpower. They fail because almost all the advice is written by and for people with a 9-to-5. "Same time every day" works beautifully when every day is the same shape. For a nurse on rotating 12s, for warehouse staff, for the parent of a newborn, there is no "every day." There's just whatever the schedule did to you this week.

So I stopped anchoring to a clock and anchored to an event. The gym is the first thing I do after any shift ends. Days, nights, doesn't matter. The shift ends, I go. Fourteen months and counting, which is fourteen months longer than the 6am version ever managed.

Nothing about this is simple. Walking into a gym after twelve hours on your feet is not simple. But it's possible, which 6am on a night rotation never was. The rule finally fits the life, instead of the life having to bend around the rule.

So if yours died last February too, stop reading that as a verdict on your discipline. Look at what it was tied to. If it was a time of day, and your days don't come in a fixed shape, it was never going to survive the first rotation change. Find the thing that happens no matter what shape the day takes, and hang the habit on that. For us it's clocking out. For you it might be the kid going down, or the end of a route.

The office people can keep their morning routines. We get to build something that survives nights.
