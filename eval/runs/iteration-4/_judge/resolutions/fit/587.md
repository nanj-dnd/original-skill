# Why Most New Year's Resolutions Fail

By the third week of January, the gym is already emptier. The new journal has four entries. The meal-prep containers are back in the cupboard. If that sounds familiar, you're in good company, and it's probably not because you lack willpower.

Here's what I think is actually going on.

**The goals are too big and too vague.**
"Get healthy." "Read more." "Be less stressed." These sound like goals, but they're really wishes. No clear action is attached to them, so you can't tell whether you did it today. A resolution you can't act on this afternoon is one you'll quietly drop by February.

**We change everything at once.**
January 1st feels like a clean slate, so we pile it on: new diet, new workout plan, no phone in bed, 5 a.m. alarms. Every one of those draws from the same limited supply of attention and energy. When one slips, the whole stack tends to topple with it.

**The date is arbitrary.**
Nothing about your life actually changed at midnight. You have the same job, the same schedule and the same couch. The fresh-start feeling is real, but it's borrowed. It runs out once ordinary life resumes, and if the plan depended on that feeling, the plan goes with it.

**One bad day becomes "I've failed."**
Miss a workout, eat the cake or skip the journal, and suddenly the whole thing feels ruined. This all-or-nothing thinking does more damage than the slip itself. Habits aren't built on perfect streaks. They're built on getting back on track quickly.

**We focus on outcomes, not systems.**
"Lose 20 pounds" is a result. It depends on a lot of things you don't fully control, and progress is slow enough to be discouraging. "Walk for 20 minutes after dinner" is a system. You can do it today, and you know whether you did.

**So what works instead?**

- **Pick one thing.** Just one. Get it stable before you add anything else.
- **Make it small enough to feel almost silly.** Two pages, not two chapters. Five push-ups, not an hour at the gym. You can always do more.
- **Attach it to something you already do.** After your morning coffee, or right before you brush your teeth. Existing routines make the easiest hooks.
- **Plan for the miss.** Decide now: "If I skip a day, I never skip two." That one rule saves more resolutions than any amount of motivation.

None of this is glamorous. But the people who actually change aren't the ones with the boldest January plans. They're the ones who are still going in March.

Maybe this year, aim for March.
