Fixed: 5 minutes (about 600 to 700 spoken words), a company all-hands, next Friday, an outline plus an opening paragraph. Open: the claim, the angle, who it's for, the structure and the ending.

**What everyone gets**

Main claim: "AI coding assistants are powerful tools, not a replacement for engineers. Use them for some things, not everything, and keep a human in the loop."

Choices it makes:
- **Thesis:** the balanced middle ("it depends, use judgment").
- **Examples:** AI is good for boilerplate, tests and docs. It's bad for architecture, security and anything novel. Generic statistics like "X% faster".
- **Structure:** a hook, then benefits, then risks, then guidelines, then "the future is human + AI".
- **Assumed listener:** engineers, even though an all-hands is mostly people who aren't engineers.
- **Speaker:** a neutral voice with no stake and no story of their own.
- **Leaves out:** anything specific to your company, like a real incident, a real number or a decision that's actually pending.
