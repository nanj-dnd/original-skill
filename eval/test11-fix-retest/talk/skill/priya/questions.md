**What everyone gets**

The main claim a pasted prompt produces: "AI assistants are a powerful tool, but not a replacement for judgment — use them for boilerplate and tests, stay hands-on for architecture and anything you don't understand." It chooses:

- **Angle**: balanced "tool, not crutch" — nobody in the room disagrees, nobody remembers it.
- **Examples**: generic — "it hallucinates APIs," "it's great for unit tests," no case from your own codebase.
- **Structure**: hook → three benefits → three risks → "use judgment" close.
- **Reader**: an undifferentiated audience; it doesn't know whether the room is already all-in, sceptical, or split.
- **Tone**: polished and a little corporate; sounds like a policy memo read aloud.

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`)

1. The default takes a "both sides, use judgment" stance. Where do *you* actually land?
   - a) Yes, for nearly everything — the holdouts are the problem
   - b) Mostly yes, but one specific area should stay off-limits (tell me which)
   - c) No — we're overusing it and it's costing us in review time / understanding / quality
   - d) Genuinely undecided; I want to open the question, not settle it

2. The default uses invented examples. What's one real thing that happened on your team — a win, a bug, a PR, a review that went sideways — and if you have a number (hours saved, % of PRs touched, incidents, review backlog), what is it?
   - a) I'll give a case and a number: ___
   - b) I'll give a case but no number: ___
   - c) Nothing specific I can share; keep examples general

3. The default assumes a neutral room. Where is the company already?
   - a) Most engineers are heavy users; leadership is nervous
   - b) Leadership is pushing it; engineers are resisting or quietly ignoring it
   - c) Split roughly down the middle, with some tension between camps
   - d) Barely used yet; this is early

4. The default sounds like a memo. How should this sound — spoken by you, to this room?
   - a) Plain and direct, a bit blunt, like I'd say it at standup
   - b) Conversational but careful — all-hands has non-engineers and execs in it
   - c) Formal; this is on the record and HR/leadership will reference it
