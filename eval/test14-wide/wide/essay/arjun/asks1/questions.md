Happy to help. A few questions so the thesis and outline fit what you actually need:

1. **Context and audience** — Is this for a class (if so, what level and subject: high school, undergrad political science, a writing course?), a publication, a debate, or personal interest? Who's the reader?

2. **Your own position** — Do you already lean one way (net good, net bad, "it depends")? Or do you want me to propose the most defensible thesis? A 1,500-word essay works best with a clear, arguable claim rather than a balanced survey, so I want to know whether you're comfortable taking a strong stance.

3. **Scope of "democracy"** — Should this focus on a particular country (e.g., the US), be global, or compare cases (e.g., Arab Spring vs. Myanmar vs. Brexit)? And by "democracy," are you most interested in elections and institutions, public discourse/polarization, participation and mobilization, or press freedom?

4. **Scope of "social media"** — All platforms generally, or specific ones (Twitter/X, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube)? Any particular time window (e.g., 2010–present, or the last 5 years)?

5. **Evidence requirements** — Do you need to cite specific sources or studies (academic research, reports, named incidents like Cambridge Analytica, January 6, etc.)? Any required citation style?

6. **Structure constraints** — Any required format (e.g., five-paragraph, must include a counterargument section, must address a specific prompt wording)? Should the outline include rough word counts per section?

7. **Tone** — Formal academic, op-ed style, or conversational?

Answer whichever of these matter to you, and I'll draft the thesis and outline.
