## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Build an AI-powered personal carbon footprint tracker that gamifies sustainable choices."

- **Angle:** individual behaviour change, with consumers as the user.
- **Examples:** scanning receipts or connecting a bank account to estimate emissions, then suggesting greener swaps.
- **Structure:** problem, solution, features list (dashboard, AI tips, leaderboard, badges), tech stack, then demo.
- **Demo:** a polished walkthrough of a mobile UI with fake data, ending on "imagine this at scale."
- **Tone:** startup-pitch upbeat, with impact claims that aren't sized.

Judges see several of these at every climate hackathon. The questions below are meant to move you off it.

## A few questions first

Reply in one line, like `1b 2a 3c 4b`, and add any detail you want.

**1. Who is it for?** The default builds for individual consumers tracking their own footprint.
- a) Individuals, but for one specific action, such as commuting, heating, or food waste
- b) A specific trade or small business, such as restaurants, farms, landlords, or event organisers
- c) A local institution, such as your school, city council, or workplace
- d) Someone else: name them

**2. What real material does your team have?** The default assumes generic app skills and made-up data. Pick what applies, and add one real thing if you can: a problem you've seen up close, a dataset, or a number such as team size or build hours.
- a) Hardware such as sensors, a Raspberry Pi, or Arduino, and someone who can wire it
- b) Strong ML or data skills, plus access to a public dataset (weather, grid carbon intensity, satellite, transit)
- c) Mostly web or mobile developers
- d) A real problem we know first-hand: describe it in a sentence

**3. How ambitious should the build be?** The default is a wide app with many features, mostly mocked.
- a) One narrow tool that works end to end, live, on real data
- b) A bold concept with a convincing mocked demo
- c) A physical prototype people can touch at the demo table

**4. Who's judging, and how should the pitch sound?** The default is an upbeat startup pitch with big impact claims.
- a) Technical judges: plain and precise, showing the live system and the code
- b) Startup or investor judges: crisp, covering who pays, market size, and measurable impact
- c) Mixed or community judges: story-led, built around one person's problem solved on stage
