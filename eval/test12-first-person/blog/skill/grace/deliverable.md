# Why most New Year's resolutions fail (and the one of mine that didn't)

Every January most of us pick something and quit it by February. I don't think that's a character flaw. I think most resolutions are built in a way that makes quitting easy, and the fix is in the design, not the person.

Here's what usually goes wrong.

**1. The goal is a direction, not a thing you do.** "Get fit." "Read more." "Learn a language." You can't do "learn a language" on a Tuesday. Until the resolution becomes a specific action of a specific size, one lesson, one chapter, one walk, there's nothing to actually start, so you don't.

**2. It's sized for January-you.** On the first of the month you're rested and keen, so you plan for someone with an hour a day and no bad weeks. Then February happens. A resolution that only works when you're at your best won't survive the first ordinary week.

**3. Nobody would notice if you stopped.** This is the big one, and it's the one the Welsh thing taught me. Most resolutions are private. You tell no one, or you tell people once and they forget. So when you miss a day, the only person who knows is you, and you are very easy to talk round.

**4. There's no plan for the first slip.** The first missed day feels like the end because the plan never had a line for what happens after a miss. One day off becomes "well, that's over," and the whole thing folds.

## The one that stuck

I've been learning Welsh on an app for 700 days straight. I don't think that's because I'm unusually disciplined. It's because my 12-year-old does it too, and at dinner we check each other's streaks.

Look at that against the list. The action is small and specific: open the app, keep the streak alive. It fits a bad day. And the check isn't something I have to remember to do. It just happens, at a table I'm already sitting at, with someone who will absolutely notice if my number didn't go up. I don't have to supply the willpower. The setup supplies it.

So if you're picking a resolution this year, skip the dramatic one and ask three questions instead. What's the smallest version I'd do on a rough day? Who's going to see whether I did it? And when does that check happen, not "sometime," but a specific spot that's already in your week?

Get those three right and you don't need to be a better person in January. You just need a better setup.
