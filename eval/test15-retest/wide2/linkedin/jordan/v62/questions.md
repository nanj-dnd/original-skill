## What everyone gets

**Claim:** "Management isn't about doing the work anymore. It's about enabling your people."
- **Opening:** "A year ago, I became a manager. Here's what nobody told me 👇"
- **Structure:** a numbered list of 5 to 7 lessons (delegate, listen more than you talk, feedback is a gift, your team's wins are your wins, it's okay to not have all the answers).
- **Examples:** generic or none, such as "I used to jump in and fix things myself."
- **Reader:** aspiring or new managers, in broad inspirational terms.
- **Tone:** humble and polished, ending with "What would you add? 👇"

## A few questions first

Reply all at once, for example `1b 2c 3a 4b`, and add any free text you want.

**1. The default uses generic lessons with no real moment. What real material can it be built on?**
- a) A moment I got wrong as a manager. Tell me what happened and what you did after.
- b) Something a team member said to me that changed how I manage. Give me the line, roughly.
- c) A number from the year, such as team size, hires, someone leaving, or 1:1s held. Give me the figure and what it meant.
- d) Something else. Write it in.

Please also add any facts the post needs: your field or role, team size, and anything to leave out (company name, people's names, topics).

**2. The default is a listicle of 5 to 7 lessons. What shape should it take?**
- a) Keep the list, but make each lesson specific to my year.
- b) One lesson, told through one story.
- c) One belief I held a year ago that turned out to be wrong.
- d) What I'm still bad at. Honest and unfinished, no tidy bow.

**3. The default speaks to aspiring managers in an inspirational tone. Who is this for, and how should it sound?**
- a) Other people promoted from individual contributor roles in my field. Plain and practical.
- b) My own team and colleagues, who will read it. Warm and direct.
- c) My wider network and future employers. Polished and professional.
- d) Casual and conversational, like I'm talking to a friend.

**4. The default's core claim is "it's about enabling people, not doing the work." Where do you stand?**
- a) That's right. Say it well and make it mine.
- b) True, but the hardest part for me was something else. Tell me what: being the expert, being liked, saying no, or something different.
- c) I'm honestly still not sure management suits me, and I'd say that.
- d) Something else. Write it in.

Optional: give a length if you have one. Otherwise I'll aim for about 150 to 250 words, a typical LinkedIn post. Or say "just write it" and I'll go ahead without personal details.
