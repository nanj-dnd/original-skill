# Why Most Resolutions Fail (and Why My Welsh Didn't)

Ask me why most New Year's resolutions fail and I'll give you my honest answer: people lose motivation. Laziness and badly worded goals aren't really the problem. The buzz you feel on January 1st just doesn't last, and it was never going to. Nobody feels in March the way they felt on the first.

That's what I believed, anyway. Then I looked at my phone. I'm on a 700-day streak learning Welsh on an app. Seven hundred days. If motivation were what kept a resolution alive, I'd be the proof, and I'm not sure I am.

[One real day or stretch when you didn't feel like doing your lesson and did it anyway.]

I didn't keep going on those days because I was excited about Welsh mutations. I kept going because my daughter, who's 12, is learning too, and at dinner we check each other's streaks. That's it. That's the whole system. There was no speech and no carefully worded goal. There's just a moment every day when someone I love is going to look at my number, and I'd rather it hadn't gone back to zero.

So I still think resolutions fail because people lose motivation. I just don't think that's the interesting part anymore. Motivation always runs out. The resolutions that fail are the ones with nothing holding them up once it does. Willpower is private: you can quietly let yourself off the hook at ten at night and nobody ever knows. A twelve-year-old across the table asking "what's your streak?" is not private.

If you're making a resolution this year, I'm not going to tell you to make it smaller or more specific, though that won't hurt. My advice is this: before you start, work out what's going to carry it on the day you no longer care.

- **Do it with someone, not just for someone.** My daughter and I are both learning, so neither of us is only the one being checked on. We're both on the hook.
- **Tie the check-in to something that already happens.** Dinner happens every day whether I'm motivated or not.
- **Make progress something you can count.** "What's your streak?" is an easy question to ask over pasta. "How's the Welsh going?" isn't.

You won't stay motivated. Hardly anyone does. Plan for that from the start and you might make it to February, or to day 700.
