Eighteen staff, one rota.

I used to post it a week ahead. Now it goes up three weeks ahead.

Last-minute sick calls went from around six a month to two. Two people who'd been talking about leaving stayed.

I can't prove the rota did all of that. But it's the one thing I'd point to from my first year managing, and it isn't a glamorous lesson.

Going in, you expect the job to be about decisions. Most of it isn't. It's making things predictable, then doing the same unexciting thing again next week so they stay that way.

If someone doesn't know whether they're working on the 28th, they can't book the dentist, swap childcare, or say yes to a weekend away. Some of them will ring in sick that morning instead. Some will quietly start looking elsewhere. None of that shows up as a complaint. It shows up in the numbers.

So, for anyone a few months into managing a team: before you worry about the big calls, check whether people can see when they're working. Then post it again next week. And the week after.
