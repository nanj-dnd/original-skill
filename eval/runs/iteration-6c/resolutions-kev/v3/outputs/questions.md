**Fixed:** about 400 words, a blog post, for your personal blog, on why New Year's resolutions fail. Any statistic in it has to be real and checked.
**Open:** the reason it gives, the angle, what goes in, the structure, and who it's written for.

### What everyone gets

Most resolutions fail because people set them badly: they're too vague and too big, and they run on willpower and January motivation instead of a system. The answer is smaller, specific goals and habits.

- **Thesis:** blame how the goal is set (vague, too ambitious, all-or-nothing), not the person or the idea of resolutions.
- **Evidence:** opens with a "~80% of resolutions fail by February" figure (often repeated, rarely sourced), maybe "Quitter's Day" in mid-January, maybe a nod to *Atomic Habits*.
- **Structure:** stat hook, then 3 to 5 numbered reasons, then a matching fix for each, then an upbeat "you can do it this year" ending.
- **Reader:** an anonymous self-improver about to write this year's list.
- **Voice:** impersonal advice. No "I" and no story.
- **Leaves out:** whether failing even matters, whether January 1 is the problem, and anything you've actually lived.

### A few questions

Answer as many as you like, e.g. `1b 2c 3: <your own words> 4a 5b`.

**1. The default blames how resolutions are *set* (too vague, too big). Where do you put the blame?**
- a) Same: badly set goals
- b) The opposite: the goals are usually fine, and something else kills them (the timing, the life around them, the reason we picked them)
- c) "Failing" is the wrong frame. Most resolutions do their job even when they lapse, or were never meant to last
- d) Your own words

**2. The default is a fix-it listicle: reasons, then tips. What should the post *do*?**
- a) Same: explain and give practical fixes
- b) Make an argument with no tips at all
- c) Tell a story from my own life and let the point come out of it
- d) Your own words

**3. Is there a real resolution of yours behind this post?**
- a) Yes, one I broke (say which, and roughly when or how it ended)
- b) Yes, one that actually stuck (which one, and what was different)
- c) I stopped making them (when, and why)
- d) Not really. Keep it general

**4. The default leans on the "80% fail by February" kind of stat. How do you want evidence handled?**
- a) Use stats and studies (I'll keep only ones I can verify)
- b) No stats. Personal experience and observation only
- c) One checked fact at most, and maybe debunk the famous one
- d) Your own words

**5. The default writes for an anonymous self-improver drafting this year's list. Who reads your blog?**
- a) That's about right
- b) Regular readers and friends who know me
- c) People who've already given up on resolutions, or never make them
- d) Your own words
