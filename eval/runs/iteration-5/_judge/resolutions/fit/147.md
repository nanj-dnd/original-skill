# My Resolutions Kept Dying in February. February Wasn't the Problem.

Three years in a row, I made the same New Year's resolution: gym at 6am. Three years in a row, it was dead by February.

Each time, I decided the problem was me. Not disciplined enough, too fond of my bed. Then I'd buy new gym shoes and try again.

It took the third February before I noticed the date. Every time, the habit died the week my rotation flipped me to nights. 6am stopped being "before work" and became "the middle of my sleep." No amount of grit makes that a good time to deadlift.

Most resolution advice quietly assumes you work 9 to 5. "Same time every day." "Build a morning routine." That's fine if your mornings land in the same place every week. If you're on rotating 12s, or straight nights, or up every three hours with a newborn, that advice was never built for your life. When it fails, that isn't you failing. It's a method breaking on a schedule it was never designed for.

What finally worked was dropping the clock. My rule now: the gym is the first thing I do after any shift ends, day or night. Off at 7:30pm, I go. Off at 7:30am, I go (slowly, with coffee). The habit follows the shift instead of fighting it. I've kept it for 14 months, which is about 13 more than any version of 6am ever managed.

If your resolutions keep dying, here's what I'd try:

**Find the date it broke.** Think about the last habit you dropped. Did it die at a schedule change, like a new rotation, a run of nights or a sick kid? Then the break was predictable, and you can plan for a predictable break.

**Tie it to an event, not a time.** "After I badge out." "After I get home from any shift." "After the baby's first feed." Events come with you when the clock doesn't.

**Build it for your worst week.** If the habit only survives a stretch of day shifts, it won't survive the year. Design it for three nights in a row, and let the easy weeks be a bonus.

**Don't count a hard stretch as quitting.** Missing the gym because you worked four nights isn't failure. Start again after the next shift ends.

You probably don't need more willpower. You need a habit that knows what week it is.
