## The Problem With January 1st

I've made a few New Year's resolutions. I can't tell you what most of them were. That's not a confession, it's the whole point.

The usual explanation for why resolutions fail is that they're badly built. Too vague, too big, no plan. Set smaller goals, track them, stack them onto habits you already have. All true, probably. But it skips over something stranger: why are we all trying to change on the same day?

Every other decision to change comes from somewhere. You start running because your knees ache going up the stairs. You quit something because you catch your reflection and don't like it. You save money because the account hit a number that scared you. The change has a cause, and the cause arrived first.

New Year's Day reverses that. The date shows up and demands a reason. So you go looking for one, and you find something plausible: read more, drink less, call people back. It sounds like a goal. It's really just an answer to the question "well, what are you going to do?"

Nothing in your life has shifted between December 31st and January 1st. Your schedule is the same. The things that made the old habit easy are all still there. The only thing that's different is the calendar, and the calendar has never once stopped anyone from doing anything.

This is why forgetting them is so easy. There's nothing to remember them by. A change you made because of a real moment has that moment attached to it — you can go back to it when you waver. A change you made because it was January has January attached to it, and by the third week that's gone.

I don't think the fix is better resolutions. I think it's noticing that the ones that stick tend to start on unremarkable days, for reasons you didn't choose and couldn't have scheduled. Some Tuesday in March you'll be fed up with something, and that will do more than any list written on a hangover with a pen you had to search for.

So if you've already forgotten what you resolved this year, maybe don't feel bad. You didn't fail at a resolution. You just found out it was never really yours. The real one is coming. It just doesn't know the date yet.
