# Count attempts, not outcomes

Attempt two died at a wedding.

Five weeks in, I was fine. Then: drinks, everyone outside smoking, and that was it. Back on a pack a day inside a week.

By the usual scoring, that's a failed resolution. One attempt, one outcome, file it. Most New Year's resolutions get scored exactly like this, which is why "most resolutions fail" is a statistic everyone nods at and nobody learns anything from.

The frame is wrong. It treats one attempt as the whole experiment.

I smoked for fifteen years. I quit on my fourth serious attempt, and I've been clean for six. Attempts one through three failed. That isn't a 25% success rate. It's one quit that took four tries, which is how quitting smoking usually goes. Most people who get there needed several runs at it. The studies disagree on how many; none of them say one.

Here's what the failed attempts actually were.

Attempt one taught me about the first coffee of the day. Attempt three taught me about driving, and about my brother. Attempt two taught me weddings and alcohol. Each one found a trigger I didn't know I had, or didn't want to know I had. Each one ended at exactly the point where I'd have needed a plan and didn't have one.

Attempt four was planned around those things. Not around willpower, not around a date. Around first coffee, driving, my brother, drinks, weddings. The five things that had already beaten me three times.

It held. Not because I was stronger that year. Because I finally had the information.

That's what "resolutions fail" hides. The failures aren't the opposite of the success. They're the raw material for it. A quit that ends at week five isn't the experiment failing. It's the experiment returning a result: here is where it breaks.

Count outcomes and you get a string of zeros and a story about yourself. Count attempts and you get a list of conditions, and the next attempt starts where the last one stopped.

So, one move. Not a list.

When an attempt ends, write down what broke it. Not "I was weak." The actual conditions: where you were, what you'd had, who was there, what time of day. Then plan the next attempt around exactly that, and nothing else.

The resolution didn't fail. It came back with a finding. Use it.
