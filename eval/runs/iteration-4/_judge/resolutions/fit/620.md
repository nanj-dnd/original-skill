# Most Resolutions Fail Because We're Lazy. Good.

Here's an unfashionable explanation for why most New Year's resolutions don't last: people are lazy. Not as a moral failing, just as a description. Given two options, most of us take the one that costs less effort, most of the time. That isn't a flaw to be fixed by February. It's how a tired brain gets through the day.

The trouble is that the typical resolution is built as if that weren't true. "Go to the gym four times a week" assumes a future you who, at 6 p.m. on a wet Tuesday, will pick the harder option over the sofa, again and again, for a whole year. The plan only works if you stop being the person you've been every other year of your life.

So the resolution doesn't really fail in March. It fails on January 1, the moment it's written to depend on effort you don't reliably have.

The fix isn't to try harder. It's to stop fighting the laziness and put it to work. If you're going to take the easiest path anyway, make the easiest path the one you want.

**Make the good option the default.** Lay out your running clothes the night before. Put the fruit on the counter and the snacks on a high shelf. Defaults win because they ask nothing of you.

**Make the bad option annoying.** Log out of the app you want to use less. Leave the card at home. Every extra step is a small tax, and lazy people avoid taxes.

**Shrink the first move until it's embarrassing.** Not "write a novel" but "open the document." Not "get fit" but "put on your shoes." Once you've started, carrying on is often easier than stopping.

**Decide once, not daily.** Every time you have to choose whether to do the thing, you give laziness another vote. Book the class, set up the automatic transfer to savings, sign up for the vegetable box. A choice made once in January doesn't need willpower in July.

**Plan for the skipped day.** You will miss days. A lazy person's plan has to survive one without collapsing, so the rule is "never miss twice," not "never miss."

None of this requires becoming a more disciplined person. That's the point. The resolutions that last tend to be the ones that would still happen on your worst, laziest day.

So this year, don't resolve to change who you are. Resolve to rearrange things so the person you already are ends up doing the right thing anyway.
