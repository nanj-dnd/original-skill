Happy to help. A few questions so the thesis and outline fit what you actually need:

1. **Context and audience** — Is this for a class (if so, what level and subject: high school, college intro, political science, media studies?), a publication, or personal writing? Who's the reader?

2. **Position** — Do you already lean one way (good, bad, or "it's complicated")? Or do you want me to propose the most defensible position? Note that for 1,500 words, a clear stance with a strong counterargument usually works better than a pure "both sides" survey.

3. **Scope** — Any particular geography or time frame? (e.g., US-focused, global; 2010s Arab Spring through present; recent elections; the AI-generated content era.) Any specific platforms you want to emphasize or avoid?

4. **Definition of "democracy"** — Are you thinking primarily about elections and voting, public deliberation and information quality, civic participation and movements, institutional trust, or some combination? This shapes the thesis a lot.

5. **Evidence requirements** — Do you need citations to academic research or specific cases (e.g., Cambridge Analytica, Arab Spring, Myanmar, January 6, EU DSA regulation)? Any required sources or a minimum number?

6. **Format** — Do you have a required structure (e.g., five-paragraph, intro–body–counterargument–conclusion), or should I just use what works best for 1,500 words? Do you want the outline as bullet points with topic sentences, or more detailed with evidence per section?

7. **Tone** — Formal academic, op-ed style, or conversational?

Answer whatever's relevant and I'll draft the thesis and outline.
