**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because people set vague, oversized goals with no system behind them — motivation fades by February, so build habits, start small, track progress.

The choices a pasted prompt makes:
- **Angle:** it's a planning/willpower problem, fixable with better technique.
- **Examples:** the gym-in-January crowd, "lose weight" vs. "walk 20 minutes daily."
- **Structure:** hook, 3–4 numbered reasons, upbeat "here's what to do instead" close.
- **Reader:** a generic self-improver who wants tips.
- **Tone:** cheerful coach, light on the writer's own life.

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`):

1. The default says resolutions fail for *mechanical* reasons (vague goals, no system). What's your actual read?
   - a) Agree — it's mostly a how-you-set-them problem
   - b) The date is the problem — January 1st is an arbitrary, badly timed trigger
   - c) People resolve things they don't actually want; the goal is borrowed from someone else
   - d) Most "failures" aren't failures — people drop goals that stopped mattering, and that's fine

2. The default structure is a numbered list of reasons plus tips. For your blog:
   - a) Keep it — list of reasons, practical close
   - b) One idea argued through, no list
   - c) Personal essay built around one story

3. Your real material: have you made a resolution that failed (or stuck)? Give me the resolution, roughly what happened, and any number if there is one (how many weeks it lasted, how many years you tried it, etc.). If nothing comes to mind, say "none" and I'll write around it.

4. How should it sound on your blog?
   - a) Plain, first-person, like talking to a friend
   - b) Wry / a bit self-deprecating
   - c) Reflective, slower, more essay-like
   - d) Crisp and practical, minimal "I"
