**Why New Year's resolutions fail (it's the date)**

I've made a few resolutions over the years. I couldn't tell you what they were. None of them crashed and burned in any way worth a story; they just stopped being a thing I thought about, usually without my noticing when.

For a while I assumed that was a me problem. Then I looked at when we're all supposed to start.

It's the first week of January. The days are at their shortest. You've just spent two weeks eating and sitting and staying up late, and your sleep is wrecked. Your bank account is recovering. The house is still half-full of decorations and leftovers. Everyone you work with is coming back groggy and behind, so the week is a scramble. And into this, of all weeks, you're supposed to drop a brand-new version of yourself and keep it running.

Nobody would pick that moment on purpose. If a friend said "I want to overhaul my habits, when's good?" you would not say "right after the holidays, in the dark, while you're tired and skint." You'd say spring, or after that work thing calms down, or whenever you actually feel like it.

But the resolution isn't started when it makes sense. It's started because the number on the calendar rolled over. The date chose you. And things that start because of the calendar tend to end for the same reason: nothing inside you was holding them up, so when the calendar stops caring, you stop too.

That's the part I find oddly comforting. The forgettable resolutions weren't evidence of some weakness. They were just things I'd agreed to on a schedule somebody else set, at a time no sensible person would choose, and then my actual life came back and quietly took the slot.

I'm not going to tell you what to do about it. I don't think there's a trick. Mostly I've stopped expecting the first of January to mean anything, and that's been fine. The things I've actually changed in my life started whenever they started, for reasons that had nothing to do with fireworks.

If you made one this year and it's already gone, I'd put it down to the date before you put it down to yourself.
