**Fixed vs open.** Fixed: about 400 words, a blog post, the topic (why resolutions fail), and any statistic we cite, which has to be checked (the popular "80% fail by February" figure has weak sourcing). Open: the claim, the angle, the examples, the structure and who it's written for.

## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** Resolutions fail because they're vague, too ambitious and run on willpower. Make them specific and small, build habits or systems instead, and you'll succeed this year.

- **Thesis:** failure is a *method* problem with a fix: SMART goals, tiny habits, "systems not goals."
- **Stance on failure:** failing is bad, and the post exists to help you avoid it.
- **Evidence:** a scary statistic up top ("80% quit by February", Strava's "Quitter's Day") plus a nod to James Clear or BJ Fogg.
- **Structure:** stat hook → 3 to 5 numbered reasons → tips → upbeat "you've got this" close.
- **Reader:** a stranger who wants to do better this January and is looking for advice.
- **Leaves out:** the writer. There's no real resolution and no real person in it, and it never asks whether the goal was worth wanting.

## A few questions

Reply like `1b 2a 3: <own words>`. Pick any letter or write your own.

**1. The cause.** The default says resolutions fail because of *method* (too vague, too big, too reliant on willpower). What do you think?
- a) Yes, it's mostly method.
- b) The opposite: method is fine. People quit because they never really wanted the thing. It was a borrowed or "should" goal.
- c) They don't fail as often as we say. We count anything short of perfect as failure, and the partial change gets ignored.
- d) Your own words.

**2. Is failing bad?** The default treats a dropped resolution as a problem to fix with tips.
- a) Yes, help people stick with it.
- b) No. Dropping one is often the right call, or tells you something useful.
- c) Skip the whole ritual. January 1 is the problem.
- d) Your own words.

**3. A real case.** Is there a specific resolution behind this post, from you or someone close?
- a) One I made and dropped (say which, roughly when, and what happened).
- b) One that actually stuck (say what was different).
- c) Someone else's (a partner, friend, parent…).
- d) No, keep it general.

**4. Shape and reader.** The default is a numbered list of reasons plus tips, written for a stranger looking for advice.
- a) Keep the list-and-tips format.
- b) A personal essay: one story, one point, no tips.
- c) An argument: one claim, pushed hard, written for readers who'd disagree.
- d) Your own words.

**5. Numbers.** The default opens with a failure statistic.
- a) Use a stat, but only one that holds up.
- b) No stats. It's a personal blog.
- c) Go after the stat itself (where "80%" comes from, or how "failure" gets measured).
