# The Resolution That Lasted Has a Witness

Most nights at dinner, my 12-year-old daughter and I check our streaks. We both learn Welsh on an app. I'm on day 700. Neither of us wants to be the one who breaks it, and that, more than anything else, is why the streak is still alive.

I think most New Year's resolutions fail for a boring reason: people lose motivation. That's about it. There's no hidden flaw in the goal and no goal-setting technique they missed. On January 1st you want the thing. By late January you want it a bit less. By March you want it about as much as you did in November, which is to say not enough to do anything about it.

What's interesting is that this is true of everything, including the things that stick. Nobody is motivated to do a language lesson 700 days running. The people who keep going don't feel more than the people who stop. At some point they stop needing to feel anything, because something else is carrying the habit.

In my case, that something is a person. The lesson itself is a few minutes. The part that holds it in place is that someone will ask about it at dinner, every day, and she has a streak of her own to protect, so the asking goes both ways. It's a small, mild, daily pressure. Nothing dramatic. Nobody gets told off. But you'd have to explain why you let it go, out loud, to a twelve-year-old, and that turns out to be enough.

So here's the alternative to willpower. Before you make a resolution, find your witness. Not a coach, not an app that sends you notifications you'll learn to swipe away. A person you already see, ideally one who is trying to keep something going too, so you're checking on each other rather than being supervised. Then put the check somewhere you'll both already be: a meal, a commute, the school run. If it needs its own slot in the week, it'll be the first thing to go.

Motivation is real, and it's great for getting you to January 1st. It just doesn't last, and it was never going to. The question for any resolution isn't "how badly do I want this?" It's "who notices on the day I stop wanting it?"
