**What everyone gets**

Main claim: "AI assistants are great for boilerplate and tests, risky for core logic — use them, but review everything." The balanced-middle take.

Choices a pasted prompt makes:
- Angle: "it depends / use judgement," offending no one
- Examples: generic ("it wrote a bug once," "it saved me an hour on tests")
- Structure: pros, cons, three guidelines, "let's find the balance"
- Reader: a general audience, not your company's engineers specifically
- Tone: TED-lite, upbeat, a quip about robots taking jobs

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

**1. The default argues "use it, but review everything." What do you actually think?**
a) Yes, that's roughly my view — balanced
b) Lean hard in: most engineers are under-using it and should be pushed to use it for far more
c) Lean skeptical: "for everything" is the problem; it's eroding understanding of our own codebase
d) Something else — tell me in a sentence

**2. The default uses generic examples. What's a real moment from your team I can build on?**
a) A specific win (a feature, migration, or bug AI helped ship faster) — give me a line on it
b) A specific failure (a bug or incident where AI-written code caused trouble) — give me a line
c) Both, briefly
d) I'd rather keep it general

Also: any number you'd trust? (% of PRs with AI involvement, hours saved, review time up/down, anything.)

**3. The default aims at "engineers in general." Who's actually in the room, and what do you want them to do on Monday?**
a) Mostly engineers; I want them to change a habit (e.g., try it on something they'd never use it for, or stop using it for something)
b) Mixed company, including non-engineers and leadership; I want them to understand where we stand and why
c) Leadership specifically; I'm making a case for a policy, budget, or norm

**4. The default is upbeat conference-talk tone. How should this sound coming from you?**
a) Plain and direct, like I'd talk to my team
b) Measured and professional — this is on the record
c) A bit provocative; I want people to argue with me afterward

And one quick thing the questions don't cover: what's your role? (IC, lead, manager, exec) — it changes how much you can prescribe versus suggest.
