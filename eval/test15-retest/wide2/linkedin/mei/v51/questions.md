## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Management isn't about doing the work, it's about enabling others. The hardest part was letting go."
- **Angle:** the IC-to-manager identity shift ("my job is now my team's success")
- **Examples:** stopped fixing things myself, learned to delegate, 1:1s matter, feedback is a gift
- **Structure:** hook line, then a numbered list of 5 to 7 lessons, then "What would you add?"
- **Reader:** other new or aspiring managers
- **Tone:** humble, upbeat and grateful, ending with a thank-you to the team

---

A few questions before I write. Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`.

**1. What's the core lesson?** The default is "letting go of doing the work myself."
- a) Something I got wrong and had to undo (a decision, a habit, a call I made)
- b) Something nobody warned me about (the loneliness, the politics, how slow the feedback loop is)
- c) Something I changed my mind on (I believed X going in, now I believe Y)
- d) Letting go is right for me, but I want it told through my own story

**2. Give me one real moment the post can be built on.** The default uses no specific moment, just general lessons. Reply in a sentence or two with any of these:
- a) A conversation: something someone on your team, or your boss, actually said to you
- b) A mistake: what happened and what you did next
- c) A number: team size, people hired or lost, attrition, a metric that moved, how many 1:1s you held
- d) A before and after: how you used to handle something versus how you handle it now

**3. What shape?** The default is a numbered list of 5 to 7 lessons.
- a) One story, one lesson, no list
- b) Three lessons, each tied to a real moment
- c) A short "what I thought vs. what's true" contrast

**4. How should it sound?** The default is humble and upbeat with a thank-you at the end.
- a) Plain and direct, no motivational language
- b) Candid, a bit raw: admits what was hard
- c) Warm, crediting the team, but specific rather than gushing
- d) Dry, lightly funny

Also useful if you have them: your field or role, and roughly how long you want it (short, 100 to 150 words, or longer, 250 to 300).
