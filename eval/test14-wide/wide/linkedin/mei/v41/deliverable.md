Eighteen people, one rota, and the main thing I learned in my first year as a manager.

I used to post the rota a week ahead. Now I post it three weeks ahead.

That's the whole change.

After it, sick calls went from about six a month to about two. Two people who were thinking of leaving stayed.

I'm not going to claim a rota alone keeps anyone in a job. But think about what a week's notice does. You can't book a dentist. You can't say yes to a wedding. You can't sort childcare without hoping it lines up. Three weeks means you can plan a life around the job instead of squeezing the job into a life you can't plan.

Most of what gets written about management is about the big stuff: vision, feedback, difficult conversations. Some of that matters. But with eighteen people, what mattered most this year was whether the day-to-day ran properly. Whether people knew when they were working, far enough ahead to actually live.

Nobody gets praised for a rota. It doesn't look like leadership from the outside. It turned out to be most of the job.

If you manage a team and you're wondering where to start, start with the boring thing everyone depends on.
