**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because they're vague, too ambitious, and rely on willpower instead of systems — so set smaller, specific habits instead.

The choices a pasted prompt makes:
- **Angle**: it's a design problem (bad goals), not a motivation problem
- **Examples**: gym memberships emptying out by February; "lose weight" vs "walk 20 minutes daily"
- **Structure**: hook about January crowds → three reasons → "do this instead" close
- **Reader**: generic self-improver, assumed to have made and broken resolutions
- **Tone**: warm, slightly knowing, light on personal detail

**Questions** (reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`):

1. The default says resolutions fail because they're *badly designed* — vague, too big, no system. Which claim do you actually want to make?
   - a) Keep it: design/habit problem, fixable with smaller goals
   - b) They fail because January is an arbitrary date — the calendar isn't why you want to change
   - c) They fail because people pick goals they're *supposed* to want, not ones they actually want
   - d) Most of them *should* fail — abandoning a resolution is often the right call

2. Your real material: a resolution you made and dropped (or kept), and roughly when it died or what made it stick.
   - a) I have one — I'll describe it in a line
   - b) Use a composite "most people" example instead, no personal story
   - c) I kept one once; build from what was different that time

3. The default closes with "do this instead" advice. How should it end?
   - a) Practical advice, 2–3 concrete steps
   - b) An observation, no advice — leave the reader thinking
   - c) A single suggestion, not a list

4. Tone for a personal blog with your name on it:
   - a) Plain and conversational, first person throughout
   - b) Essayistic — more reflective, fewer "you" statements
   - c) Dry/wry, a bit skeptical of the whole genre
