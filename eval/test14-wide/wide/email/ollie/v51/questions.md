**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt gives you: a short email that opens with "I noticed [Company] runs a sizeable fleet," claims "we help logistics companies cut fuel costs by 15–20% and miles driven by 10%," names a vague client win ("a similar-sized carrier saved $X last year"), and closes with "Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week?" Its choices:

- **Angle**: cost savings, led by percentage claims.
- **Example**: an anonymised "similar company" result.
- **Structure**: observation about them → our claim → proof → call ask.
- **Reader**: a generic ops head who cares about fuel and miles.
- **Tone**: polished SaaS-sales, slightly warm, borrowed urgency.

Four questions, answer in one go (e.g. `1b 2a 3c 4b`):

**1. The default leads with fuel/mileage savings. What's the pain you actually solve best for a mid-size ops head?**
a) Fuel and miles (keep the default)
b) Dispatcher time: planning routes by hand each morning
c) Missed delivery windows and the customer calls that follow
d) Driver overtime and fleet utilisation

**2. Real material. The default uses a vague "similar carrier saved X." What do you actually have?**
a) A named customer and a specific number (tell me both)
b) A specific number but the customer can't be named (tell me the number and roughly who)
c) A concrete thing about *this* prospect you noticed (their fleet size, a region they just expanded to, a job post for dispatchers, etc.)
d) Nothing yet; write around it

**3. The default ends with "15-minute call next week." What are you really asking for?**
a) A short call
b) Permission to send a one-page analysis of their routes (you'd do a small free sample)
c) A reply to one yes/no question
d) An intro to whoever actually owns dispatch

**4. Whose name is on it, and how should it sound?**
a) Founder writing personally; plain, short, no sales polish
b) Sales rep; professional but direct
c) Plain and a bit blunt: assume they get 20 of these a week and say so
d) Formal; this is a conservative, older-school company
