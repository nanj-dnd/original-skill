**One year ago I became a manager. Here's what I got wrong, and what I'd tell myself on day one.**

I thought the job was "the same work, plus people." It isn't. It's a different job entirely, and most of what made me good at the old one got in the way at first.

A few things I learned the hard way:

**1. Your output is no longer the work. It's the team.**
For months I kept jumping in to fix things myself because it was faster. It was — in the short term. In the long term I was the bottleneck, and my team wasn't learning anything except that I didn't trust them. [Example: the time you took back a project vs. the time you let someone own it.]

**2. "I'll just give them space" is sometimes a way of avoiding a hard conversation.**
I confused being hands-off with being respectful. People actually want to know where they stand. The feedback I was most nervous to give turned out to be the feedback people thanked me for.

**3. 1:1s are theirs, not yours.**
Early on, mine were status updates. Once I stopped asking "what's the update on X?" and started asking "what's getting in your way?" the meetings got shorter and more useful.

**4. You have to repeat yourself far more than feels reasonable.**
Context that lives in your head doesn't exist for anyone else. If I said something once in a meeting, I'd assumed everyone knew it. They didn't. Say the priorities out loud, again, until you're bored of hearing yourself.

**5. The hardest part isn't the people problems. It's that you stop getting the feedback loop that told you you were doing well.**
Nobody ships "good management." You go home some days with no idea whether you helped. Learning to be okay with that was the biggest adjustment.

I'm still early. But if you're about to make this move: the job is less about having answers and more about making sure the right people are in a position to find them.
