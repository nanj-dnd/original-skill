## Angle

- **Angle**: Being liked and being a good manager aren't the same thing. You can't tell the difference without a regular, written way of giving feedback.
- **Built from**: 1a (one lesson), 2a (the month-4 exit interview and the weekly 1:1s you started afterwards), 3a (new managers), 4a (plain).
- **Strongest objection**: One person's exit interview is a single data point. A reader could fairly say she left for several reasons and the feedback comment was the easiest one to name. **Test**: before posting, ask two people on your team whether the weekly "one thing to change" is useful to them. If they hesitate, soften the claim about what the 1:1s fix.
- **Other direction**: Write it as a myth you held ("no complaints means it's going well") and how that belief broke.

---

In month four, one of my engineers quit.

In her exit interview, she told me she'd never once had clear feedback on her work.

I'd thought things were fine.

That gap is the most useful thing I learned this year. Being liked and being a good manager aren't the same thing.

A friendly working relationship can sit right on top of someone having no idea where they stand. When nobody complains, it's easy to read that as things going well. Sometimes it just means nobody has been told anything they could react to.

So I started weekly 1:1s with a written agenda and one thing to change.

The written part matters. "Good work" is easy to say and hard to act on. A named thing to change is something you can both come back to the following week. With one on the page, it's much harder for either person to leave the room assuming everything's fine.

If you manage people, don't wait for an exit interview to find out what they needed from you.

---

**Before you use it**
- Former colleagues may recognise the engineer from "month four." Make sure you're comfortable with that, or loosen the timing to "early in my first year."
- If the 1:1s have had a visible effect since you started them, such as something a report said or a change you saw, one line about it would make the ending stronger. I left it out because you didn't mention one.

**Distance from the default**: The numbered listicle, headline hook, team thank-you, closing question and hashtags are gone. In their place is one story told straight, which came from 1a and 2a.
