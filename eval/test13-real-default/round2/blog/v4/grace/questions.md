**What everyone gets**

Main claim: resolutions fail because they're vague, too ambitious, and driven by a calendar date rather than real motivation.

- Angle: the problem is the goal-setting method, not the person
- Examples: "lose weight", "go to the gym", "read more", the gym emptying by February
- Structure: hook about January crowds → 3–4 numbered reasons → "set small, specific habits instead" close
- Reader: a generic self-improvement audience
- Tone: friendly, lightly motivational, ends on an upbeat fix

Four questions, answer like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

1. **Angle.** The default says resolutions fail because of how they're set (vague, too big). Where do you actually land?
   a. That's right, the method is the problem
   b. The date is the problem: January 1st is arbitrary and nothing about your life has changed
   c. They don't really fail; people quietly outgrow them, and that's fine
   d. Most resolutions are about who people think they should be, not what they want

2. **Your own moment.** It's your blog, in your voice, so one real thing beats any gym statistic. Which can you give me?
   a. A resolution you made that failed, and roughly when it fell apart
   b. One that actually stuck, and what was different about it
   c. A line someone said to you about resolutions (a friend, family member)
   d. Nothing specific; write it in general terms

   (Add a sentence of detail if you pick a, b or c: what it was, how long it lasted, what happened.)

3. **Ending.** The default closes with "set smaller, specific habits instead."
   a. Give a practical alternative, like the default
   b. Make the case for not making resolutions at all
   c. End on the observation, no fix offered
   d. Question back to the reader

4. **Sound.** Default is friendly and lightly motivational.
   a. Plain and dry, a bit wry
   b. Personal and reflective
   c. Direct and a little blunt
   d. Friendly, like the default
