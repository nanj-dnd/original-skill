# Everyone loses motivation. That's not why resolutions fail.

Ask anyone why their New Year's resolution didn't last and they'll tell you they lost motivation. I'd tell you the same. The January feeling wears off, and the running shoes go back under the stairs.

I just don't think losing motivation is the problem. It's the one part of any resolution you can count on.

I've got a 700-day streak learning Welsh on an app. That isn't because I'm unusually motivated. I don't think anyone stays keen on anything for 700 days straight. What's kept it going is that my daughter, who's 12, is learning too, and most evenings at dinner we check each other's streaks.

There's no chart and no system. It's just a question that gets asked across the table: have you done your Welsh? And because it gets asked, it gets done.

That's the difference I keep coming back to. Most resolutions live entirely in one person's head. They run on how that person feels on a wet Tuesday in late January, and when the feeling goes there's nothing else holding them up. Nobody asks. Nobody notices when you stop. A resolution like that doesn't fail when you lose motivation; it was always going to fail then, because motivation was the only thing it had.

So if you're making one this year, I'd spend less time getting the goal exactly right and more time on a different question: who's going to ask?

A few things that seem to matter, going by our dinner table:

**Pick someone who's doing it too.** It works because the checking goes both ways. My daughter isn't my coach and I'm not hers. We're in it together, and it's much harder to let your own streak slide when someone across the table is keeping theirs going.

**Hang the asking on something that already happens.** There's no separate check-in to remember. We eat dinner anyway, so the question rides along for free.

**Keep it small enough to answer yes.** A daily lesson is a low bar, and that's the point. The question only works if the honest answer is usually yes.

You'll lose motivation. Everyone does. Plan for the day it goes, and make sure someone will notice.

And if anyone reading this wants a person to ask them in February, you know where to find me.
