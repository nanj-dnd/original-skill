Most advice for new managers is written for a dramatic year. The difficult report. The resignation. The moment you realise your work is no longer the work.

My first year as a manager was mostly fine. Nobody warns you that this is the likely outcome, and it leaves you with an odd problem: what do you learn from a year where nothing went badly wrong?

The honest answer is something small.

Ask people whose teams run quietly what they do, and the answers are dull. A note sent after the meeting. The same question asked every week. A priority repeated long after it feels necessary. None of it would make a talk. All of it is what stops two people leaving the same room with different versions of it, or a commitment nobody can locate three weeks later.

Those are the things that turn a fine year into a hard one. They are cheap to prevent and expensive to repair, and preventing them is mostly routine.

The dramatic lessons may come later. The useful one from a calm year is that calm is not the absence of work. It is the result of the boring kind.
