Here are the questions that would most shape the thesis and outline:

1. **Context and audience:** Is this for a class (which level and subject, e.g. high school civics, an undergrad poli-sci or comm course), a publication, or personal use? Did your instructor give a prompt or rubric?

2. **Your position:** Do you already lean one way (good, bad, or mixed/conditional)? Or should I choose the most defensible position?

3. **Scope:** Which democracy or democracies? For example, the U.S. only, Western democracies, or a global view including cases like the Arab Spring, India, or Brazil. And over what timeframe?

4. **Definition of "democracy":** Should the essay focus on a particular part of it, such as elections and voting, public discourse and deliberation, protest and mobilization, trust in institutions, or press and accountability? Or should it cover them broadly?

5. **Essay type:** Argumentative, with a firm thesis and rebuttals? Or more analytical/exploratory, weighing both sides?

6. **Evidence expectations:** Do you need scholarly sources (e.g. research on polarization, misinformation, filter bubbles), or is general reasoning with well-known examples enough? Should the outline suggest specific sources?

7. **Outline format:** Do you want a bare skeleton (section headings plus key points), or a detailed outline with topic sentences, evidence for each paragraph, and approximate word counts per section?

8. **Anything to include or avoid:** Are there required readings, angles you've already covered in class, or a counterargument you specifically want addressed?

Answer whichever ones you can. If you don't have a preference, I'll make a sensible choice and tell you what I picked.
