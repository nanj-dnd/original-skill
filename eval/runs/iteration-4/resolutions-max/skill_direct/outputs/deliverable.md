# January 1 Is a Terrible Day to Start Anything

I've made New Year's resolutions. I couldn't tell you what most of them were. They didn't fail, exactly; nothing that dramatic. I just forgot them somewhere in the first few weeks, the way you forget a password you only typed once.

The usual explanation is that resolutions die because they're too vague or too ambitious. That's partly true. But I think the bigger problem is the date.

Think about the conditions under which a resolution gets made. It's late. You've been eating cheese for a week. Someone asks what yours is, and you need an answer before the countdown. The goal isn't coming from anything in your actual life. It's coming from the calendar. The calendar said "now," so you picked something.

Then January 2 arrives, and it's a normal day, only darker and with more email. Nothing about it is different from December 28, except that you've now promised yourself something on top of everything else. The mood that made the promise has gone home, and the person who has to keep it never agreed to it.

That's the trap. A resolution made because it's January has no particular reason to survive into February. The changes that do stick tend to start because something happened: a bad week, a doctor's comment, a moment where you noticed a thing and actually minded. The reason comes first and the start date follows. New Year's runs that backwards.

So if you're going to make one anyway (I'm not going to stop you), here are a few ways to work with the date instead of against it:

**Wait a week.** Write it down on New Year's Eve, then look at it again on January 8. If it still seems worth doing once the glow wears off, start then. If you've already forgotten it, you've learned something cheaply.

**Give it a reason, not a date.** "Because it's a new year" isn't a reason. "Because I get winded on the stairs" is. If you can't finish the sentence, pick a different resolution.

**Make it hard to lose.** A lot of resolutions aren't abandoned so much as misplaced. Put yours somewhere you'll trip over it: a reminder for mid-January, a note on the fridge, a friend who'll ask.

**Let yourself start on another day.** Your birthday, the first warm weekend, a random Tuesday in March. Any day you actually feel like beginning beats the one everyone else picked for you.

If I make one this year, I'll probably forget it too. But I'm setting a reminder for the 8th, just to see.
