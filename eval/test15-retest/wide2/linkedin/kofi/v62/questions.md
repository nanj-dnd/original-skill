## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Managing isn't about doing the work, it's about helping others do theirs."
- **Angle:** the shift from individual contributor to leader, framed as humbling growth
- **Examples:** generic lessons like "listen more than you talk," "1:1s matter," "delegate," "feedback is a gift," "you can't please everyone"
- **Structure:** hook line ("A year ago I became a manager. Here's what I learned 👇"), then a numbered list of 5 to 7 lessons, then a thank-you to the team
- **Reader:** other new or aspiring managers
- **Tone:** earnest, inspirational, short punchy lines, ending with "What would you add?"

## A few questions first

Reply like `1b 2a 3c 4a`, or add your own text after any number.

**1. The default is a list of 5 to 7 universal lessons. What should the shape be?**
- a) Keep the list, but every lesson comes from something that actually happened to you
- b) One lesson only, told through one story, in depth
- c) What you got wrong: what you believed on day one, and what changed your mind
- d) Something else (tell me)

**2. The default is generic advice. Give me one real moment from this year (this matters most).**
- a) A conversation that changed how you manage, and roughly what was said
- b) A mistake you made and what it cost (a project, a person, a deadline)
- c) A number that shows the change: team size, attrition, a delivery metric, hours you got back
- d) Write it in your own words: what happened, who was involved (no names needed), and any line someone said

**3. The default sounds earnest and inspirational, with short punchy lines and emojis. How should it sound for your network?**
- a) Plain and honest, like telling a peer over coffee, no emojis
- b) Professional and measured, for recruiters and senior leaders
- c) Warm and a little self-deprecating
- d) Keep the classic LinkedIn style

**4. Details only you know.**
- a) Just give me your role, field and team size, and I'll keep everything else general
- b) Also tag or thank the team (tell me how to refer to them)
- c) Add a limit: length (for example under 150 or around 300 words), things to avoid (naming the company, mentioning layoffs, hashtags)
- d) Type anything else: who you were before (engineer, analyst, etc.), whether you were promoted from within the team
