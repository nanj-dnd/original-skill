## Outline (5 minutes)

**1. Open — the question is the wrong shape (0:00–0:45)**
"Should we use AI for everything?" invites a yes/no, and nobody actually believes either. Reframe: the real question is *which things*, and that answer lives in the room, not on the slide.

**2. Where I honestly am (0:45–1:30)**
You use Copilot sometimes, it's fine, you haven't had a disaster or a miracle. Say that plainly — it's probably the median experience and it gives everyone else permission to be unimpressed and un-alarmed. Your rough rule: use it for some stuff, be careful. The talk is about what "some stuff" and "careful" mean in practice.

**3. The two sorting questions (1:30–3:00)**
Offer a lightweight way to sort tasks, framed as a proposal rather than policy:
- *Can I check the output faster than I could write it?* Boilerplate, tests for code I already understand, a regex, a migration — yes. A concurrency fix, an auth change — no, checking it is the hard part.
- *Will I have to explain this in six months?* If the answer is yes and I can't explain it now, I shouldn't be merging it now.
Make the point that "be careful" isn't a vibe, it's those two questions.

**4. The ask — compare notes (3:00–4:15)**
The concrete action. Propose one of:
- A short thread or doc: everyone posts one task where AI saved real time and one where it cost them time. Twenty entries would tell the team more than any blog post.
- Or: next retro, five minutes on the same two questions.
Set a date so it isn't vapor.

**5. Close (4:15–5:00)**
Circle back: you're not telling anyone to use it more or less. You're saying the team's actual answer is sitting in forty people's heads and it's worth ten minutes to collect it. Land on the two questions as the thing to remember.

## Opening paragraph

So the question I was given is whether engineers should be using AI coding assistants for everything. And I want to be upfront: I don't think that question has an answer, because nobody in this room actually believes either side of it. Nobody thinks you should paste a prompt and merge whatever comes back, and nobody thinks we should be typing out boilerplate by hand to prove a point. For what it's worth, I use Copilot sometimes, and it's fine. That's it — not life-changing, not dangerous, fine. Which I suspect is where most of you are, and it's a weird place to give a speech from. So instead of telling you where the line is, I want to spend five minutes on a smaller question that I think we can actually answer, together, and that question is: *which things?*
