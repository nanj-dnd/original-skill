## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Our software cuts your fuel costs and delivery times. Can we talk?"

- **Opening:** a pain question or industry stat ("Rising fuel costs eating into margins?").
- **Body:** a feature list (real-time traffic, dynamic rerouting, AI-powered optimisation).
- **Proof:** a vague result ("companies like yours see 15–20% savings").
- **Ask:** "Do you have 15 minutes next week?"
- **Tone and reader:** polished vendor pitch to a generic ops head, with the prospect's own business barely mentioned.

## A few questions first

Reply with something like `1b 2a 3c 4a`, and add free text wherever it's useful.

**1. What real proof do you have?** The default uses a vague "15–20% savings" line.
- a) A customer result with a number. Type it, e.g. "regional 3PL, 80 vans, cut route miles 12% in 8 weeks", and say whether you can name them.
- b) Pilot or trial numbers only. Type them.
- c) No results yet. Lead with something specific about this prospect instead, and type what you know about them.
- d) Other. Type it.

**2. Which pain should it open on?** The default leads with fuel and cost.
- a) Fuel and cost, done well.
- b) Driver hours, overtime and retention.
- c) Missed delivery windows and failed drops.
- d) Planner time: the dispatcher rebuilding routes by hand every morning.

**3. What's the ask?** The default asks for a 15-minute call.
- a) A short call.
- b) Run last week's routes through your software for free and show them the difference.
- c) A short video or one-pager they can look at in their own time.
- d) Other. Type it.

**4. How should it sound, and what must it include?** The default is a polished vendor pitch.
- a) Plain, peer to peer, as if a founder is writing.
- b) Professional and formal, from a sales rep.
- c) Warm and casual.

Also add your name and role, the product and company name, the prospect's name and company if you have them, and anything to avoid or any length limit.
