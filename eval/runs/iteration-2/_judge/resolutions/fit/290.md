# Your Resolution Wasn't Built for Your Week

Three years in a row, I resolved to hit the gym at 6am. Three years in a row, it was dead by February.

You've heard that February is when resolutions die, usually offered as proof that willpower runs out. Mine didn't run out. My rotation flipped me to nights, and 6am stopped being the start of my day and became the end of a 12-hour shift.

Most resolution advice quietly assumes you work 9 to 5. Same time every day. Build a morning routine. It's all built on a clock, and the clock is the one thing a lot of us don't control. If you're on rotating 12s, doing nights in a warehouse, or up with a newborn, "same time every day" isn't a discipline test. It describes a life you don't live.

Not every broken resolution is the schedule's fault. But when the plan only works on a week you never get, the habit dies, and you blame yourself for it. A lot of resolutions fail because the method was never built for your life in the first place.

What finally worked for me was dropping the clock entirely. The new rule: the gym is the first thing I do after any shift ends, day or night. Off a day shift in the evening? Gym. Off nights in the morning? Gym, then bed. No clock involved. I've kept it up for 14 months, which is about 13 months longer than any 6am version lasted.

Psychologists call this an "if-then" plan: when X happens, I do Y. The trick for anyone whose schedule won't hold still is picking an X that happens no matter what the rotation does.

**Tie it to an event, not an hour.** Shifts move. "My shift ended" always comes.

**Pick an event both kinds of day share.** Clocking out, the first coffee after you wake up, getting home. If it only happens on day shifts, it's a clock in disguise.

**Plan for your worst stretch, not your best week.** Build around the third night in a row. If it only works on your days off, it doesn't work.

**Treat the flip as a test.** If the habit needs renegotiating every time the rotation changes, it was tied to the wrong thing.

So if this year's resolution is already gone, don't ask whether you're disciplined enough. Ask what it's attached to. If the answer is a time on a clock, and your clock keeps changing, you already know how it ends.

Attach it to the end of your shift instead.
