## What everyone gets

**Main claim:** "Build a personal carbon footprint tracker that turns everyday choices into visible emissions and nudges people to cut them."

- **Angle:** individual consumer behaviour, where guilt and gamification are expected to drive change.
- **Examples:** scanning receipts or bank transactions with an LLM, food and commute emissions, and a leaderboard with streaks.
- **Build:** React or Next.js front end, an LLM API, and a public emissions-factor dataset.
- **Demo:** sign up, log a burger and a car trip, watch a CO₂ number appear, show a "swap this for that" tip.
- **Reader and tone:** generic judges, upbeat pitch language ("empower users to make sustainable choices").

Most climate hackathons get several of these. Before I write yours, answer these four questions. You can reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b` and add notes.

**1. Who is it for?** The default targets individual consumers changing their own habits.
- a) Keep it consumer-facing, just sharper.
- b) A specific operator: a small business, farm, landlord, school or city office making one costly decision.
- c) A community or local group: neighbourhood, campus, tenants, or a local response to heat, flood or air quality.
- d) Other. Tell me who.

**2. What real material can you bring?** The default runs on generic emission factors and made-up users.
- a) We have a real person or organisation with a problem we can talk to or name. Describe them in a line.
- b) We have a dataset or API we want to use, from a sponsor, an open government source or our own sensor. Name it.
- c) We know a local problem firsthand, such as heat in a building, flooding on a street, waste at our campus, or a power bill. Tell me what happened and any number you have.
- d) Nothing yet. Pick something buildable from open data.

**3. What can your team actually deliver this weekend?** The default assumes a web app plus an LLM call.
- a) Web or mobile app with an API or LLM. Tell me team size and hours.
- b) We can do hardware, such as a sensor or Raspberry Pi.
- c) We're strong in data, ML or maps and want the analysis to be the impressive part.
- d) Mixed or beginner team. Keep the scope small and solid.

**4. What does the demo need to win?** The default does a feature walkthrough for general judges.
- a) General judges and a short pitch. Tell me the time limit (for example, 3 minutes).
- b) There are sponsor prizes or required tools we should target. Name them.
- c) There are judging criteria such as impact, technical depth or feasibility that you can paste in.
- d) A live demo is risky, so plan for a recorded video or a slide-led demo.
