**Your resolution didn't fail. Your schedule did.**

Every January, the same advice goes round. Make it small. Make it specific. Do it at the same time every day. Build a morning routine.

Same time every day. I'd love to meet this "same time."

I work rotating 12s. Days for a few weeks, then nights, then back. There is no same time. My morning is sometimes 5am and sometimes 7pm, and some weeks I'm not fully sure which one I'm in. Half my coworkers live like this. So do warehouse staff, parents of newborns, anyone on call. And nearly every piece of habit advice out there is written by, and for, someone whose life runs 9 to 5 with weekends off.

So here's my theory. Most resolutions don't fail because people planned them badly. They fail because the plan assumes a schedule the person doesn't have.

I know because I ran the experiment three years in a row. Resolution: gym at 6am. Year one, done by February. Year two, done by February. Year three, I'd like to tell you I learned something. Done by February. Each time the thing that killed it was the rotation flipping. A 6am gym habit does not survive a switch to nights. It can't. 6am is now the middle of my sleep.

What finally worked wasn't a smaller goal or a tracking app. I stopped tying it to a time and tied it to an event: the gym is the first thing I do after any shift ends. Day shift, night shift, doesn't matter. Shift ends, I go. I've kept that one for 14 months, through every rotation, which is 11 months longer than the previous record.

Why does that work when 6am didn't? Because "shift ends" happens no matter what the clock says. The clock moves; the event doesn't. I'm already dressed, already out of the house, already too tired to invent an excuse. The habit lives on top of something I have to do anyway.

So if you're making a resolution this year and your life doesn't come with a stable morning, skip "same time every day." Pick an anchor event instead. Not 6am. After handover. Not 9pm. After the kids are down, whenever that is tonight. Not "before work." Right after the drive home, before you sit down, because once you sit down after a night shift that's where you live now.

Then protect the anchor, not the time. Shift runs late? The anchor moves with it. You're not behind. You're just not done yet.

None of this is small or simple. But at least it's built for the week you actually have.
