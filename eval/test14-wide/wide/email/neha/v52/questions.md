**What everyone gets**

The default cold email opens with "Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] is growing" or "fuel and driver costs are rising", claims "companies like yours cut delivery costs 15–25% with route optimisation", lists three bullet benefits (fewer miles, more stops per driver, happier customers), name-drops a vague "similar logistics firm", and closes with "Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week?" Choices it makes:

- **Angle**: cost savings, framed around industry trends rather than anything specific to them
- **Proof**: a round percentage and an unnamed customer
- **Structure**: compliment → problem → bullets → call ask
- **Reader**: a generic "busy exec", so it stays short and salesy
- **Tone**: friendly-corporate, with "I'd love to" and "quick call"

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

**1. The default pitches generic cost savings. What's the actual sharpest thing your software does that an ops head would care about?**
a) Fewer miles / fuel per route (the default)
b) More stops per driver per day without adding fleet
c) Handling same-day changes (cancellations, new orders, traffic) without re-planning by hand
d) Something else (tell me in a line)

**2. The default uses "companies like yours save 15–25%". What real proof can you give me?**
a) A named customer and one number (e.g. "X cut empty miles 18% in 3 months") — write it in
b) A number but the customer can't be named
c) Nothing citable yet; we're early — I'd rather the email be honest about that
d) Something concrete you noticed about *their* company (a job post, a route pattern, a news item) that we can open with

**3. The default asks for a 15-minute call. What's the ask?**
a) A short call (the default)
b) Send them a sample optimised plan from one of their public routes / a quick analysis — low commitment on their side
c) A reply to one specific question (e.g. "how are you re-routing when a driver calls in sick?")
d) A free pilot on one depot or region

**4. How should it sound, and whose name goes on it?**
a) Plain and direct, from a founder (no "I'd love to", no "synergies")
b) Plain and direct, from a sales rep
c) Warmer and a little more formal
d) Short to the point of blunt — three or four sentences, no pleasantries
