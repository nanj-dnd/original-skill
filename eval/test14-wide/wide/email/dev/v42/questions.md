**What everyone gets**

A pasted prompt returns the "we cut fuel costs by 15%" email. Its choices:

- **Angle:** cost savings — fewer miles, less fuel, as the one reason to care.
- **Examples:** a round-number stat ("10–20% fewer miles") and an unnamed "a similar logistics company saved…".
- **Structure:** subject with a number → "I hope this finds you well" / "I noticed [company]…" → pain → product → proof → "open to a 15-minute call next week?"
- **Reader:** a busy ops head who only wants ROI.
- **Tone:** polished sales-professional, with "streamline", "leverage", "seamlessly".

Four questions, answer like `1b 2a 3c 4a`:

**1. Angle.** The default leads with fuel and mileage savings. Lead with:
a) fuel/mileage cost (the default)
b) dispatcher time — planners spending hours a day building and re-building routes by hand
c) on-time delivery and SLA misses with customers
d) driver hours and retention — shorter, more predictable days

**2. Your real material.** The default uses a made-up "similar company" stat. What can you give me?
a) a real customer result — name (or sector), fleet size, and one number (miles, hours, cost, on-time %)
b) something specific you know about this prospect — fleet size, a recent expansion, a problem you've heard about
c) both of the above (paste what you have)
d) nothing checkable — write around it with no numbers

**3. The ask.** The default closes with "15-minute call next week?"
a) a 15-minute call
b) offer to run their actual routes through it and send back the comparison
c) one short question that only needs a reply, no call
d) a link to a two-minute demo recording

**4. Tone and sender.** Whose name goes on it, and how should it read?
a) plain and short, from the founder — five or six sentences, no sales words
b) polished and professional, from a sales rep
c) plain but a bit warmer, from me, with one line about why I'm writing to them specifically
