**What everyone gets**

The main claim: a personal carbon-footprint app — scan receipts or connect a bank feed, estimate emissions, nudge the user toward lower-carbon swaps, maybe with an AI chat layer. Choices it makes:

- Angle: individual consumer behaviour is the lever.
- Example: food and transport emissions, shown as a dashboard with a score.
- Structure: pitch slide, feature list, a 3-minute walkthrough of the happy path.
- Reader: generalist judges who reward polish and an "AI" badge.
- Tone: startup pitch, slightly breathless.

Four questions, reply like `1b 2a 3c 4b`:

1. Who's the user? (Default: an individual trying to reduce their own footprint.)
   a. Individuals / consumers
   b. A specific operator: a small business, a farm, a school, a building manager
   c. A city or utility team working with public data
   d. Researchers or journalists who need to verify climate claims

2. What's your team actually good at, and what will you have on hand? Name it honestly — this decides what's buildable in a weekend.
   a. Web/app front-end, happy to fake the backend
   b. Data / ML: can ingest a real dataset or satellite feed and model something
   c. Hardware: sensors, a Raspberry Pi or ESP32, something physical to show
   d. Mixed, with a strong designer

3. What's the one real thing you have or know that most teams won't? (Default: nothing specific; it's built from generic emissions factors.) A dataset, an API key, a domain contact, a local problem you've seen, a number — tell me in a few words, or pick:
   a. Access to a local dataset or sensor network (say which)
   b. A person we can call during the weekend who lives the problem
   c. A past project we can reuse
   d. Nothing yet — pick for us

4. What are the judges rewarding, and how should the write-up read? (Default: polished pitch for generalists.)
   a. Technical judges — show the hard part working, plain language
   b. Sponsor/industry judges — show a plausible customer and a number
   c. General audience — show a demo that gets a reaction in 90 seconds
   d. Internal/company hackathon — show something your own org could ship
